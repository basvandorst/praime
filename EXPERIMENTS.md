## Lessons (self-improvement)

- Validate an instrument on synthetic data with a known answer before reading a new number off it (the pair estimator: coin partner 1.01, local-density partner 2.06).
- A sum weighted logarithmically over scales shares its shot noise between nested ranges; a per-zero pattern that repeats when X grows is not structure until it survives cutting the common small primes.
- Cut the small primes before comparing spectra (below 10³ the density picture fails, below 10⁴ for the longer pair distances), and keep prime powers out of a like-for-like comparison.
- Add the analytic tail when a zero sum is truncated (Li coefficients: n² (ln(T/2π) + 1)/(2πT) was the whole −555 at n = 5000).
- Check a theorem's stated range before calling a ratio above 1 a violation (Schoenfeld's π − li bound starts at 2657).
- Sanity-check magnitudes against the theory before interpreting (a Nicolas margin of −15 was a missing logarithm, not a counterexample).
- Digit statistics of primes inherit mod-3 structure through the digit sum; compare against all integers coprime to 6 before claiming a bias.
- The singular series of a pair at two scales is not the twin constant: (p, 3p+2) has twice it. Compute every constant from its root counts, never by analogy.
- The partner of a pair feels the zero wave with a phase shift γ ln(1 + h/p); a coefficient below 2 for distant partners is decoherence, not a missing law.
- Size a spectrum run before launching it: survivors × frequencies × 30 ns; 12 million survivors against 400 frequencies is five minutes, not two.
- An indicator built from divisibility (a wheel's survivors) is not zero-free: its Dirichlet series is ζ(s) times an Euler factor, so it dips at the zeros too; only the coin's share is what the sieve cannot see.
