FINDINGS - results that surprised us
=====================================

Old records fell to old ideas
- Many records broke by plugging in a known object, without a new search. The largest known Mersenne prime gave the largest self prime (373) and a larger Smith number (307). Old twin-prime records gave 1,290,006 prime factors (450). The largest palindromic prime, raised to the 7th power, has 13 million digits and not one 6 (607).
- Moving a known polynomial so its middle sits in the middle of the range beat a record (597). Smaller values are prime more often.

One remainder does all the work
- Primes that are 17 mod 40 always have an even period of 1/p. One formula built on that beat 1230. The same formula then beat 1135 too, because every value ends in two odd digits.
- A prime pine with 10 rows was open since 2012. Make every entry 1 mod 3, 5, 7 and 11, and the small primes can no longer interfere (77).

The rules were looser than intended
- Floor and mod formulas can glue together blocks of primes in arithmetic progression. That took 120 primes to 570 (1099) and 65 to 130 (1097). With such rules, a formula can reproduce any list you choose.
- Half the prime gap stays small for a very long time. Euler's polynomial with that input stays prime for 1,319,943 consecutive n (1217).

Some questions answered themselves with a proof
- A prime is never followed by a base-2 pseudoprime: n + 1 even hands n a divisor (170).
- A palindrome with 2000 digits is always divisible by 11, so three "2000" questions have no answer (75).
- 2^n - 2^k + 1 is a square only for k = 3: Ramanujan's equation and a theorem of Beukers (490).

Some page claims needed a second look
- A listed run of 45 unlucky primes contained five lucky numbers. The real run was 26 (1209).
- A page claim of 51.6% is not a coincidence. 30030 = 2·3·5·7·11·13, and Hardy-Littlewood predicts 51.7% (1204).

New this session
- Rotondo's 109-prime "irregular" polynomial (1139) is really two quadratic forms of one discriminant, interleaved. We searched every monic case with D up to 2*10^9, and filtered cases up to 10^13: nothing beats it.
- His odd-looking "unless the result is 2" clause is essential. Without it the run stops after 34 values.
- "All known n with 2^n - 7 prime are composite" looks like a law but is a bias (1257). Small primes leave 8 classes mod 60, and 5 of them are multiples of 3 or 5. A heuristic expects 2 to 3 prime n by now, so finding none has a 6-13% chance.

What we learned about the method
- Asking "why does the old record work?" helped more than computing power. Most wins came from a short argument and a small search.
- Many results were a trick from another puzzle, reused. The same prime blocks served 1097, 1099 and 1028.
- Several claimed records did not survive an independent re-check. Re-checking is not optional.
