# The logic of the primes: one identity, three layers

> **In short:** The primes' regularities sort into three layers: local (each prime's residues, the singular series), archimedean (size, the pole) and spectral (the zeros). For linear prime sums the explicit formula joins them exactly; gaps and twins rest on Hardy–Littlewood. Measured links: the small primes fix the lowest zero (rank 0.96–0.99, degrees 1–4); the lowest zero sets the phase of prime races; record quadratics sit on low zeros, mostly through inert small primes; each prime missing from an Euler product makes the zeros more rigid by Berry's per-prime term (slopes 0.94–1.02 at one mean spacing), and over other window lengths by the off-diagonal term of Bogomolny and Keating, at its predicted size (0.998 ± 0.014 on two fresh samples); and ζ's rigidity is the sum of all primes' waves, to 0.004.

The documents this map draws on: [STUDY.md](STUDY.md) (seven lines, rounds 1–137), [RIEMANN.md](RIEMANN.md) (sections 1–23), [MERSENNE.md](MERSENNE.md) (sections 2–55), [FACTS.md](FACTS.md), [FINDINGS.txt](FINDINGS.txt). Each statement carries its status: *theorem*, *computed*, *heuristic*, *conjecture* or *open*; *ours* marks what this repo derived or measured first, as far as we found. Nine adversarial reviews with their own code recomputed the headline numbers (rounds 104, 105, 107 and the identity of L3; rounds 113, 116, 117 and one row of 119; rounds 120–123; rounds 124–126; rounds 127–129; rounds 130–133; round 134; round 135; round 137; all confirmed except the precision claimed for round 112, the amplitude of round 134, 0.93 of the predicted one, and the one missed cell of round 135, which was chance) and corrected the wording of this map, including the limits of L6 and the small-prime confound of round 124.

## 1. The identity, and what it covers

For a test function h with Fourier transform ĥ, Weil's explicit formula (*theorem*) reads, schematically,

  Σ_ρ h(γ) = [pole terms, only for ζ] + (1/2π)∫ h(r)·[ln(q/π) + Re Γ′/Γ-term] dr − 2 Σ_n Λ(n) Re χ(n) ĥ(ln n)/√n,

with the zeros ρ on the left, the archimedean place (the pole of ζ at s = 1 and the gamma factor of the completed L-function) in the middle, and every prime power on the right. Riemann's formula ψ(x) = x − Σ x^ρ/ρ − … is the case of a sharp cut-off; the variance laws, the tube and the races of this repo are other choices of h. This covers exactly the *linear* statistics, sums Σ Λ(n) χ(n) f(n): counts in intervals and in residue classes, their variances over windows, the races. Statistics of pairs and tuples (gaps, twins, k-tuples, prime values of polynomials, the last-digit bias) are not terms of the identity; for them the local layer is the Hardy–Littlewood heuristic, a *conjecture*, which this repo has tested to the noise in many windows.

## 3. The links, tested

Each link joins two layers. All are measured in this repo; the theory behind each is classical unless marked ours.

**L1. Local → spectral, ζ: the pair constants are the zeros' pair correlation.** The Hardy–Littlewood pair constants summed over a window give Montgomery–Soundararajan's variance; the same variance read from the zeros (Goldston–Montgomery) is ln(1/δ) + B. *Computed*: primes alone give R within 0.066 of ln(1/δ) + B for δ = 10⁻³ … 10⁻⁶ at 10⁹ (rounds 91–92, pre-registered); the 2,419 zeros below height 2,949 reproduce the primes' R(X, δ) within 0.003 at X = 10⁸, 10⁹, 10¹⁰ and δ = 0.1, 0.01 (round 93, a numerical identity).

**L2. Archimedean → variance.** With a test function whose Fourier support lies below ln 2, no prime enters the explicit formula, so the mean variance is fixed by the pole and the gamma factor alone: D(δ) = ln(1/δ) − γ − ln 2π + 2 ln(1 + δ) + ((2 + δ)/δ) ln(1 + δ/2) (round 95, numerical check 2·10⁻⁸; its δ → 0 constant 1 − γ − ln 2π is Montgomery–Soundararajan's and Chan's; the closed form at finite δ was not found in print). This is now a *theorem* (fact 2050; proof and referee's second derivation in RIEMANN.md §23): the sum over zeros equals δ·D(δ) with no hypothesis, and it is the tube identity of fact 2048 read at 1 + δ; under RH the mean over ln X exists and equals the sum with squared multiplicities, so it is D(δ) exactly when all zeros are simple; for δ > 1 the prime powers up to 1 + δ enter, −(2/δ) Σ Λ(n)((1 + δ)/n − 1). The race between residue classes has no pole: D_χ(δ) lacks 2 ln(1 + δ) and gains ln q, exactly so for even χ, with a further δ/2 − δ²/4 + … for odd χ (*theorem* under GRH, fact 2050; round 102; the δ → 0 form with ln q is Bui–Keating–Smith's, 2016). So the "+1" of the counting function, the pole, shows in the primes as an extra variance that races do not have.

**L3. Local → the lowest zero.** Σ_ρ 1/(ρ(1 − ρ)) = 2 Re L′/L(1, χ) + ln(q/π) + ψ((1 + a)/2) (*theorem*: twice the first Keiper–Li coefficient, from Hadamard's product; fact 2049), and under GRH the left side is Σ_ρ 1/|ρ|². The first review checked it from the zeros: 3.5·10⁻⁶ for χ₋₄, 2.4·10⁻⁶ for χ₅. L′/L(1, χ) weighs each prime power by about ln p/p, so characters that are −1 on many small primes have a large right side, and the lowest zero must be near the central point. *Computed* (round 104, 3,040 fundamental discriminants with |D| ≤ 5,000): the first zero predicted from the right side alone, the other zeros taken as typical for that size, has rank correlation 0.987 (D < 0) and 0.986 (D > 0) with the true one (a baseline that uses only the size gives 0.32 and 0.40). The same holds in higher degree: for the Artin L-functions ζ_K/ζ of all S3 cubic fields with |disc| ≤ 5,000 (degree 2) and all S4 quartic fields with |disc| ≤ 20,000 (degree 3), with S = 2 L′/L(1) + ln(N/π^d) + Σ_j ψ((1 + μ_j)/2), the rank correlations are 0.983–0.987 and 0.984–0.985 (rounds 111 and 114, pre-registered; 0.979 for the 44 totally real quartic fields, reported only), and in degree 4, for the 131 F5 quintic fields with |disc| ≤ 10⁷, 0.964 (round 130, pre-registered; S alone already gives −0.919, and the first pair of zeros is about a third of S, so part of the agreement is built into the identity). The same weights cut off at p^k ≤ 30 already give 0.969 and 0.954, so what fixes the zero is the fixed weights ln p/p together with the variable 1/(¼ + γ²), more than the far primes. For complex characters (the family of RIEMANN §18.1, where a free fit on Re χ(p), p ≤ 199, gave R² = 0.45 mod 395,137), round 107 tested all 1,007 characters mod 1009: rank correlation 0.937 (passes), but the registered straight-line measure failed (R² 0.22 against 0.745 for a free fit on Re χ(p), p ≤ 199, in sample); in the identity's variable, 1/(¼ + γ²) against S, Pearson is 0.926 (chosen afterwards, and a different target variable than the free fit's).

**L4. Local → prime-rich quadratics → low zeros.** A quadratic is prime-rich when no small prime divides its values, i.e. χ_D(p) = −1 for small p. Its Bateman–Horn constant is (factor at 2) × (ramified factors) × K(χ)/L(1, χ_D), with K = ∏(1 − χ(p)/(p − 1))/(1 − χ(p)/p) absolutely convergent; K varies with D far less than L(1) (sd of ln K 0.19 against 0.59 for ln L(1), from the review), so the constant is mostly 1/L(1, χ_D) (the density itself is *conjecture*). Small L(1) goes with low zeros: Spearman 0.87 between ln L(1, χ_D) and the scaled first zero g = γ₁ ln|D|/2π, for both signs (round 104, pre-registered). Euler's n² + n + 41 sits on L(s, χ₋₁₆₃): first zero 0.2029, scaled 0.164, the 11th lowest scaled zero of 1,524 negative discriminants up to 5,000 (36th by γ₁ itself; χ₋₄: 6.02). The pre-registered guess that Heegner discriminants would be among the ten lowest failed.

**L5. The lowest zero → the phase of the race.** The race between the −1 and the +1 primes of χ_D is E(x) = 1 + 2 Σ Re(x^(iγ)/ρ) (Rubinstein–Sarnak; the 1 is the prime squares, a local term). A low zero is a slow wave in ln x of period 2π/γ₁: with γ₁ ≈ 0.07 the −1 side leads at every prime below 10⁵, with γ₁ ≈ 0.3 the wave has turned near 10³ and the +1 side leads. *Computed* (round 105): the zeros up to height 30 predict, for each D, the share of primes at which the −1 side leads with Pearson 0.981 (mean error 0.039); the lowest zero alone gives 0.889. The share is not a function of any one number: its Spearman correlation is −0.23 with S, −0.19 with L′/L(1) and +0.22 with γ₁ (from the review), because it depends on the phase γ₁ ln x. Our pre-registered guess, a lower zero means a larger bias, failed with the wrong sign. For races between any two classes mod a prime q, all characters enter: from the zeros with |γ| ≤ 50 of every character mod q = 5 … 31, the share of primes up to 10⁶ at which class b leads class 1 is predicted over 137 races with Pearson 0.992 and mean error 0.027 (round 131, pre-registered); without the prime-squares term the same zeros give 0.903 and 0.099, so the local term is needed alongside the spectral one. Mod 8 and mod 12, where all three characters are real and each brings the prime-squares term, the zeros predict that class 1 is last at every prime from 23 to 10⁷, which is FACTS 1605 (round 133; mod 12 the sieve has ties up to 461, so the registered strict reading failed there). On the classical race mod 4 (round 112), the zeros of L(s, χ₋₄) alone, with no prime counted, find all four regions below 10⁹ where the primes ≡ 1 lead (from 26,861, 616,841, 12,306,137, 951,784,481), with ends off by up to 3.3 % and the last region 1.1 % early, but its registered reading (1) failed on false dips at 461 and 8,694–8,778; with li(x^ρ) and the prime squares added (round 113, three layers together) the three regions below 10⁸ come out within 0.3 % at both ends and the share of primes to 10⁹ within a factor 3 (0.0057 % against 0.015 %, reading (3) passed), while readings (1) and (2) failed: two short false dips at x = 127 and 461 remain and the shallow region at 951,784,481 is not reproduced.

**L6. Missing primes → rigid zeros.** In the zeros' form factor the primes enter at τ = ln p^k/ln(T/2π); since no prime power is below 2, it has no weight below τ = ln 2/ln(T/2π) (Berry's shortest-orbit argument), while its saturation at τ = 1 comes from the off-diagonal terms (Bogomolny–Keating, via the Hardy–Littlewood constants; *heuristic*). Reading the primes as the "periodic orbits" of the zeros is the Berry–Keating analogy (*heuristic*). What was measured:

- **ζ.** The swing of R(X, δ) over ln X is below stationary GUE by 3.2–4.6σ at δ = 0.01–0.003, and the form-factor prediction accounts for it (round 100; its overall normalisation was not re-derived independently). ζ's finite-height spacing deficit is also treated by Bogomolny, Bohigas, Leboeuf and Monastra (2006), through an effective matrix size with an arithmetic constant. At equal density ζ fits the per-prime law below as the L-function with nothing missing: ten real characters of prime conductor 29–67 sit below ζ's Σ²(1) by −0.0041 ± 0.0017 against Berry's −0.0045 (round 128, pre-registered; ζ's 40,000 zeros), so the pole of ζ makes no visible difference here. And all the waves together give ζ's rigidity: Berry's sum over the prime powers below e^L (plus saturation above τ = 1) matches the number variance of 26,812 zeros at L ≈ 8.2 within 0.004 for every ℓ from 1 to 8, while GUE is off by up to 0.19; the measured Σ²(ℓ) peaks near ℓ = 4.5 and falls again, as the waves of the small primes together make it (round 129, pre-registered; the formula is Berry's). The data lie a systematic 0.002 (0.5 %) above the leading-order formula, and 25–30 % of its value is the saturation term beyond τ = 1, assumed rather than computed from primes (fortieth review).
- **Every Dirichlet L-function tested is below GUE** (round 110), and a small prime dividing the conductor, so missing from the Euler product, makes it more rigid. For real characters with 100 ≤ |D| ≤ 400 at heights 100–600 the **spacing** variance drops by about 0.014 per missing prime among 2, 3, 5 (pre-registered replication on 60 fresh L-functions: t = −12.8; rounds 115–116; adding ln|D| as a covariate changes nothing). S does not order it (round 115).
- **The size, at windows of one mean spacing.** Berry's 1988 number-variance formula taken one prime at a time, with no fitted parameter (for Dirichlet L-functions this is the diagonal term of Bogomolny–Leboeuf, 1994, and Bogomolny–Keating, 2013, with the primes dividing the conductor removed; the formula is theirs, the measurements are this repo's): removing p changes Σ²(1) by −(2/π²) Σ_k sin²(πk ln p/L)/(k² p^k), L = ln(qt/2π). Fitted against predicted on all 181 real L-functions (round 120, pre-registered): 2: −0.0111/−0.0100, 3: −0.0130/−0.0122, 5: −0.0135/−0.0125, 7: −0.0116/−0.0117, 11: −0.0100/−0.0100, 13: −0.0075/−0.0093, slope 1.02 (with the primes above 13 that divide D added as an offset, p = 2 is within 5 % and p = 13 at −0.0088). For 88 complex characters (round 121): Σ²(1) = a + β·(predicted drop summed over p | N) with β = 1.003 ± 0.038 (1.005 ± 0.039 after completing the zero sets). At heights 900–1500 (round 122): β = 0.944 ± 0.042 (after completing the zero sets; 0.950 before). Only whether a prime is present counts, not the sign of χ(p): χ_D(2) = ±1 makes no difference (−0.0008 ± 0.0009, round 118). The formula is Berry's; these tests are ours as far as we found.
- **Other window lengths: first order only.** Over ℓ mean spacings the formula, −(2/π²) Σ_k sin²(πℓk ln p/L)/(k² p^k), oscillates with period L/ln p, and to first order so do the data (round 123, pre-registered, all readings passed): 6 primes × 16 lengths give a slope of 0.991 after completing 22 zero sets that missed a close pair (0.977 ± 0.011 by bootstrap before the repair, then significantly below 1); the drop for p = 13 nearly vanishes at ℓ = 3.5 and returns, that for p = 5 near ℓ = 5.5, that for p = 2 grows eightfold by ℓ = 6.5. In detail the diagonal formula is rejected: the residual oscillates with period 1 in ℓ (too negative at whole ℓ, too positive at half ℓ, growing with ℓ; χ² 15–85 on 6 at most lengths), it fits only near ℓ ≈ 0.75–1.1, and small windows fail (slope 0.56 at ℓ = 0.5 for real, 0.30 for complex characters; thirty-eighth review). The correction is the off-diagonal term of Bogomolny and Keating's two-point function (2013; *heuristic*), which carries e^(2πir) in unfolded units and a factor F_p = (p − p^(−iε))/(p − 2 + p^(iε)) per prime dividing the conductor. It cancels the diagonal change at zero distance, so for small ℓ the total drop is −(2 ln²p/((p − 1)L²))·(ℓ² − sin²(πℓ)/π²): 0.6 of the diagonal term at ℓ = 0.5 and equal to it at ℓ = 1 (*derived* from their formula). *Computed*, blind (round 134: predictions for each L-function frozen before the zeros of 184 fresh discriminants, 400 < |D| ≤ 700, were computed): the slope of the diagonal law's residual on the predicted off-diagonal part is 0.932 ± 0.024 (diagonal law 0, full law 1); calibrated χ² on 96 cells 81.7 for the full law against 715.8 for the diagonal law; the mean level with no fitted constant within 0.0005 at all 16 lengths; the 88 complex characters agree (0.943 ± 0.104), so real and complex characters follow one law. The product over all bad primes is needed (one prime at a time: χ² 210.9 against 85.9 in the review's version). In that sample the amplitude was short of the prediction by 7 % (two-sided p = 0.003; forty-second review). No lower-order term gives that: the ratios-conjecture function equals Bogomolny–Keating's term by term and its gamma-factor ratio has modulus 1 (*derived*, round 136). Two fresh tests with thresholds set from the real errors (round 136: the discriminants of round 120 at heights 600–1,000, and 181 new ones with 700 < |D| ≤ 1,000) give 1.000 ± 0.024 and 0.997 ± 0.018, together 0.998 ± 0.014, so the amplitude is the predicted one and the first sample was low. A numerical test of these bad-prime factors on zeros was not found in print.

**L7. The swing → three variance misses.** The variance of a linear statistic over a window of finite length in ln x carries the zeros' slow swing, which the long-run laws average out. *Computed*, pre-registered: (round 106) MERSENNE §34's variance of ψ(n + h) − ψ(n) − h over n in [N/2, N], N = 10⁷, at 0.963, 0.925, 0.879 of Montgomery–Soundararajan for h = 10³, 3·10³, 10⁴, "unexplained", is 0.969, 0.936, 0.881 from the first 10,000 zeros; (round 109) round 102's race variances, whose χ₃ reading at δ = 0.01 failed (4.449 against the law 4.296), are 4.438 from the zeros of L(s, χ₃) alone, and all four means within 0.017, point by point at 200 heights with correlation 0.9988–1.0000. (round 119) the six variances of STUDY rounds 10 and 34 at H = 10⁵ and 10⁶, all 0.4–5.5 % below the law and called noise, come out of the zeros nearer the measurement than the law in all six cases, within 0.8 % in four (the window at 3·10⁹ misses by 1.8–2.3 %, so two registered readings failed); with the first 40,000 zeros (round 127) all six come within 0.8 %, the three at H = 10⁶ within 1 %, though at H = 10⁵ the tail model still carries 43–85 %. What this shows: the explicit formula reproduces the sampled values (at 10⁹, H = 10⁶, interval by interval with correlation 0.88–0.95 per block), so a window's deviation from the long-run law is the zeros' term for that window. It does not show a systematic effect: round 119's deviations are about 1σ of the block-to-block scatter, as rounds 10 and 34 said, and at H = 10⁵ much of the zero value comes from the random-phase tail model, not from the zeros used (84 % with 10,000 zeros, 43–85 % with 40,000; 5–14 % at H = 10⁶).

**L8. Local → the polynomial records.** Every record prime-producing polynomial of degrees 2 to 6 has a late first prime P that can divide a value (41 to 89); values below P² are then automatically prime (*computed*, STUDY polynomial section). The cubic record is a cubic analogue of Euler's: inert at 5, 7, 13, …, 43, with 2, 3, 11 sent to infinity by its leading coefficient 66. The quadratic records sit on low zeros, measured (round 124, pre-registered): the 19 fundamental discriminants of the polynomials with runs of 36 or more found by the quadratic search (A ≤ 300) have scaled first zeros at a median percentile of 0.020 among random discriminants of the same size, all 19 below the median (Euler's −163 lowest among the ~40 in its range; Ruby's 7213, first zero 0.0725, at 0.000). Most of this restates that their small primes are inert: matched on χ(p) for p ≤ 23 the median percentile is 0.220 (15 of 17 below the median; thirty-ninth review), so a smaller remainder comes from the larger primes. The reverse direction is weak (round 125, pre-registered, both readings failed): the 15 lowest zeros among 2,000 random discriminants in [10⁵, 10⁶] give best runs with median 6 against 3 for random ones (p = 0.025), because a low zero weighs all primes by ln p/p and can come with a split or ramified 2 or 3, which ends every run: a low zero is close to necessary for a record, not sufficient. With the smallest primes filtered first (2, 3, 5, 7, 11 inert), the zero does rank the discriminants (round 126, pre-registered; replicated with another seed): the 15 lowest zeros among 600 such discriminants give best runs of median 12 (up to 29) against 8 for the 15 highest (p = 1.3·10⁻⁵); but the first prime ≥ 13 that is not inert ranks them as well (Spearman 0.67 against −0.64), so the zero mostly sums up the next small primes. On all 600 filtered discriminants (round 132, pre-registered) S seemed to add to the first non-inert prime (t = 8 beside 24), but the forty-first review showed the extra is local too: with the next Euler factors up to about 100 and ln d as covariates S adds nothing (t = 0.6). Prime-richness is set by the local layer; S, which contains the same Euler factors, is a summary of it. For cubic fields the chain holds at small discriminants (round 111, pre-registered, all 891 S3 fields with |disc| ≤ 5000): small L(1) of ζ_K/ζ goes with a low first zero (Spearman 0.78 and 0.80 for the two signatures) and the identity predicts the first zero to rank 0.983 and 0.987; the first prime with a root is a poor measure for cubics (reading failed). For S4 quartic fields (degree 3 L-functions, round 114) small L(1) goes with a low first zero at 0.70 and 0.80. For the record polynomials themselves (discriminants 5·10¹⁴ for the cubic, 8·10¹² for the quartic) the zeros needed more than the 1.4 GB PARI stack available here, so that link is *open*.

**How the links chain.** On the same 3,040 L-functions, link by link: the small primes, weighted by ln p/p, fix where the lowest zero lies (rank 0.99); small L(1), which is what makes the quadratic prime-rich, goes with a low zero (0.87); and the zeros, the lowest one first, set the phase of the race (Pearson 0.89 with the lowest zero, 0.98 with the zeros up to 30). Euler's polynomial, low zeros and Chebyshev's bias, treated apart in the repo, are linked this way; they are not one number, since the race depends on γ₁ ln x and L(1) and L′/L(1) are different quantities.

## 4. The anomalies, sorted

Two catalogues of everything in the repo that did not fit (one agent per side, both read-only) give the list below. *Explained* means a measured explanation in the repo; *proposed* means a reading that has not been tested.

**Lower-order terms of the local layer (open).**
- The conditioned gap law's residuals are all positive and grow with g (largest 0.063 % at g = 640; STUDY round 53); the law's share of gaps ≥ 40 is 0.26 % high at 10⁸–10⁹ (round 41) and the two-gap law 0.27 % high in long-gap cells at 10⁹ but not at 10¹² (stress test); but in the twin-gap tail beyond 1,200 the data are 0.24 % above the law (rounds 59, 64). The two-gap law's row sums are 1.0006–1.0012 and the k-th neighbour law's mass is off by 2·10⁻⁴. The signs differ, so one normalisation error cannot produce all of these; order 3 would decide.
- Runs of prime values of n² + n + 41: observed/expected fell from 1.000 (r = 1) to 0.856 (r = 8) in round 103; on fresh n in [10⁷, 3·10⁷) the ratio at r = 8 is 1.22 (round 108, pre-registered), so the fall was chance (*explained*).

**The archimedean layer at finite height (explained).** The (7/4)δ of the variance (round 95); the 7/8 of N(T); the race without the pole (round 102).

**The spectral swing (reproduced, L7).** MERSENNE §34's long-range variance shortfall (round 106); round 102's failed χ₃ reading (round 109); the values below the law at H = 10⁵–10⁶ in STUDY rounds 10 and 34 (round 119: nearer than the law in all six cases, within 0.8 % in four, mostly model tail at H = 10⁵). The explicit formula gives these windows' values; whether each was more than noise is a separate question (round 119's were about 1σ).

**Rigidity, and a noise model that ignores it (partly explained; a remainder open).** The repo has a long list of χ² values below their degrees of freedom: last digits 20.0–25.4 on 36 (rounds 85–86), k-th neighbour distances 8.2 on 19 (round 83), block errors 0.75–0.87 of binomial (round 85), the residue tables mod 210 at 0.691 (rounds 13, 39), prime bytes, the lighthouse, pair clocks (MERSENNE §10, §45, §53), the Goldbach comet 13 % narrower than sieved fakes (§28); as fractions of their nulls, 0.43 to 0.87. Counts of primes and of gap events per residue class are rigid, their long-range correlations negative, so binomial or multinomial nulls overstate the noise (*proposed* as the common reading; not tested item by item). Part of it is predicted by the local layer: the Hardy–Littlewood long-range sums give the residue tables 0.847 against 1 for independent draws, about half of the measured shortfall (round 39), and they predict most of the gap-event rigidity of round 40 (0.746 and 0.834 of Poisson, against 0.595 and 0.797 measured). The remainder is *open*: measured/predicted 0.80 at train and 0.79 at 10⁸–10⁹ (1.00 at A) after calibration (round 40, eighth review), and twin counts spread across residue classes more evenly than the four-point sums predict (u² = 0.13 against 0.45 at 10⁸–10⁹, also low in a short window at 10¹⁰; Fisher p ≈ 0.002 over three windows; rounds 41–44). Twins have no L-function, so no explicit formula supplies the missing term. The zeros of Dirichlet L-functions are rigid too (L6), but whether that rigidity accounts for the class-count remainder was not tested. Round 135 (pre-registered, three fresh windows at 3·10¹¹–9·10¹¹; forty-third review with 16 more windows) gives the class-count part a formula: from the Hardy–Littlewood pairs each class count mod q in a window of length H has Var/mean = 1 − [ln(H/q) + Σ_(p | q) ln p/(p − 1) − B]/ln x, the logarithm sitting on pairs in the same class only (in substance Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan 2016 and Goldston–Yıldırım 1998). Measured χ²/dof for equal classes mod 3, 4, 10, 210 in blocks of 10⁶: 0.493, 0.439, 0.496, 0.575 against 0.477, 0.488, 0.496, 0.564; on nine windows the law also holds at block lengths 10⁴ and 10⁵ (every |z| ≤ 1.3) and its slope in ln H is right (−0.083 per decade against −0.085). For tables of consecutive-prime classes the χ² splits exactly into a column part and a within part. The column part is fixed by the class counts, with a closed formula and no sieve model, E[col]/(C − 1) = tr[M̃⁺(I − Pᵀ)K(I − P)]/(C − 1) (a design-effect correction in the sense of Rao and Scott; measured 0.51–0.59, formula 0.49–0.58), so it is the same law, not a second confirmation. The within part is 1 for next-but-one primes (the registered low value of 0.762 mod 3 was chance: 1.05 ± 0.06 on six fresh windows) and below 1 for consecutive primes (0.955, 0.984, 0.986 mod 3, 5, 7 in blocks of 10⁴), which is rigidity outside the class counts. Round 137 gives it a formula: at leading order 1 − 1/((φ(q) − 1) ln x), from the repulsion between p_n and p_(n+2) in Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan's conjecture (known in substance), and a finite-height version from the triples of consecutive primes that fits fresh windows at 6·10⁹–8.4·10⁹ in level (0.9490, 0.9760, 0.9807 against 0.9486, 0.9783, 0.9809). Its fall with the block length is about twice what the pair correlations give for mod 5, in the directions that mix the two characters mod 5, at both heights (forty-fourth review), and that is *open*. The family was also selected: four statistics of the same kind (rounds 15, 71, 82, 84) are not low.

**Polynomial tails (open).** The heuristic chance per candidate overestimates the run-length tail of sextic searches by about 10³ (STUDY polynomial section).

**Clarified by this map.** RIEMANN §18.1 (the lowest zero against small primes): L3 gives a rank prediction of 0.94–0.99 from fixed weights, with the caveat of round 107; the low-zero discriminants with the "wrong" race sign (L5); MERSENNE §34 and round 102 (L7); RIEMANN §23's spacing variance below GUE, now seen prime by prime for Dirichlet L-functions (L6).

## 5. What "not random" means here

A reading, *heuristic*. The primes are not random in any layer. The local layer is a sieve, described by the singular series for every pattern the repo has tested (a conjecture for patterns, a theorem for single residue classes); the archimedean layer is a smooth function of size. What looks random is the spectral layer, the sum over zeros, and that is not free either: the zeros are recovered from the primes (backwards decoding, RIEMANN §6 and §13; the lowest zero from a weighted prime sum, L3), and the zeros' rigidity is set by the primes: ζ's number variance is the sum of one wave per prime power to within 0.5 % (round 129), and each prime missing from an Euler product makes the zeros more rigid by its own wave (L6). What behaves like chance is that the heights γ are believed to be linearly independent over the rationals (a conjecture), so sums of x^(iγ) behave like sums of independent phases. In one sentence: for linear statistics the primes and the zeros determine each other through one exact identity, and what looks random is the incommensurability of the zeros' heights.

## 6. Open tests

- **L8 beyond degree 2:** degree 2 is measured (round 124); for the cubic, quartic and quintic records, the first zero of ζ_K/ζ against fields of similar discriminant (needs a larger machine or an explicit-formula estimate of the first zero).
- **Twins across classes:** a long-range correction to the four-point sums, of the kind L2 gives single primes.
- **The low-χ² family:** class counts and the column part of transition tables are predicted by formula (round 135 and its review). Open: the fall of the within part of consecutive-prime tables with the block length, twice the pair-correlation tail for mod 5 and concentrated in the mixed character directions (round 137 and its review; the level itself is explained); whether the class-count law is slightly low at H = 10⁶ (+2.4 errors mod 210); round 13's training window (0.691 against 0.856) and round 83's bands at k = 4.
- **Order 3 of the gap laws:** whether the long-gap excess (law high) and the twin-gap tail (law low) come from two different lower-order terms.
- **Rigidity of L-function zeros mod q:** at ℓ = 1 the per-prime drop matches Berry's formula (2 to 13, real and complex, two heights); over window lengths the period-1 residual and the failure at ℓ = 0.5 are the off-diagonal term of Bogomolny and Keating (round 134, blind). Its amplitude, 0.93 ± 0.02 in the first sample, is 0.998 ± 0.014 on two fresh samples (round 136), and the ratios conjecture has no lower-order term that would change it. Open: nothing at this precision; a round at larger conductors or heights would test the claimed error of order E^(−1/2).
- **Goldbach comet:** the primes' comet is 13 % narrower than fakes sieved to 100 even after class means are removed (MERSENNE §28); fakes carry thinning noise that primes lack, so the fair comparison is the Hardy–Littlewood four-point variance of r(n), which was not computed. It may be the same binary rigidity as the twins across classes.
