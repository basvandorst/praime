# The logic of the primes: one identity, three layers

> **In short:** The regularities this repo has measured in the primes sort into three layers: a local layer (each prime p on its own: residues, the sieve, the singular series), an archimedean layer (size: the density 1/ln x, the pole, the gamma factor) and a spectral layer (the zeros). For linear prime sums the explicit formula joins the three exactly; correlations such as gaps and twins rest on the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture. The layers determine each other: the small primes fix the lowest zero, the lowest zero sets the phase of the prime race, and the zeros' slow swing explains two variance misses. Measured on 3,040 L-functions and at 200 heights.

The documents this map draws on: [STUDY.md](STUDY.md) (seven lines, rounds 1–112), [RIEMANN.md](RIEMANN.md) (sections 1–23), [MERSENNE.md](MERSENNE.md) (sections 2–55), [FACTS.md](FACTS.md), [FINDINGS.txt](FINDINGS.txt). Each statement carries its status: *theorem*, *computed*, *heuristic*, *conjecture* or *open*; *ours* marks what this repo derived or measured first, as far as we found. An adversarial review with its own code recomputed every number of section 3 (all confirmed) and corrected the wording of this map.

## 1. The identity, and what it covers

For a test function h with Fourier transform ĥ, Weil's explicit formula (*theorem*) reads, schematically,

  Σ_ρ h(γ) = [pole terms, only for ζ] + (1/2π)∫ h(r)·[ln(q/π) + Re Γ′/Γ-term] dr − 2 Σ_n Λ(n) Re χ(n) ĥ(ln n)/√n,

with the zeros ρ on the left, the archimedean place (the pole of ζ at s = 1 and the gamma factor of the completed L-function) in the middle, and every prime power on the right. Riemann's formula ψ(x) = x − Σ x^ρ/ρ − … is the case of a sharp cut-off; the variance laws, the tube and the races of this repo are other choices of h. This covers exactly the *linear* statistics, sums Σ Λ(n) χ(n) f(n): counts in intervals and in residue classes, their variances over windows, the races. Statistics of pairs and tuples (gaps, twins, k-tuples, prime values of polynomials, the last-digit bias) are not terms of the identity; for them the local layer is the Hardy–Littlewood heuristic, a *conjecture*, which this repo has tested to the noise in many windows.

## 3. The links, tested

Each link joins two layers. All are measured in this repo; the theory behind each is classical unless marked ours.

**L1. Local → spectral, ζ: the pair constants are the zeros' pair correlation.** The Hardy–Littlewood pair constants summed over a window give Montgomery–Soundararajan's variance; the same variance read from the zeros (Goldston–Montgomery) is ln(1/δ) + B. *Computed*: primes alone give R within 0.066 of ln(1/δ) + B for δ = 10⁻³ … 10⁻⁶ at 10⁹ (rounds 91–92, pre-registered); the 2,419 zeros below height 2,949 reproduce the primes' R(X, δ) within 0.003 at X = 10⁸, 10⁹, 10¹⁰ and δ = 0.1, 0.01 (round 93, a numerical identity).

**L2. Archimedean → variance.** With a test function whose Fourier support lies below ln 2, no prime enters the explicit formula, so the mean variance is fixed by the pole and the gamma factor alone: D(δ) = ln(1/δ) − γ − ln 2π + 2 ln(1 + δ) + ((2 + δ)/δ) ln(1 + δ/2) (under RH and simple zeros; round 95, numerical check 2·10⁻⁸; the closed form at finite δ is ours as far as we found, its δ → 0 constant 1 − γ − ln 2π is Chan's, 2002). The race between residue classes has no pole: D_χ(δ) lacks 2 ln(1 + δ) and gains ln q (*ours* as far as we found; round 102). So the "+1" of the counting function, the pole, shows in the primes as an extra variance that races do not have.

**L3. Local → the lowest zero.** Σ_ρ 1/(ρ(1 − ρ)) = 2 Re L′/L(1, χ) + ln(q/π) + ψ((1 + a)/2) (*theorem*: twice the first Keiper–Li coefficient, from Hadamard's product; fact 2049), and under GRH the left side is Σ_ρ 1/|ρ|². The review checked it from the zeros: 3.5·10⁻⁶ for χ₋₄, 2.4·10⁻⁶ for χ₅. L′/L(1, χ) weighs each prime power by about ln p/p, so characters that are −1 on many small primes have a large right side, and the lowest zero must be near the central point. *Computed* (round 104, 3,040 fundamental discriminants with |D| ≤ 5,000): the first zero predicted from the right side alone, the other zeros taken as typical for that size, has rank correlation 0.987 (D < 0) and 0.986 (D > 0) with the true one (a baseline that uses only the size gives 0.32 and 0.40). The same weights cut off at p^k ≤ 30 already give 0.969 and 0.954, so what fixes the zero is the fixed weights ln p/p together with the variable 1/(¼ + γ²), more than the far primes. For complex characters (the family of RIEMANN §18.1, where a free fit on Re χ(p), p ≤ 199, gave R² = 0.45 mod 395,137), round 107 tested all 1,007 characters mod 1009: rank correlation 0.937 (passes), but the registered straight-line measure failed (R² 0.22 against 0.745 for a free fit on Re χ(p), p ≤ 199, in sample); in the identity's variable, 1/(¼ + γ²) against S, Pearson is 0.926 (chosen afterwards, and a different target variable than the free fit's).

**L4. Local → prime-rich quadratics → low zeros.** A quadratic is prime-rich when no small prime divides its values, i.e. χ_D(p) = −1 for small p. Its Bateman–Horn constant is (factor at 2) × (ramified factors) × K(χ)/L(1, χ_D), with K = ∏(1 − χ(p)/(p − 1))/(1 − χ(p)/p) absolutely convergent; K varies with D far less than L(1) (sd of ln K 0.19 against 0.59 for ln L(1), from the review), so the constant is mostly 1/L(1, χ_D) (the density itself is *conjecture*). Small L(1) goes with low zeros: Spearman 0.87 between ln L(1, χ_D) and the scaled first zero g = γ₁ ln|D|/2π, for both signs (round 104, pre-registered). Euler's n² + n + 41 sits on L(s, χ₋₁₆₃): first zero 0.2029, scaled 0.164, the 11th lowest scaled zero of 1,524 negative discriminants up to 5,000 (36th by γ₁ itself; χ₋₄: 6.02). The pre-registered guess that Heegner discriminants would be among the ten lowest failed.

**L5. The lowest zero → the phase of the race.** The race between the −1 and the +1 primes of χ_D is E(x) = 1 + 2 Σ Re(x^(iγ)/ρ) (Rubinstein–Sarnak; the 1 is the prime squares, a local term). A low zero is a slow wave in ln x of period 2π/γ₁: with γ₁ ≈ 0.07 the −1 side leads at every prime below 10⁵, with γ₁ ≈ 0.3 the wave has turned near 10³ and the +1 side leads. *Computed* (round 105): the zeros up to height 30 predict, for each D, the share of primes at which the −1 side leads with Pearson 0.981 (mean error 0.039); the lowest zero alone gives 0.889. The share is not a function of any one number: its Spearman correlation is −0.23 with S, −0.19 with L′/L(1) and +0.22 with γ₁ (from the review), because it depends on the phase γ₁ ln x. Our pre-registered guess, a lower zero means a larger bias, failed with the wrong sign. On the classical race mod 4 (round 112), the 1,399 zeros of L(s, χ₋₄) below 1500, with no prime counted, place all four regions below 10⁹ where the primes ≡ 1 lead (from 26,861, 616,841, 12,306,137, 951,784,481) to about 1 % in x, with two false dips at small x and regions too wide; the share they give is right only within a factor 2 to 1.4·10¹¹ and 50 times too large to 10⁹.

**L6. The missing prime below 2 → rigid zeros (ζ).** In the zeros' form factor the primes enter at τ = ln p^k/ln(T/2π); since no prime power is below 2, it saturates with no weight below ln 2 (Berry's shortest-orbit argument; Bogomolny–Keating supply the Hardy–Littlewood off-diagonal terms; *heuristic*). *Computed*: the swing of R(X, δ) over ln X is below stationary GUE by 3.2–4.6σ at δ = 0.01–0.003, and the form-factor prediction accounts for it (round 100; its overall normalisation was not re-derived independently). So the zeros of ζ are more rigid than random matrices because of a local fact at the smallest prime.

**L7. The swing → two variance misses.** The variance of a linear statistic over a window of finite length in ln x carries the zeros' slow swing, which the long-run laws average out. *Computed*, pre-registered: (round 106) MERSENNE §34's variance of ψ(n + h) − ψ(n) − h over n in [N/2, N], N = 10⁷, at 0.963, 0.925, 0.879 of Montgomery–Soundararajan for h = 10³, 3·10³, 10⁴, "unexplained", is 0.969, 0.936, 0.881 from the first 10,000 zeros; (round 109) round 102's race variances, whose χ₃ reading at δ = 0.01 failed (4.449 against the law 4.296), are 4.438 from the zeros of L(s, χ₃) alone, and all four means within 0.017, point by point at 200 heights with correlation 0.9988–1.0000. Not failures of the laws and not noise: deterministic terms that the zeros give.

**L8. Local → the polynomial records.** Every record prime-producing polynomial of degrees 2 to 6 has a late first prime P that can divide a value (41 to 89); values below P² are then automatically prime (*computed*, STUDY polynomial section). The cubic record is a cubic analogue of Euler's: inert at 5, 7, 13, …, 43, with 2, 3, 11 sent to infinity by its leading coefficient 66. By L3–L4 the quadratic records sit on low zeros. For cubic fields the chain holds at small discriminants (round 111, pre-registered, all 891 S3 fields with |disc| ≤ 5000): small L(1) of ζ_K/ζ goes with a low first zero (Spearman 0.78 and 0.80 for the two signatures) and the identity predicts the first zero to rank 0.983 and 0.987; the first prime with a root is a poor measure for cubics (reading failed). For the record polynomials themselves (discriminants 5·10¹⁴ for the cubic, 8·10¹² for the quartic) the zeros needed more than the 1.4 GB PARI stack available here, so that link is *open*.

**How the links chain.** On the same 3,040 L-functions, link by link: the small primes, weighted by ln p/p, fix where the lowest zero lies (rank 0.99); small L(1), which is what makes the quadratic prime-rich, goes with a low zero (0.87); and the zeros, the lowest one first, set the phase of the race (Pearson 0.89 with the lowest zero, 0.98 with the zeros up to 30). Euler's polynomial, low zeros and Chebyshev's bias, treated apart in the repo, are linked this way; they are not one number, since the race depends on γ₁ ln x and L(1) and L′/L(1) are different quantities.

## 4. The anomalies, sorted

Two catalogues of everything in the repo that did not fit (one agent per side, both read-only) give the list below. *Explained* means a measured explanation in the repo; *proposed* means a reading that has not been tested.

**Lower-order terms of the local layer (open).**
- The conditioned gap law's residuals are all positive and grow with g (largest 0.063 % at g = 640; STUDY round 53); the law's share of gaps ≥ 40 is 0.26 % high at 10⁸–10⁹ (round 41) and the two-gap law 0.27 % high in long-gap cells at 10⁹ but not at 10¹² (stress test); but in the twin-gap tail beyond 1,200 the data are 0.24 % above the law (rounds 59, 64). The two-gap law's row sums are 1.0006–1.0012 and the k-th neighbour law's mass is off by 2·10⁻⁴. The signs differ, so one normalisation error cannot produce all of these; order 3 would decide.
- Runs of prime values of n² + n + 41: observed/expected fell from 1.000 (r = 1) to 0.856 (r = 8) in round 103; on fresh n in [10⁷, 3·10⁷) the ratio at r = 8 is 1.22 (round 108, pre-registered), so the fall was chance (*explained*).

**The archimedean layer at finite height (explained).** The (7/4)δ of the variance (round 95); the 7/8 of N(T); the race without the pole (round 102).

**The spectral swing (explained, L7).** MERSENNE §34's long-range variance shortfall (round 106); round 102's failed χ₃ reading (round 109). The values below the law at H = 10⁵–10⁶ in STUDY rounds 10 and 34 lie within their noise (t₉ from −0.35 to −1.6) and were not recomputed.

**Rigidity, and a noise model that ignores it (partly explained; a remainder open).** The repo has a long list of χ² values below their degrees of freedom: last digits 20.0–25.4 on 36 (rounds 85–86), k-th neighbour distances 8.2 on 19 (round 83), block errors 0.75–0.87 of binomial (round 85), the residue tables mod 210 at 0.691 (rounds 13, 39), prime bytes, the lighthouse, pair clocks (MERSENNE §10, §45, §53), the Goldbach comet 13 % narrower than sieved fakes (§28); as fractions of their nulls, 0.43 to 0.87. Counts of primes and of gap events per residue class are rigid, their long-range correlations negative, so binomial or multinomial nulls overstate the noise (*proposed* as the common reading; not tested item by item). Part of it is predicted by the local layer: the Hardy–Littlewood long-range sums give the residue tables 0.847 against 1 for independent draws, about half of the measured shortfall (round 39), and they predict most of the gap-event rigidity of round 40 (0.746 and 0.834 of Poisson, against 0.595 and 0.797 measured). The remainder is *open*: measured/predicted 0.80 at train and 0.79 at 10⁸–10⁹ (1.00 at A) after calibration (round 40, eighth review), and twin counts spread across residue classes more evenly than the four-point sums predict (u² = 0.13 against 0.45 at 10⁸–10⁹, also low in a short window at 10¹⁰; Fisher p ≈ 0.002 over three windows; rounds 41–44). Twins have no L-function, so no explicit formula supplies the missing term; whether the zeros of L-functions mod q are as rigid as ζ's (L6) is untested.

**Polynomial tails (open).** The heuristic chance per candidate overestimates the run-length tail of sextic searches by about 10³ (STUDY polynomial section).

**Clarified by this map.** RIEMANN §18.1 (the lowest zero against small primes): L3 gives a rank prediction of 0.94–0.99 from fixed weights, with the caveat of round 107; the low-zero discriminants with the "wrong" race sign (L5); MERSENNE §34 and round 102 (L7).

## 5. What "not random" means here

A reading, *heuristic*. The primes are not random in any layer. The local layer is a sieve, described by the singular series for every pattern the repo has tested (a conjecture for patterns, a theorem for single residue classes); the archimedean layer is a smooth function of size. What looks random is the spectral layer, the sum over zeros, and that is not free either: the zeros are recovered from the primes (backwards decoding, RIEMANN §6 and §13; the lowest zero from a weighted prime sum, L3), and ζ's zeros are more rigid than random matrices because of the primes (L6). What behaves like chance is that the heights γ are believed to be linearly independent over the rationals (a conjecture), so sums of x^(iγ) behave like sums of independent phases. In one sentence: for linear statistics the primes and the zeros determine each other through one exact identity, and what looks random is the incommensurability of the zeros' heights.

## 6. Open tests

- **L8 beyond degree 2:** the first zero of ζ_K/ζ for the cubic, quartic and quintic records against fields of similar discriminant (needs a larger machine or an explicit-formula estimate of the first zero).
- **Twins across classes:** a long-range correction to the four-point sums, of the kind L2 gives single primes.
- **The low-χ² family:** recompute the χ² of rounds 83–86 with errors from the rigidity of round 40 instead of binomial errors; the prediction is χ²/dof near 1.
- **Order 3 of the gap laws:** whether the long-gap excess (law high) and the twin-gap tail (law low) come from two different lower-order terms.
- **Rigidity of L-function zeros mod q:** round 110 found every Dirichlet L-function tested below GUE in spacing variance, but not as a function of the density alone (real q = 3, 4, 5 more rigid than ζ at equal ln(qt/2π), z ≈ −8; complex characters in between; χ₋₁₆₃ less rigid). What sets the order is open; the Bogomolny–Keating arithmetic factor for each L-function is the next thing to compute.
