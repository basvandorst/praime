# Adversarial review of rounds 159 and 161 (26 Nov 2026)

Everything here was computed with my own scripts in this folder. They read the stored data and the frozen prediction files and import nothing from the repo.

## Round 159 (Sym^2, prime level, k = 2, 4): verdict STANDS as a computation, WEAKENED in what it means

1. **Reproduced.** I rebuilt nu(m) from nu_l(e) in derivation.md and M_k(y) from the STUDY.md formula. I then binned the data myself (dim-weighted bin means, empirical bin errors).
   - k=2: amplitude 1.0420 +- 0.0699, chi2 51.44 / 60.
   - k=4: amplitude 1.0041 +- 0.0746, chi2 61.90 / 60.
   - Both match the registered values to all printed digits.
   - Jackknife over levels: 0.073 for k=2 and 0.039 for k=4. The per-level amplitude z rms is 0.93 and 0.50. So the registered errors are right for k=2 and conservative for k=4 (`r159_fit.py`).
2. **Exact decomposition of the data** (`r159_exact.py`, PARI qfbhclassno for every (N, p, m), all 22 levels). The data are V = Plancherel + hyperbolic A3 (exact) + CN_exact (the exact class-number term A2^{N^2}) + R (the twisted sum A2^chi).
   - CN_exact against its nu-average: chi2 in data errors is 0.1 / 55 (k=2) and 0.0 / 55 (k=4). The pooled ratio is 0.99992 and 0.99931.
   - So the class-number average is right to about 1e-4 and carries no test noise. Checking it needs no forms.
   - The whole test is therefore a test of R, the normalized A2^chi, against its registered main term 24 rho y^{3/2} [k=2] (0 for k=4).
   - R against that term: chi2 51.1 / 60 and 61.6 / 60. In y < 1/4: 3.3 / 5 and 3.4 / 5. For k=2 the fitted coefficient of R is 0.9994 +- 0.0027.
   - Only one conjectural step is tested: heuristic step 2.4 (A2^chi averages to its smooth term and has no term of size p^{k-1}). Plainly, the blind test establishes the family mean at 22 small prime levels, which is the same thing as step 2.4 at those levels. It cannot establish the limit N -> oo. It cannot establish anything about degree 3.
3. **Where the signal sits.** Delta chi2 against no signal is 222 (k=2), of which 127 comes from y < 1/4. For k=4 it is 181, of which 59 comes from y < 1/4.
   - Below 1/4 the prediction is the exact A3 hyperbolic term plus the claim of step 2.4. The registration says the survival of the hyperbolic term was decided on discovery data.
   - For k=2 the smooth part and the class-number part cancel to leading order. With the smooth part fixed, the class-number scale is 1.0007 +- 0.0030. That only says that mean nu = 1 and step 2.4 hold.
4. **Class-number part alone** (y > 1/4, smooth part fixed, scale free). I compared nu with three alternatives: nu = 1, a squarefree-only nu, and Zubrilina's local factor 1 + l^2/(l^4 - 2l^2 - l + 1) for l | m.
   - Delta chi2: 190, 103 and 82 for k=2; 86, 40 and 38 for k=4.
   - A free fit of nu(1..11) agrees with nu: Mahalanobis 7.3 (k=2) and 10.7 (k=4) on 11 dof.
   - Precision per m: about +-0.05 at m=1, up to +-0.24 at m=11 (k=2); twice that for k=4. So the forms data decide nu at 6-14 sigma overall, but only coarsely per m. The direct class-number check is about 1000 times sharper.
5. **Trend with N.** k=2 levels 211-241 give 0.934 +- 0.104; levels 251-283 give 1.103 +- 0.092 (1.2 sigma apart). For k=4: 1.025 +- 0.116 and 0.996 +- 0.097. Discovery levels (101-199) gave 0.98 and 1.03. No trend is visible at the 10 % level, and the limit is not tested.
6. **Degree-3 link.** chi(-2w) Ktilde21(w) = G_3(iw) holds to 3e-30 at 3 complex w x k = 2, 4, 6 (`r159_kernel.py`). Ktilde21 is my closed form from DLMF 10.22.57, checked by quadrature to 2e-5 (`r159_kernel2.py`). Prod(1 - l^-2 + l^-5) = 0.636446.
   - The data do not test the link. The GL(3) series is claimed formally equal to the closed form, it was never summed numerically, and its main line alone is rejected (Delta chi2 217 and 233).
   - The fair reading holds: at y <= 3 the corners at m/4 dominate, and nothing of degree 3 is visible.
- **Honest size.** This is an explicit trace-formula evaluation of the Sym^2 family mean at p ~ N^2, confirmed at 22 small prime levels. Its one heuristic, step 2.4, holds there. It is not evidence for a degree-3 law. It plausibly becomes a theorem with averaging over N, by Zubrilina-type methods (not checked).

## Round 161 (S3 cubic fields): verdict WEAKENED

1. **Data reproduced exactly.** A fresh nflist gives the same field lists (3,421 and 938). I recounted all 4,359 fields with my own root counts (x^p mod (f, p) plus the Legendre symbol of disc f; brute force for p < 50; idealprimedec only at 1,484 index-divisor primes). Every per-field bin sum is identical to the stored one (`r161_recount.py`).
2. **Amplitude by range**, jackknife over fields. In brackets: the jackknife over 40 |D|-blocks and over groups of fields with the same D.

   | range | D < 0 | D > 0 |
   |---|---|---|
   | (0,3] | 1.088 +- 0.015 (0.009, 0.016): fails at 6 sigma | 1.198 +- 0.028: fails at 7 sigma |
   | (0.2,3] | 1.043 +- 0.018: 2.4 sigma, passes | 1.138 +- 0.033: 4.2 sigma, fails |
   | (0.4,3] | 1.047 +- 0.019: 2.4 sigma, passes | 1.123 +- 0.035: 3.5 sigma, fails |
   | (0.4,1] | 1.038 +- 0.029 | 1.169 +- 0.048 |
   | (1,2] | 1.055 +- 0.030 | 1.103 +- 0.069 |
   | (2,3] | 1.048 +- 0.035 | 1.068 +- 0.063 |

   - The errors are right: the three jackknives agree within 15 % (block jackknife smaller).
   - The excess is constant in y within errors.
   - The D<0 pass needs y <= 0.2 excluded, and that cut was chosen on discovery data. It holds on (0.2,3].
3. **Shape at the native resolution.** The registered test uses 13 coarse bins of width 0.2, which average out the spikes.
   - On the 52 bins of width 0.05 in (0.4,3], with the amplitude fitted and the full jackknife covariance: chi2 73.9 (D<0) and 75.3 (D>0) on 51 dof. That is p ~ 0.02 each, about 3 sigma combined (`r161_fine.py`).
   - Residuals above 2.5 sigma sit at y = 1.25 (D<0) and at 2.35 and 2.75 (D>0).
4. **Fixed-p two-term count comparator** (`r161_anal.py`).
   - Construction: E[a_K(p) | p unramified, |D| = Z] from N_sigma = C c_sigma Z + K k_sigma Z^{5/6}, with the main and secondary local densities at p, then sqrt(Z) E per field and prime with the data's ln p weights.
   - It equals the lines formula's small-y tail exactly: (5/6)K/C = -1.024947 and -1.775261, the registered c.
   - It equals the registered tt_secondary_only comparator within 5.6 % per bin.
   - It does not describe the data even at y <= 0.2: chi2 171 and 416 on 4 bins, against 1.7 and 7.5 for the lines formula times the finite-X factor. On (0.4,3] it gives 3,255 and 1,658, against 80 and 87.
   - So the lines formula is not this known result in disguise. But decision D (the y <= 0.2 amplitude equals 1/(1+eps)) is exactly the normalization of that count.
   - Caveat on the densities: only the (1^3) secondary local factor was read in a source (as quoted from BTT Thm 1.3 in arXiv 2601.19283). The unramified ones I reconstructed as mass x prod_{P|p}(1 + N(P)^{-1/3}), which reproduces that entry and the total of 1. The constant of the comparator depends only on the leading term x = p^{-1/3}.
5. **The spikes at n^2/4 are elementary.**
   - Every prime with 4p = t^2 + |D|n^2 (D<0), or 4p = |D|n^2 - t^2 (D>0), has a_K(p) = 2: 221,761 and 49,223 primes, no exception. Class field theory forces this: such primes are principal, so they split completely.
   - Removing them removes the spikes. Bin (0.25,0.30] goes from 6.85 to -2.26; bin (1.00,1.05] goes from 6.23 to -4.02.
   - Alone, these primes give c/sqrt|y - n^2/4| with c = 1.014 +- 0.018, 1.005 +- 0.010, 1.006 +- 0.016 (D<0, n = 1, 2, 3) and 0.987 +- 0.034, 0.983 +- 0.019, 0.989 +- 0.029 (D>0). The shape matches to 1-5 % rms.
   - The registered coefficient 1 is therefore an averaged Hardy-Littlewood density of primes of the form (t^2 + |D|n^2)/4. The one-point law is not needed for this feature.
   - Likely, not checked: the whole density is the count of primes represented by forms of small leading coefficient, weighted by the probability that the form's class lies in the index-3 subgroup. On that reading the J_0 kernels are the Poisson dual of a lattice-point count.
6. **Where the excess lives is unresolved.** The principal-form spikes alone fit with coefficient about 1.0. A two-component fit of the full data (spikes against the rest of the prediction) gives 1.058 and 1.042 (D<0) and 1.131 and 1.117 (D>0), with correlation 0.85-0.89. So the split depends on the weights and settles nothing.
- **Honest size.** A formula with no free parameter fits the shape of the cubic-field average at |D| ~ 3e4 at coarse resolution, but is marginal at fine resolution. The amplitude is 5 % and 12 % high. Its most visible features have elementary explanations: the tail is the known two-term count, and the n^2/4 spikes come from principal-form primes. Whether the one-point law adds anything beyond class field theory plus prime-counting heuristics is open.
