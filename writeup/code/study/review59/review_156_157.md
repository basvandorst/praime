# Adversarial review of rounds 157, 156, 154 (26 Nov 2026)

All code here is own code (scripts in this folder); the repo was only read. Compute used: about 12 minutes.

## Round 157: conductor and root number from a_p (verdict: stands as a test of the law; weakened as a capability claim)

(a) Leak, code reading of spectrometer_blind.py. The truth (ellglobalred, ellrootno, c) is computed in `truth()` after
`best.sort()`; it enters only rank, z_true and the printout. The names in testset157.json contain the conductor
("T00_N176") but the method never reads the name. The sha256 of testset157.json, spectrometer_blind.py and spectro_lib.py
match the registration. The input a(p) is PARI ellap on the given model: all 40 models are minimal (u = 1), and own point
counts agree with ellap at every p <= 1000 (0 mismatches). No leak found.
Not a leak, but a misleading baseline: "chance 1 in 11,998" ignores the bad primes, which are visible in the data
(a(l) in {0, +-1}, exponent 1 if |a(l)| = 1, exponent 2 if a(l) = 0 and l >= 5). These local rules alone leave a median of 49
candidate conductors (min 11, max 154; truth always among them), about 100 (N, eps) pairs. The rival N +- 1 (N > 2000) would
be removed by this filter. The rivals 4N, 9N, N/3, N/4 stay (they differ only in the exponent at an additive 2 or 3).

(b) Trivial comparator: the functional-equation check (`t1_fe_detector.py`, `t1_fe_out.txt`). For every N' <= 6000 and
eps = +-1: build a_n from a(p) (Hecke at p !| N', a(p)^k at p | N'), phi(t) = sum a_n exp(-2 pi n t/sqrt N'), and test
phi(1/t) = eps t^2 phi(t) at t = 1.15, 1.3, 1.45. Result: 40 of 40 with a(p) for p <= 1000 (truth: discrepancy <= 7e-15,
best wrong >= 1.4e-4), 40 of 40 with p <= 300 (5e-10 against 1.4e-4), 40 of 40 with p <= 150, 34 of 40 with p <= 80.
The spectrometer uses 215,587 primes in [1e4, 3e6]. So N and eps follow from about 35-62 primes with a margin of 5 orders of
magnitude. This is the classical method (PARI lfunconductor, Dokchitser), and it needs no conjecture beyond modularity.
What the spectrometer adds is not the capability. It adds (i) a test of the predicted line weights, at additive primes
too, and (ii) D4, the amplitude c to a median of 1.3 %. The FE check says nothing about c. The defensible headline is
"the law's lines and constant hold on 40 new curves", not "N and eps can be read from a_p".

(c) Own statistic (`t1c_ownz.py`, `t1c_out.txt`): direct sums at the exact line positions, own weights, PARI a(p).
z_true: T07 40.88 (stored 40.86), T13 50.81 (50.82), T22 21.55 (21.50), T38 22.22 (22.18). Rivals: T13 at N' = 123, eps = -1:
24.15 (stored 24.1); T38 at N' = 2944: 11.49 (11.5). Agreement within 0.25 %. Calibration: the exact random-sign variance,
which includes the overlap of nearby lines, gives z within 1 % of the registered z (ratio 0.99-1.01). A permutation null
with 20 draws gives z 10 % lower or higher (noisy). So the "errors" are honest. Only 4 curves were done, not 5.

(d) Prior art. Pozdnyakov, "Predicting root numbers with neural networks", arXiv 2403.14631 (abstract read): ML on
murmurations and Mestre-Nagao sums; evidence that standard ML cannot predict eps from few traces. The search snippet says the
exact method needs about N^(1/2) traces; this was not verified. Farmer-Koutsoliotas-Lemurell, "Varieties via their
L-functions", arXiv 1502.00850: conductor, sign and bad factors from the FE (search snippet only, not opened). PARI
lfunconductor: documentation read in the local cypari2 (a heuristic search of N in [1, setN] by FE checks). Mestre 1986
(conductor bounds from the explicit formula) and Dokchitser 2004: from memory, not opened. A detection of N from the
resonance lines on primes was not found, but it is dominated by these methods.

## Round 156: shifted primes for the Hecke character of Q(i) (verdict: stands; the sharp part is the factor at 3)

(a) `t2_shift.py` (own segmented sieve; lambda(n) = (1/4) sum_{a^2+b^2=n} Re((a+bi)^4)/n^2 by lattice enumeration, no
factoring) on primes in [5e8, 7e8): 20 chunks, 9,897,064 primes. Chunk counts are identical to sums_holdout.jsonl.gz, and
the sums for h = 2 and h = 6 agree to 6e-15 and 9e-15 noise units. The sub-range reading (main term integrated over the
sub-range): h = 2: 1.054 +- 0.056, h = 6: 1.034 +- 0.045 (noise errors). Constants from the own Euler product (`t2_euler.py`,
l <= 1e7, the same at 1e5): C(2) = 0.77910, C(6) = 1.24657, ratio exactly 1.6000 = (4/3)/(5/6). This is the factor at the
inert prime 3, where lambda(3) = 0 and rho = -1/3. Checks: lambda(5) = -0.56, lambda(9) = 1, lambda(25) = lambda(5)^2 - 1.

(b) Not a theorem. Even the one-prime main term is the conditional heuristic of Iwaniec-Luo-Sarnak. Pitt (lambda(p - 1))
and Zhao 2006 (math/0508129, abstract only; the snippet says a bound N^(5/6) for sum lambda(n) Lambda(n) e(alpha sqrt n))
give bounds with no main term, and the main term is about x^(3/4). The constants are a singular-series computation: the
density of p + h in each class mod l times the per-class resonance over the integers. That is a Hardy-Littlewood/
Bateman-Horn independence heuristic. Status: a conjecture with numerics, as STUDY says.

(c) Separation, in registered errors, from the holdout readings (h = 2: 0.9905 +- 0.0262; h = 6: 1.0217 +- 0.0240), given as
h = 2 / h = 6 / chi^2: registered -0.4 / 0.9 / 0.9; no factor at 3: -8.0 / 11.3 / 193; sign of rho flipped: -23 / 19 / 895;
l | h rule ignored: -0.4 / 16.6 / 274; integers: -11.2 / 9.2 / 209. But for single factors at l >= 5: no factor at 5:
-1.7 / -0.6 / 3.3; no factor at 7: -1.3 / -0.1 / 1.7; none at 5..13: -2.8 / -1.8 / 10.9; only l <= 3: -3.0 / -2.0 / 13.1.
So the test decides the rule at l = 3. The product over l >= 5 shows only as a whole, at about 3 errors. Single factors at
l >= 5 are not resolved.
