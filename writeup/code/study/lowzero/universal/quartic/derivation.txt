0. Object [proved, standard]. K an S4 quartic field, L(s, rho_K) = zeta_K/zeta: degree 3, conductor |D|, root number +1, det rho = sign character
   = chi_D (the quadratic resolvent Q(sqrt D); the cubic resolvent has the same discriminant class), a_K(p) = #roots - 1 (trace of the standard
   representation). Gamma factor from rho(complex conjugation): r2 = 0: Gamma_R(s)^3 (mu = 000); r2 = 1 (D < 0): Gamma_R(s)^2 Gamma_R(s+1) (001);
   r2 = 2: Gamma_R(s) Gamma_R(s+1)^2 (011). Three families, fields X <= |D| < 2X of one signature.
1. Kernel and normalisation [derived]. Dictionary of murmur_gate.md 3.1 in degree d: E[sqrt p lambda(p)] = M(p/Q) with M(y) = sum_rho A(rho) K(rho y),
   int_0^oo z^(w-1) K(z) dz = prod_j Gamma_R(1/2+w+mu_j)/Gamma_R(1/2-w+mu_j). Statistic m(y) = E[sqrt|D| a_K(p)] = M(y)/sqrt y = sum_rho B(rho) k(rho y),
   B = A sqrt rho, k(z) = z^(-1/2) K(z), Mellin transform Kt(w) = prod_j Gamma_R(w+mu_j)/Gamma_R(1-w+mu_j). k is bounded up to logs at 0 (poles of Kt
   at w = 0 of order #{mu_j = 0} = 3, 2, 1) and by stationary phase k(z) ~ C z^(-1/6) cos(6 pi z^(1/3) + phi): m is O(1) at small y and its
   oscillation decays like y^(-1/6); the O(1) oscillating normalisation is y^(1/6) m = E[|D|^(1/3) p^(1/6) a_K(p)]; natural variable u = y^(1/3).
   Checked: Meijer-G form 2 G^{3,0}_{0,6}((pi^3 z)^2 | a; 1-a), a_j = (1/2+mu_j)/2, against the Mellin-Barnes integral on Re w = 0.1, 18 cases,
   <= 9.4e-7; degree-2 version = 2 pi J_0 and 2 pi(-Y_0 + (2/pi)K_0) of round 161 exactly (kcheck4_out.txt). [computed]

4. Prime powers [derived]. Squares: E[sum alpha^2] = Frobenius-Schur indicator = 1 gives M = 1/2, i.e. -1/(2 sqrt y) in m; cubes: E[sum alpha^3] = 1
   gives (1/3)(yX)^(-1/6) -> 0: no cube term at the conductor scale. Res_{s=1/2} D = 1/2 exactly, since sum_v F_l(v) l^(-v/2) = l/(l-1) for every
   type: the y^(-1/2) terms cancel, as E[a_K(p)] = (1/p + 1/p^2)/Z_p at fixed p requires (no p^(-1/2) bias). The identity is per type, so it survives
   any change of the local frequencies (finite-X models below).
5. Small y [derived given 3]. m(y) = c3 y^(-1/3) + c4 y^(-1/4) + O(log^2), poles of zeta(3s), zeta(4s): c3 = (1/3) A1 zeta(2/3) zeta(4/3) Q(1/3) Kt(1/3)
   = -9.2985 (r2=0), -5.3685 (r2=1), -3.0995 (r2=2) (ratio 3 : sqrt3 : 1 from Kt); c4 = +14.534, +6.020, +2.494; they nearly cancel for y < 0.25.
   Reading (heuristic, as in round 161): c3 <-> an X^(5/6) secondary term with a p^(-1/3) root-count bias, c4 <-> an X^(3/4) term with p^(-1/4).
   Shankar-Tsimerman (arXiv 2503.02381, read through a summary of the HTML) prove X + X^(5/6) for S4-families with archimedean masses M_i = M, sqrt3 M, M
   (and M, sqrt3 M, M/3 for the Hasse-invariant part): their signature dependence does not match 3 : sqrt3 : 1 for any combination (r2=1 vs r2=0):
   UNRESOLVED (either the identification of 5 or our reading of their constants is wrong); not used in any decision.
6. Spikes [heuristic, stationary phase; not checked numerically]: the cube lines r n^3 have phase linear in n, so inverse-square-root singularities sit at
   u = k/(3 r^(1/3)) (main ones u = k/3, k/6); squares and fourth powers (phase n^(2/3), n^(4/3)) give no singularity. Bin edges at odd multiples of 1/24
   in u avoid u in (1/12)Z.
