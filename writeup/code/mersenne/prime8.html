Prime turtle

A turtle walks one step for every number 1, 2, 3, … and turns at the numbers that a rule picks out, for example at every prime. What path do the primes draw? Compare with a turtle that turns at random with the same frequency (the chance that a number near n is prime is about 1 / ln n). Galton board: prime7, race: prime6, balance: prime5.

Steps per second: 100

Does the prime turtle walk like chance? Measured up to 1,000,000

The rule "left at every prime". Diffusion: the mean squared distance the turtle covers in 1,000 steps, divided by 1,000. The fake turtles turn with the same density as the primes, each figure is the mean of 8 runs (they spread by about 0.2). The prime turtle covers clearly less ground than Cramér's fake primes. The reason is the length of the straight stretches, the gaps between primes: the turtle diffuses like the variance of the gaps divided by their mean, and prime gaps are more regular than random ones. A fake that also avoids the multiples of 2, 3, 5, … comes closer and closer: the regularity comes from the small primes (Granville made this point against Cramér's model). Up to 5,000 the same order shows for short windows (30 steps: 4.5 for the primes against 6.1 and 6.8 for the fakes, 4 to 5 spreads), while for windows of 300 steps the run is too short to tell. The page and a separate program agree on the end position, the turns and the farthest distance of every rule, up to 5,000 and up to 1,000,000.
