Prime radio

Turn the primes into a sound: give every prime p the wave e2πi p θ and add them up. At most dial settings θ the waves cancel and you hear noise. At the fractions a/q they line up, and you hear a station: its strength is about 1/φ(q) of the number of primes when q has no square factor, and nothing when it has one. Between the stations the primes are quieter than random numbers. Rain: prime12, Goldbach comet: prime10, waterfall: prime9, race: prime6.

The stations, exactly

At θ = a/q the sum depends only on how the primes fall into the remainders modulo q, so it can be computed exactly from those counts. Measured strength: |F(a/q)| divided by the number of primes, the mean over all a coprime to q. Predicted: |μ(q)| / φ(q) (1/φ(q) when q has no square factor, else 0).
