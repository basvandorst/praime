Prime race

A horse race of the primes. There is one horse for every remainder a prime can leave after division by a number q, for example the last digits 1, 3, 7 and 9 when q is 10. Every prime moves its own horse one step. In the long run all horses are equally fast (Dirichlet), yet some lead almost all the time. Watch the squares (marked ■): they lag. Balance: prime5, beads: prime4, gears: prime3, a Galton board: prime7.

Primes per second: 20

Every change of the leader

Backtest: a race to 5,000, checked to 1,000,000

For every q: how much of the time a square led (the squares are listed after the number of horses; if the race were fair in every respect they would lead a share equal to their number of horses), and which horse led longest, up to 5,000 and in the long run. All 40 races were run twice, by this page and by a separate program, with the same counts, times in the lead, first leads and changes of the leader. What holds: the squares lag at both sizes. What does not: which of the other horses leads longest; among them the race is close, and the winner at 5,000 is often not the winner at 1,000,000.
