Prime trains

Couple consecutive primes into a train; the train reaches the sum of its primes. 41 has three trains: 41 on its own, 11 + 13 + 17, and 2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 11 + 13. Some numbers have none (4, 6, 9, 11 …). Moser proved in 1963 that on average a number has ln 2 = 0.693 trains. Watch how slowly the average gets there. Compass: prime20, spot the primes: prime18, bricks: prime17, baskets: prime16.

How slowly the average reaches ln 2

Counted exactly up to 109 (by a separate program; the page counts up to 107), and by Sorenson and Waiss (2026) up to 1014: the average number of trains per number falls from 0.684 at 1,000 to 0.6450 at 1010, its lowest decade value, and then rises very slowly: 0.6477 at 1014. The smooth model replaces the k-th prime by the number below which the logarithmic integral counts k primes, and counts trains of these smooth numbers; it uses no other property of the primes, and it follows the true averages ever more closely (0.6 % high at 107, 0.004 % at 1014). So the slow approach is a property of the density of the primes. Worked out, the average is about ln 2 − (ln ln x − 1.96) / ln x: the gap shrinks only like ln ln x / ln x, and at 1030 it would still be about 0.03. The page and the separate program agree on all counts up to 10,000,000.
