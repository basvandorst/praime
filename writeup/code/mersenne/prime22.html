Prime window

Hold a window over the numbers from x/2 to x and count the primes in it. Bertrand's postulate says there is always at least one. Ramanujan proved in 1919 that the count grows past every n, and the last x at which the window held fewer than n primes, plus one, is the n-th Ramanujan prime: 2, 11, 17, 29, 41, … Slide the window and watch the count wobble upward. Doorman: prime21, baskets: prime16, trains: prime19.

How many primes are Ramanujan primes, counted up to 10,000,000

Straight from the definition, the number of Ramanujan primes up to x equals the smallest window count from x onward: min over y ≥ x of π(y) − π(y/2) (the yellow staircase); a separate program checks this at 103 to 106. Since the window count wobbles only a little below its trend, the share of the primes up to x that are Ramanujan primes is close to (li(x) − li(x/2)) / li(x), which tends to one half from below like 1/2 − ln 2 / (2 ln x). That one half is the theorem of Amersi, Beckwith, Miller, Ronan and Sondow (first observed by Sondow, Nicholson and Noe). The page and the separate program agree on the Ramanujan primes up to 10,000,000 and match the start of OEIS A104272.
