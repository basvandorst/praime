Pair clocks

Every prime q carries two clocks. The powers 2, 4, 8, … mod q repeat with a period; if that period is even, q divides some 2n + 1 (Hasse: 17/24 of all primes do). The Fibonacci numbers mod q first hit 0 at the rank z(q); if z(q) is even, q divides some Lucas number 2, 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, … (Lagarias: 2/3 of all primes). Now take prime pairs (p, p + d). How often are both clocks even? Multiplying gives (17/24)2 = 0.502 and (2/3)2 = 0.444 for every d. Our conjecture says the share depends on d, through the classes of p mod 5 and mod powers of 2, and is an exact fraction: for twins (d = 2) 11/24 and 4/9, for d = 6 only 2/9 on the Lucas clock, for d = 8 already 43/64 on the 2n + 1 clock. The fractions were committed before any pair was counted; then the pairs were counted to 109 and 1010. Rabbit clocks (one prime at a time): prime29, jumping champion: prime34, overview: all pages.

Why the share depends on d

Write e for the power of 2 in q − 1. Some cases are proved, the rest are coin flips with a known bias:

  • 2n + 1 clock: q ≡ 3, 5 mod 8 is always even (2 is not a square mod q); q ≡ 7 mod 8 is always odd (the period divides the odd number (q − 1)/2); q ≡ 1 mod 8 is even with chance 1 − 21−e.
  • Lucas clock: q ≡ 3 mod 4 is always even; q ≡ 1 mod 4 with q ≡ 2, 3 mod 5 is always odd; q ≡ 1 mod 4 with q ≡ 1, 4 mod 5 is even with chance 1 − 21−e.
  • Proved consequences: if d ≡ 2 mod 4, one of p, p + d is 3 mod 4, so a pair with both Lucas clocks odd never occurs. If d ≡ 4 mod 8, a pair with both 2n + 1 clocks odd never occurs (an odd clock needs q ≡ 1 or 7 mod 8, and adding d turns 1 into 5 and 7 into 3).
  • The conjecture adds two assumptions: prime pairs spread evenly over the classes mod 8 · 5 (and mod higher powers of 2) that they can occupy, and within a pair the two coins are independent. Then the share is an average over classes, an exact fraction. Example d = 6: one member is 3 mod 4 and always even; the other must be 1 or 4 mod 5. But p ≡ 4 mod 5 would make p + 6 a multiple of 5, and p + 6 ≡ 1 mod 5 would make p one, so that member lands in a good class a third of the time instead of two thirds (as for twins), and the Lucas share halves from 4/9 to 2/9.
