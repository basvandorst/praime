Prime gap heap

No box: the balls fall on one spot in the middle and pile up on the floor. Each ball drops straight down, rolls over the balls that are already there and stops where two of them, or one ball and the floor, hold it. A ball that lands exactly on top of another has no reason to go either way, so it takes turns: the first such ball rolls left, the next right, then left again. Choose what falls. Prime numbers of balls: round 1 drops 2 equal balls, round 2 drops 3, then 5, 7, 11, 13, …, each round in its own colour. Prime gaps: one ball for every gap between consecutive primes, in order, its diameter the gap.

This is not a physics simulation but the rule of rolling deposition (Visscher and Bolsterli, 1972): a ball that has come to rest never moves again, and a falling ball only rolls, without bouncing or sliding, so no two balls ever overlap and the same settings always give the same heap. Speeds 1 to 10 show every ball falling and rolling; from 11 on the balls are placed without the animation, more per frame at every step. The primes come from a sieve up to 1,000,000; the heap stops at 50,000 balls.
