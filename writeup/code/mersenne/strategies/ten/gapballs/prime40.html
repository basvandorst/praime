Prime gap balls

One ball for every gap between consecutive primes, in order: 2 → 3 is a ball of diameter 1, 3 → 5 a ball of diameter 2, 5 → 7 diameter 2, 7 → 11 diameter 4, and so on. The balls fall into a box, roll and settle. The box is 100 units high; you choose how wide it is and how fast the balls come. A gap wider than the box cannot get in.

Every ball is a real gap, computed from a sieve of the primes up to 2,000,000. Where a ball enters is chosen by a fixed random sequence, so a restart with the same width gives the same pile. The physics is simple: gravity, walls, and balls that push each other apart (heavier balls move less) with a little damping, so the pile is a picture, not a measurement of how tightly circles can pack.
