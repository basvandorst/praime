Prime gap stacks

Every gap between consecutive primes is a vertical bar, half as many cells high as the gap: the twin gap 2 is one cell, the gap 6 three cells, the gap 14 after 113 seven cells (the single gap 1, from 2 to 3, is one cell too). The bars drop one by one, in the order of the primes, into a column of the box and stack up. A row that is full from wall to wall disappears and everything above it drops one row. The game ends when the next bar does not fit.

Speeds 1 to 10 show every bar falling; from 11 on the bars are placed without the fall, more of them per frame at every step (up to 5,120 per frame at 20). The primes come from a sieve up to 20,000,000 (1,270,607 primes), so a game can run that far. "The lowest column" puts each bar in the lowest column (the leftmost if several tie); "a random column" uses a fixed random sequence, so a restart gives the same game; "p mod width" puts the bar that starts at the prime p in column p mod width.
