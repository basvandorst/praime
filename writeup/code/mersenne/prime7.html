Prime Galton board

Numbers fall as balls through rows of pins. At every row a property of the number decides: left or right. A fair coin would pile the balls up in a bell (the dashed line). Choose the property and the numbers, press Start, and see whether the primes fall like coin flips, or lean. Race: prime6, balance: prime5, beads: prime4, a turtle: prime8.

Balls per second: 20

Backtest: boards to 5,000, checked to 1,000,000

Ten rows. "Leans" means that the mean bin is more than 3 standard errors from the middle, or that the shape differs from the bell with p below 0.001. All 30 boards (three rules, five kinds of balls, two sizes) were run twice, by this page and by a separate program, with the same count in every bin. The Mersenne exponents are left out of the table: with 5 to 25 balls nothing can be told.
