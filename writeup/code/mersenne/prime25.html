Euclid's orchard

A tree stands at every point (a, b) with whole coordinates. You stand at the origin and look out. The tree at (4, 6) is hidden behind the tree at (2, 3): you see a tree exactly when a and b share no prime factor. A prime p hides a share 1/p² of the trees (both coordinates divisible by p), so the share you see is the product of (1 − 1/p²) over all primes, which is 6/π² = 0.6079. Star clusters: prime26, impostors: prime24, rain (the same product idea): prime12.

How close 6/π² is, counted up to N = 10,000,000

The number of visible trees in the N × N orchard is V(N) = 2 (φ(1) + … + φ(N)) − 1 (φ is Euler's function). Mertens showed in 1874 that V(N) = (6/π²) N² + D(N) with D(N) at most a constant times N ln N; Walfisz sharpened that to N (ln N)2/3 (ln ln N)4/3. Counted, D(N) / N wobbles around a fixed level: over [103, 107] its mean is 0.6079, the value of 6/π² itself to four digits, its spread 0.28, it lies between −0.18 and 1.39 up to 107, and it is negative for only 0.2 % of the N. The mean is no accident: V(N) = Σ μ(d) ⌊N/d⌋², and for whole numbers N the fractional part of N/d averages 1/2 − 1/(2d); put into the sum, this gives an average excess of Σ μ(d)/d² · N = (6/π²) N (a heuristic route to a theorem of Pillai and Chowla, 1930; the spread 0.2819 also follows from a classical mean square of Chowla). Montgomery proved that D(N)/N is unbounded in both directions, so these extremes will be passed further out. The page and a separate program agree on V(N) and on these statistics up to 1,000,000.
