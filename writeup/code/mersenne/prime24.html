Prime impostors

Fermat's passport check: if p is prime, then 2p−1 leaves remainder 1 after division by p. A number that fails is certainly composite. But some composite numbers pass: 341 = 11 · 31 is the first. These impostors are rare, and a few of them, the Carmichael numbers (561 = 3 · 11 · 17 is the first), pass the check for every base that shares no factor with them. How rare, counted and compared

The page counts the impostors to base 2 and the Carmichael numbers up to 10,000,000; a separate program counts them up to 109, and both agree with the published counts (OEIS A055550 and A055553, from Pinch and Feitsma). Pomerance's heuristic says the number of impostors up to x is x1 − (1 + o(1)) ln ln ln x / ln ln x; with the o(1) left out it overshoots by many orders of magnitude at every size anyone has counted. The local growth, how many times more impostors per factor 10, is far below 10 and rises only slowly.
