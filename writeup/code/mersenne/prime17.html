Prime bricks

Every number is a tower of prime bricks: 360 = 2 · 2 · 2 · 3 · 3 · 5. Now take away all bricks larger than y. Which numbers can you still build? With bricks up to 10 only (2, 3, 5, 7), you can build 1,273 of the numbers up to a million. Dickman found in 1930 that the share tends to ρ(u), u = ln x / ln y, a function that falls faster than any power. At every size we can count, ρ is too pessimistic. Spot the primes: prime18, baskets: prime16, rain (the opposite: numbers with only large bricks): prime12.

Three predictions against the count, up to 1,000,000 and 10,000,000

Ψ(x, y) counts the numbers up to x without a prime factor above y. Dickman: x ρ(u), with ρ = 1 up to 1 and u ρ′(u) = −ρ(u − 1) beyond. De Bruijn showed that the difference is about (1 − γ) x ρ(u − 1) / ln x, and he defined a finer approximation Λ(x, y) = x ∫ ρ(u − v) d(⌊yv⌋ / yv), which Saias (1989) proved to be accurate to a factor 1 + O(exp(−(ln y)3/5 − ε)) when y is not too small. At these sizes every step helps but none is exact: the counted share is above all three. The page and a separate program agree on the counts, ρ and Λ, up to 100,000, 1,000,000 and 10,000,000.
