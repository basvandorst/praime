# Puzzle 857: a cascade of 24 primes 4k+1

> **In short:** The record was a cascade of 23 primes under f(p) = 2ab + 1 (p = a² + b², a < b). The construction: (1) run f backwards: p maps to T exactly when ab = (T − 1)/2 with a, b coprime and of opposite parity; (2) factor (T − 1)/2 at the top of the record cascade and test every such split; (3) exactly one split gives a prime, of 86 digits. This gives 24 proven primes, one more than the record.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_857.htm)

**Status:** beaten · proven primes (PARI)

## The question

For a prime p ≡ 1 (mod 4), write p = a² + b² with a < b (unique) and set f(p) = 2ab + 1; f(k) = 0 if k is not such a prime. A cascade is a chain p, f(p), f(f(p)), … of distinct primes. Since f(p) ≤ p, it ends in 0 or at a fixed point.

**Q.** Send your largest cascade of primes 4k+1.

## How we found it

A cascade is a path in a tree that grows away from the end of the chain. Searching forward from small starts can only produce short chains, so we searched backwards from the top of a known long chain. Inverting f needs only the factorisation of (T − 1)/2.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). Inverting f.** f(p) = T means 2ab = T − 1 with p = a² + b² prime. Since p is prime, gcd(a, b) = 1, and since p is odd, a and b have opposite parity. So every predecessor of T comes from a split (T − 1)/2 = a·b into coprime factors, which puts every prime power of (T − 1)/2 wholly into a or into b, with a < b, opposite parity, and a² + b² prime. Conversely every such split with a² + b² prime gives a predecessor, because the representation a² + b² of a prime is unique.

**Step 2 (computed). The top of the 23-cascade.** (T − 1)/2 factors completely (PARI). Of its 32 admissible splits exactly one gives a prime a² + b², namely P with a = 338143537424881387458032794. PARI proves P prime. Following f from P reproduces the 23-cascade below it: 24 distinct proven primes.

**Step 3 (computed). No 25th prime above P.** (P − 1)/2 = 2·3·19·41·97·661·1307·Q with Q a proven 74-digit prime. None of its 128 admissible splits gives a prime, so P has no predecessor. A cascade of 25 would have to branch off lower down in the tree.
