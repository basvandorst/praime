# Puzzle 1132: The longest row of k-digit primes, one digit changed at a time

> **In short:** A row is a sequence of distinct k-digit primes in which neighbours differ in exactly one digit. The page asks for the longest rows for k = 3 … 6. Optimal rows were known for k ≤ 4 (all primes). For k = 5 and 6 the best were 8283 of 8363 primes and 64306 of 68906. (1) A row is a path in the graph whose vertices are the k-digit primes and whose edges join primes that differ in one digit. (2) A prime with no neighbour cannot be in a row of length 2 or more. A prime with one neighbour can only be an end. So a row has at most (number of primes) − (isolated) − (degree-1 primes − 2) entries: 8363 − 0 − 3 = 8360 for k = 5 and 68906 − 6 − 27 = 68873 for k = 6. (3) A rotation-extension search reaches both bounds. It starts at a degree-1 prime and inserts left-over primes between two consecutive row primes; this works because all primes on a line through a prime (one digit position varied) are pairwise neighbours. The rows of 8360 and 68873 primes are therefore the longest possible.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1132.htm)

**Status:** beaten · k = 5: 8360 (was 8283), k = 6: 68873 (was 64306), both proved optimal

## The question

Write a row of K distinct k-digit primes in which each prime differs from the next in exactly one digit position. Q1 asks for the largest rows for k = 3, 4, 5, 6. Q2 asks whether for each k a row can use every k-digit prime (OEIS A006879).

## How we found it

The page's contributors already describe the graph: vertices are the k-digit primes, edges join primes at Hamming distance 1. A longest row is a longest path, which is NP-hard in general. But here a simple degree count gives a bound, and a path heuristic meets it. Plain rotation-extension (Pósa) stalls near 55000 for k = 6. The graph's structure fixes that. Varying one digit position of a prime gives a "line" of up to 9 other numbers, and all primes on one line are pairwise neighbours. So an unvisited prime v can be inserted between two consecutive row primes that are both its neighbours, or after one 2-opt reversal when that needs one further edge. With these insertions the search reaches the bound for k = 6 in about a second (42104 rotations).

## The math

**Step 1 (proved): the bound.** In a path of two or more vertices every vertex has one or two path neighbours. Interior vertices have two, and only the two ends can have one. A vertex of degree 0 is in no such path. A vertex of degree 1 can only be an end. So at most two degree-1 vertices are in the path, and

```
K ≤ N − (isolated) − (degree-1 vertices − 2)       (when there are at least 2 degree-1 vertices).
```

**Step 2 (computed): the counts.**

- k = 5: N = 8363, no isolated prime, 5 primes of degree 1 (46769, 97039, 97919, 98519, 99721), so K ≤ 8360.
- k = 6: N = 68906; isolated are the six weakly primes 294001, 505447, 584141, 604171, 929573, 971767; 29 primes have degree 1, so K ≤ 68873.

No further local obstruction exists. Removing all degree-1 primes leaves no new vertex of degree ≤ 1. The forced edges at degree-2 vertices put at most two at any vertex and close no cycle. So the bound is not lowered by a simple argument; the search then shows it is attained.

**Step 3 (computed): the rows.** The search keeps two degree-1 primes as the fixed ends, deletes the other degree-1 primes, and extends from one end, choosing the neighbour with the fewest unvisited neighbours. When stuck it rotates: if the end e is adjacent to the row prime p_i, reversing p_(i+1) … e makes p_(i+1) the new end. Unvisited primes are inserted as described above. The rows found:

- k = 5: 8360 primes from 46769 to 99721 ([`data/p1132_k5.txt`](data/p1132_k5.txt));
- k = 6: 68873 primes from 756011 to 134129 ([`data/p1132_k6.txt`](data/p1132_k6.txt)).

Both equal the bound of Step 1, so both are longest rows.

**Q2.** For k ≤ 4 a row through all k-digit primes exists (the page's solutions). For k ≥ 5 none does: some primes have only one neighbour, and from k = 6 on there are weakly primes with none. The longest row for k = 5 and 6 still misses only what Step 1 forces. For k = 7 the same count gives K ≤ 586081 − 37 − 365 = 585679 (37 isolated, 367 of degree 1; computed, no row built).

## Validation
