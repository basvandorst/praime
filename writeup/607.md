# Puzzle 607: A huge prime power that never uses some digit

> **In short:** The record prime power that never uses some digit had 7022 digits. The construction: (1) powers of a prime with few nonzero digits are short digit blocks between long runs of 9s and 0s; (2) take the largest known palindromic prime, 10¹⁸⁸⁸⁵²⁹ − 10⁹⁴⁴²⁶⁴ − 1; (3) check which digits its powers miss. This gives its 7th power: 13,219,703 digits, no digit 6.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_607.htm)

**Status:** beaten (Q1) · proven prime (Prime Pages)

## The question

Find a prime power pⁿ (with n > 1) that is missing some decimal digit d entirely, as large as possible. ("d-less": for example a 0-less number has no zeros.) Question 1: send your largest d-less prime power.

## Current record vs ours

| | Prime power | Missing digit | Digits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current record (E. Vantieghem) | (19·10¹¹⁶⁹ + 1)⁶ | 2 | 7022 |
| **Ours** | (10¹⁸⁸⁸⁵²⁹ − 10⁹⁴⁴²⁶⁴ − 1)⁷ | **6** | **13,219,703** |

## How we found it

Vantieghem's idea on the page is the key: take a prime with only a few nonzero digits, like a·10ⁿ + b. Its powers are made of a few short blocks of digits separated by long runs of zeros, so most digits simply never get a chance to appear.

We pushed this to the extreme with the largest known palindromic prime, p = 10¹⁸⁸⁸⁵²⁹ − 10⁹⁴⁴²⁶⁴ − 1 (found by Propper and Batalov in 2021, proven prime, listed on the Prime Pages). It is almost all 9s, which makes its powers almost all 9s and 0s with a few blocks of binomial-coefficient digits in between. We checked which digits each small power misses:

```
p²: 3,777,058 digits, missing 3, 4, 5, 6
p³: 5,665,587 digits, missing 1, 4, 8
p⁷: 13,219,703 digits, missing 6
```

The 7th power is the largest of these that still misses a digit.

## The math

Write p = 10ᴬ − 10ᴮ − 1 with A = 1888529, B = 944264. Then p = 10ᴬ − u with u = 10ᴮ + 1, and by the binomial theorem

```
p^n = Σ_{j=0..n} C(n, j) · 10^(A·(n−j)) · (−u)^j
```

Because A is about 2B, the terms are spread far apart in the decimal expansion. Each term contributes only a short block of digits (from the binomial coefficients and powers of u), and the subtraction leaves long runs of 9s between them. So the whole number is a few short "islands" of digits in a sea of 9s and 0s, and a digit that appears in none of the islands is missing from all 13 million positions.

## The number

```
p = 10^1888529 − 10^944264 − 1          (1,888,529 digits, proven prime, Prime Pages)
p^7                                    (13,219,703 digits, no digit 6)
```

## Validation

This script builds p², p³ and p⁷, writes them out in decimal and lists the digits each one never uses. It runs in a few seconds. The primality of p is not re-checked here; it is proven and listed on the Prime Pages.

```python
import gmpy2

p = gmpy2.mpz(10) ** 1888529 - gmpy2.mpz(10) ** 944264 - 1

results = {}
for n in (2, 3, 7):
    s = gmpy2.digits(p ** n)                      # decimal digits of p^n
    missing = sorted(set("0123456789") - set(s))  # digits that never appear
    results[n] = (len(s), missing)
    print(f"p^{n}: {len(s):,} digits, missing {missing}")

assert results[7] == (13219703, ["6"])            # the 7th power has no digit 6
assert results[7][0] > 7022                        # larger than the record on the page
# not checked here: primality of p (proven, Propper and Batalov 2021, listed on the Prime Pages)
```
