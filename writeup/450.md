# Puzzle 450: Many prime factors in a sum of two consecutive primes

> **In short:** The sum of the twin primes 2996863034895·2¹²⁹⁰⁰⁰⁰ − 1 and 2996863034895·2¹²⁹⁰⁰⁰⁰ + 1 has 1,290,006 prime factors (counted with repetition). The best on the page was 195,005, from the twin primes that were the record in 2007.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_450.htm)

**Status:** beaten · proven primes (PrimeGrid, published proof)

## The question

Take two consecutive primes p and q and factor their sum, counting repeated factors ("extended factors"). For example 113 + 127 = 240 = 2·2·2·2·3·5 has six. The puzzle asks for sums of consecutive primes with as many extended factors as possible.

## Current record vs ours

| | Consecutive primes | Extended factors of the sum |
|---|---|---|
| Current record (J. K. Andersen, 2008) | 2003663613·2¹⁹⁵⁰⁰⁰ ± 1 (twin primes) | 195,005 |
| **Ours** | 2996863034895·2¹²⁹⁰⁰⁰⁰ ± 1 (twin primes) | **1,290,006** |

## How we found it

Twin primes k·2ⁿ − 1 and k·2ⁿ + 1 are automatically consecutive (nothing fits between numbers 2 apart), and their sum is k·2ⁿ⁺¹: almost entirely factors of 2. So the count is n + 1 plus the number of prime factors of k, and the record goes to whoever has the largest known twin primes of this form.

Andersen used the largest known twin primes of 2008. Since 2016 the largest known twin primes are 2996863034895·2¹²⁹⁰⁰⁰⁰ ± 1 (found by PrimeGrid, proven prime and listed on the Prime Pages). Their sum gives the new count.

## The math

```
p = k·2^n − 1,   q = k·2^n + 1,   k = 2996863034895,   n = 1290000
p + q = 2·k·2^n = k · 2^1290001
k = 3^2 · 5 · 18583 · 3583757            (5 prime factors with repetition)
extended factors = 1290001 + 5 = 1290006
```

The two primes differ by 2, so no prime lies between them: they are consecutive.

## The numbers

```
p, q = 2996863034895 · 2^1290000 ∓ 1       (388,342 digits each)
p + q = 2^1290001 · 3^2 · 5 · 18583 · 3583757
```

## Validation

This script factors k, counts the extended factors of p + q, and confirms the size. The primality of p and q is not re-checked here: a test on 1.29-million-bit numbers takes far too long, so it relies on PrimeGrid's published proof (Prime Pages).

```python
from sympy import factorint

k, n = 2996863034895, 1290000

f = factorint(k)                                  # {3: 2, 5: 1, 18583: 1, 3583757: 1}
assert f == {3: 2, 5: 1, 18583: 1, 3583757: 1}

# p + q = (k*2^n - 1) + (k*2^n + 1) = k * 2^(n+1)
extended = (n + 1) + sum(f.values())              # factors of 2, plus the factors of k
assert extended == 1290006
assert extended > 195005                          # beats the record on the page

# number of decimal digits of p and q
import math
digits = math.floor(math.log10(k) + n * math.log10(2)) + 1
print("p and q have", digits, "digits; p + q has", extended, "prime factors")
# not checked here: primality of p and q (proven by PrimeGrid, listed on the Prime Pages)
```
