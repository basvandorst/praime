# Puzzles 764, 766, 767: Rows, grids and cubes whose neighbour gcds are 1, 2, …, E — exact minima

> **In short:** Place distinct positive integers on a row of N cells (764), an N × N grid (766) or an N × N × N cube (767) so that the gcds of neighbouring cells are exactly 1, 2, …, E (E = number of neighbour pairs), with the smallest possible sum. The best sums on the page were 102 (row of 10), 508 (row of 20), 251 (3 × 3) and 338 (2 × 2 × 2), called "almost sure" minimal. The proof: (1) a cell with labels a and b on its two edges is a multiple of lcm(a, b), so the sum is at least the cost of the cheapest ordering of the labels with cost lcm(a, b) per neighbouring pair, computable by dynamic programming over label subsets; (2) a search that places real values cell by cell and prunes with this completion cost is exact and fast; (3) a constraint solver and a plain exhaustive search confirm the small cases. This proves 102, 508, 251 and 338 minimal and gives the exact minima for rows of 11 to 19 cells: 116, 151, 163, 206, 237, 252, 292, 371, 401. For rows of 50 and 100 the same structure, searched by annealing over label orders, gives sums of 3994 and 21226 against the records 4036 and 22062.

## The question

- **764.** N distinct integers in a row whose N − 1 neighbour gcds are 1, …, N − 1 in some order, with minimal sum. Q1: N = 10. Q2: N = 20, 50, 100.
- **766.** The same on an N × N grid: the 2N(N−1) neighbour gcds are 1, …, 2N(N−1). Q: N = 3, 4, 5.
- **767.** The same on an N × N × N cube: 3N²(N−1) neighbour gcds. Q: N = 2, 3, 4, 5.

## How we found it

The structure does most of the work. Write g_i for the gcd label of the edge between cells i and i + 1. Cell i is a multiple of both g_{i−1} and g_i, so of lcm(g_{i−1}, g_i); an end cell is a multiple of its one label. So the sum is at least the cost of the cheapest order of the labels 1, …, N − 1, with cost lcm(a, b) for neighbouring labels a, b and cost a for an end label: a shortest Hamiltonian path on the labels, exact by dynamic programming over subsets. This bound alone is too low (81 for N = 10, 470 for N = 20), because it ignores that values must be distinct and that gcds must be exact. But used as a completion bound inside a search that places real values, it cuts the search to a few thousand nodes. The grid and cube cases are small enough for a generic constraint solver and a plain exhaustive search.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). A cell is a multiple of the lcm of its labels.** gcd(v_i, v_{i+1}) = g_i means g_i | v_i and g_i | v_{i+1}. So an interior cell with labels a, b is a multiple of lcm(a, b), and the two cells of an edge with label g are two distinct multiples of g whose cofactors v/g are coprime (gcd(v_i, v_{i+1}) = g exactly). In particular the two cells of label g are at least g and 2g.

**Step 2 (proved). The value bound.** If the sum is at most S and the n values are distinct, each value is at most B = S − n(n−1)/2, because the other n − 1 values are at least 1, 2, …, n − 1. So all searches below are finite: B = 57 (row of 10), 318 (row of 20), 215 (3 × 3), 310 (2 × 2 × 2).

**Step 3 (proved). The relaxation.** For a set M of unused labels and the label g on the right of the last placed cell, let rem(M, g) be the least value of lcm(g, h₁) + lcm(h₁, h₂) + … + lcm(h_{k−1}, h_k) over orders h₁, …, h_k of M (the last cell is counted by its left label only; rem(∅, g) = 0). By step 1 the cells still to place cost at least rem(M, g). The table rem is computed by dynamic programming: rem(M, g) = min over h ∈ M of lcm(g, h) + rem(M − h, h); for N = 20 it has 2¹⁹ · 19 entries. The full-path version gives the plain lower bounds 81 (N = 10), 130 (12), 198 (14), 229 (16), 340 (18), 470 (20) ([code/p764_lcm_bound.c](code/p764_lcm_bound.c)); the true minima are higher because of distinctness and exact gcds (the relaxation likes "7 1 9", which makes the end cell and its neighbour both 7).

**Step 4 (exact search). Cells one by one** ([code/p764_exact.c](code/p764_exact.c)). Choose the first cell (a multiple of its label), then repeatedly choose the next label h among the unused ones and the next value w ≤ B, a multiple of lcm(g, h), unused, with gcd(previous value, w) = g exactly; prune when (sum so far) + w + rem(M − h, h) > S. With S one below the minimum the search returns "none", with S the minimum it finds a row:

**Step 5 (computed). Second and third programs.** The generic model ([code/gcd_graph_cpsat.py](code/gcd_graph_cpsat.py), OR-Tools CP-SAT): v_x ∈ [1, B] all different, g_e ∈ [1, E] all different, (v_x, v_y, g_e) in the table {(a, b, gcd(a, b))}, minimise Σ v_x with Σ v_x ≤ S. It reports optimal 102 (row of 10, 15 s), 251 (3 × 3) and 338 (2 × 2 × 2), each under 20 minutes. For the rows, a second program with a different organisation ([code/p764_orders.py](code/p764_orders.py): enumerate the label orders first, pruned by the same relaxation, then solve the value problem for each complete order) confirms "none" one below every minimum for N = 10 … 19 (N = 19: 203203 complete orders) and for N = 20 (240002 complete orders, a few minutes); CP-SAT itself did not decide N = 20 in 30 minutes. A plain exhaustive search without the relaxation ([code/gcd_graph_dfs.c](code/gcd_graph_dfs.c)) also finds nothing with sum 101 (row of 10, 3.3·10⁶ nodes), 250 (3 × 3, 1.2·10⁸ nodes) and 337 (2 × 2 × 2, 3.4·10⁸ nodes).

**Step 6 (explained). What the optimal rows look like.** Every cell in the rows above is lcm(left label, right label), except a few doubled cells forced by exact gcds (16 = 2·8 in N = 10, 32 = 2·16 in N = 20). Three patterns recur:

- *Doubling chains* 5 10 20 8 24 12 18 9 (labels 5, 10, 4, 8, 12, 6, 9): each label divides or is divided by the next, so every cell is just the larger label, the cheapest possible.
- *The label 1 sits between two bare primes*, as in … 7 | 11 … or … 5 | 7 …: the cells are p and q themselves, the edge has gcd 1, and p and q are at the same time the cheap end-cells of the labels p and q.
- *Large primes go to the ends*: label 17 or 19 costs at least 17 + 34 or 19 + 38, and it costs least when one of its cells is an end cell with no second label. In the N = 20 row both ends carry primes: 11 77 … 38 19.

**Step 7 (heuristic search). Rows of 50 and 100** ([code/p764_anneal.c](code/p764_anneal.c)). The same structure organises a search for large N: the state is an order of the labels, scored by the cheapest value assignment for that order (cell by cell, the smallest unused multiple of the lcm with the exact gcd; the multiplier must also avoid the primes of the next label's cofactor, or the next cell becomes impossible). Simulated annealing with reversals, swaps and insertions of labels, from the page's rows and from random orders, finds 3994 for N = 50 (two different seeds, same sum) and 21226 for N = 100. For the order found at N = 50 the greedy assignment is exactly optimal (checked by a branch-and-bound over the multipliers), so the remaining gap to the true minimum, if any, lies in the order. The search reproduces the proved minima 102 and 508 from every random seed.

**What this does not settle.** The minima for the rows of 50 and 100, the 4 × 4 and 5 × 5 grids (1647, 6480) and the cubes from 3 × 3 × 3 on. The subset table of step 3 is out of reach there. A weaker relaxation that keeps the distinctness but drops the path ([code/p764_cover_bound.py](code/p764_cover_bound.py): every label needs two distinct multiples among the N cells, each cell serves at most two labels; an integer program) gives the lower bounds 94 (N = 10), 447 (20), 3111 (50) and 14382 (100). The known optima are at 92 % and 88 % of this bound, the records for 50 and 100 only at 77 % and 65 %, so those records are probably far from optimal.

## Validation

[code/p764_verify.py](code/p764_verify.py) checks every arrangement on the page and ours ([data/p764_solutions.txt](data/p764_solutions.txt)): distinct values, gcd set exactly 1 … E, the sum, and prints the bound B of step 2. The three search programs are listed above. Run:

```
python3 p764_verify.py                                   # no dependencies, a second
gcc -O2 -o p764_exact p764_exact.c && ./p764_exact 20 507    # "none" in a few seconds; ./p764_exact 20 508 finds a row
gcc -O2 -o p764_lcm_bound p764_lcm_bound.c && ./p764_lcm_bound 20
python3 gcd_graph_cpsat.py path 10 102                   # needs ortools; also grid 3 251, cube 2 338
gcc -O2 -o gcd_graph_dfs gcd_graph_dfs.c && ./gcd_graph_dfs path 10 101    # "none"; cube 2 337, grid 3 250
gcc -O2 -o p764_anneal p764_anneal.c -lm && ./p764_anneal 50 30000000 7    # about 2 minutes: 3994 (seed 7)
```
