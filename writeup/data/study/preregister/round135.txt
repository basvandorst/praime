Round 135, pre-registered before the computation (commit order shows the order; STUDY.md section 1). No statistic below
was computed on these windows before this file was committed. Source: writeup/code/study/noise/ (derivation.md,
law.py -> law_output.txt section 8; all numbers below are copied from there).

The low-chi^2 family read as rigid class counts. Hardy-Littlewood pairs give the class counts N_a mod q in a window of
length H at x (lam = ln x):
  Var/mean v_q = 1 - [ln(H/q) + S_q - B]/lam,
  Cov/mean = -Lambda(m)/(phi(m) lam) with m = q/(b-a, q),
  S_q = sum_{p|q} ln p/(p-1), B = 1 - gamma - ln 2pi.
Any table of local prime events is, to first order, a linear function of the class counts mod Q = 30030 plus a
short-range remainder. The class-response law is
  E chi^2 = E_M chi^2 - FO_M + FO_HL   (derivation.md eq. 6.1),
the sieve model's value with its class-count covariance replaced by the Hardy-Littlewood one. For transition tables
this means:
  - the C-1 column directions (class contrasts) shrink to about v_q;
  - the rest of the table keeps the short-range value.

STATISTICS

S1(q), q in {3, 4, 10, 210}: equidistribution per block (the classical part).
- X_beta = sum_a (N_a,beta - N_beta/phi)^2 / (N_beta/phi), over the phi(q) reduced classes. N_beta is the block's prime
  count.
- R1(q, W) = sum_beta X_beta / (100 (phi-1)). R1(q) = mean over W1-W3.
- Error: the standard deviation of X_beta/(phi-1) over the 300 blocks, divided by sqrt(300).

S2(q, k), (q, k) in {(3,1), (3,2), (10,1), (10,2)}: block homogeneity of the transition tables (the new part).
- X_ab,beta = #{n : p_n in block beta, p_n = a, p_{n+k} = b (mod q)}, with a, b reduced and no limit on p_{n+k} - p_n.
- N_a,beta = sum_b X_ab,beta. Phat_ab = sum_beta X_ab,beta / sum_beta N_a,beta, per window.
- chi2_beta = sum_ab (X_ab,beta - N_a,beta Phat_ab)^2 / (N_a,beta Phat_ab).
- R2(q,k,W) = sum_beta chi2_beta / (99 * R(C-1)), with R = C = phi(q): 2 dof per block for q = 3, 12 for q = 10.
- Split of each chi2_beta into two parts:
  - col_beta = c' M^+ c, with c_b = sum_a (X_ab,beta - N_a,beta Phat_ab) and
    M = sum_a N_a,beta (diag Phat_a - Phat_a Phat_a').
  - within_beta = chi2_beta - col_beta.
- R2col = sum col / (99 (C-1)). R2within = sum within / (99 (R-1)(C-1)).
- Pooled values are the means over W1-W3.
- Errors: the standard deviation over the 300 blocks of the per-block quantity divided by its dof, over sqrt(300),
  times 100/99.

The comparators:
- Multinomial rows, or a first-order chain on the classes mod q (Anderson-Goodman): 1 for R2, R2col and R2within.
- The sieve model M_Q, a first-order chain on the classes mod 30030 with the sieve's gap law and no long-range rigidity:
  the M_Q columns above.
- The exact first-order chain mod 30030 with the Hardy-Littlewood gap law (r83.py class_chain at lam of W2, simulated
  in 300 blocks of 37,800 steps) is expected within 0.03 of the M_Q column. This is optional, and part of no reading.

READINGS (thresholds fixed here; sigma = sqrt(sigma_m^2 + sigma_p^2), with sigma_m the empirical error)
(1) Classical part. For each q in {3, 4, 10, 210}: |R1(q) - law| <= 3 sigma. For q = 210 also: R1 < 0.807 - 5 sigma.
(2) The law.
    - For each of the four (q, k): |R2 - law| <= 3 sigma.
    - For (3,1) and (10,1): R2 below the M_Q value by more than 3 sigma.
(3) The mechanism.
    - For (3,1) and (10,1): |R2col - law| <= 3 sigma and |R2within - law| <= 3 sigma.
    - Equivalently: the shortfall of the table sits in the column directions, and the rest of the table behaves like
      the chain.
(4) Falsifier. The law is falsified if either holds:
    - R2within for q = 10 (k = 1 or 2) lies more than 3 sigma below the law. That would be rigidity of the transitions
      beyond the class counts, like round 13's open remainder.
    - R2(10,1) lies within 2 sigma of the M_Q value 0.980.
    Reading (1) alone passing is the classical result, not a confirmation of the law.

Clarifications, written before the computation:
- sigma_p: 0 for S1 (the law is a formula); for R2, R2col and R2within the values of the R2 column (0.010 for q = 3,
  0.004 for q = 10).
- The class-count law v_q, with the logarithm on the class c = 0 only, is in substance Lemke Oliver-Soundararajan
  (2016, Prop. 2.1), Montgomery-Soundararajan (2004) and Goldston-Yildirim (1998, eq. 1.13). The class-response law
  for tables of events (eq. 6.1) and the split into a column part and a within part were not found in print.
- The law for tables uses a sieve model (a first-order chain mod 30030) as its short-range base; only S1 is free of it.
- The model windows at 3e11 in the source folder (rigid_3e11.json, sl_rigidcmp_3e11.json, sl_reg_W1..3.json) are
  simulations at the windows' ln x; they contain no primes.
