Round 144, pre-registered: the zeros of elliptic-curve L-functions (degree 2): each prime is an orbit weighted by its Frobenius eigenvalues.
|alpha_p^k + beta_p^k|^2: the number variance of the zeros of elliptic-curve L-functions, curve by curve.
To be committed with fresh_predictions.json before any zero of test F is computed. When this draft was written: zeros
existed only for the 20 development curves (dev_set.json; used, see below) and 6 CM pilot curves (cm_pilot_set.json;
used); no zero of any curve of fresh_set.json had been computed.
Derivation and code: deg2/ (derivation.md, deg2lib.py, deg2_predict.py, deg2_zeros.py, deg2_test.py, validate.py,
contrasts.py, registry.py, power.py).

Why. Rounds 117-141 confirmed, blind, that for a Dirichlet L-function each prime acts on the zeros as a periodic orbit
and a prime dividing the conductor is a missing orbit (two-point function to 1 %, three-point function to 0.6 %). In
degree 2 the orbit p^k carries the weight W_p(k) = (alpha_p^k + beta_p^k)^2 instead of 1: a prime with a_p = 0 is
missing at k = 1 (W = 0) and doubled at k = 2 (W = 4); |a_2| = 2 gives W = 2 at k = 1 and 0 at k = 2; |a_3| = 3 gives
W = 3; a multiplicative prime has W = p^-k (nearly missing), an additive one W = 0. So, unlike degree 1, a prime can make
the zeros LESS rigid, and a curve whose small primes have large |a_p| has Sigma2 above GUE for l = 2..9 (15 of the 138
fresh curves by more than 0.02 at l = 6). The formula is known (Bui-Keating-Smith, J. London Math. Soc. 94 (2016),
Conjecture RCd; D. J. Smith, thesis, arXiv:1607.03297, Heuristic 1; from the ratios conjecture by the Conrey-Snaith
method) and was checked there on the prime side only; zeros of elliptic-curve L-functions were compared with GUE at
leading order (Rubinstein, arXiv:math/0412181: pair correlation and spacings of the first 100,000 zeros of 11a, 14a, 15a,
17a, 19a); no test of its arithmetic terms on zeros was found.

The law (derivation.md sections 2-4; conjecture: ratios recipe; local factors derived here and checked to 1e-15):
  R(r) - 1 = (2/L^2) Re[ J(i eps) + e^{-i eps L} K(i eps) ],  eps = 2 pi r/L,  L = ln(N t^2/(2 pi)^2)
  J(x) = sum_p sum_k (ln p)^2 W_p(k) p^{-k(1+x)}: good p W_p(k) = (2 cos k theta_p)^2; p || N: p^-k; additive: 0
  K(x) = prod_p O_p(x)/(1 - 1/p)^2, O_p the local four-fold ratios sum (closed form in derivation.md 3)
       = zeta(1+x) zeta(1-x) L(1+x, Sym2 E) L(1-x, Sym2 E)/L(1, Sym2 E)^2 Aot(x)   (Aot absolutely convergent)
  Mean density (1/2 pi) L = twice the density of a degree-1 L-function of conductor sqrt(N); unfolding u = theta_E/pi,
  theta_E(t) = (t/2) ln N - t ln 2 pi + Im ln Gamma(1 + it); N+(t) = theta_E/pi - r/2 + S(t).
  Sigma2(l) = Sigma2_GUE(l) + 2 int_0^l (l - r) C(r) dr, averaged over the band with the zero-density weight.
  The symmetry type is unitary (GUE) for one L-function in the t-aspect; root number and rank do not enter.

Hypotheses (all parameter-free):
  H_full   the law.
  H_GUE    Sigma2 = Sigma2_GUE for every curve.
  H_deg1   every good prime has weight 1 at every traversal, bad primes missing orbits (Bogomolny-Keating 2013 at
           density L/2 pi): "the curve acts like a degree-1 L-function".
  H_k1     every traversal k >= 2 of every good prime carries the Sato-Tate mean weight 2 (repetitions blind to a_p).
  H_k1p2   the same for p = 2 only (the doubled orbit of a_2 = 0 is absent).   H_k1p3 likewise for p = 3.
  H_w1p2   the first traversal of p = 2 has weight 1 instead of a_2^2/2.     H_w1p3 likewise for p = 3.
  H_offzeta  the off-diagonal amplitude has the degree-1 form |zeta(1+ie)|^2 Phi_off prod_{p|N} F_p (no L(Sym2 E), no
           a_p in the off-diagonal term); diagonal as the law.

carried by the weights (|gamma_deg1 - 1| > 4 se), or the second traversal of 2 is not at its predicted size
(|gamma_k1p2 - 1| > 4 se), or the first traversal of 2 is not (|gamma_w1p2 - 1| > 4 se), or the p = 2 contrasts miss
their shape (chi2_p2 beyond the 0.1 % point), or the level is off by more than 0.002.
