REGISTRATION, round 157: reading the conductor and the root number of an elliptic curve from its a(p) alone
(a blind test of the line spectrum of the one-point law on primes, used as a detector).

STATE WHEN THIS WAS WRITTEN. The method (spectrometer.py, round 156, side result) was run on ten curves only (11a1, 15a1, 27a1,
37a1, 43a1, 89a1, 389a1, conductors 105, 1053, 5077a1), p <= 3e6, candidates N' <= 6000: 10 of 10 found. For the 40 test curves
below nothing was computed except the conductor and a(p) for p <= 97 (used to keep one curve per isogeny class).
A dry run of the blind script on 11a1 and 389a1 gives the discovery values again (z = 43.2 and 24.0).

METHOD (unchanged; spectrometer_blind.py is spectrometer.py with the curves read from testset157.json, a time budget and its
own output file; the diff is three blocks). Input: a(p), p <= 3e6, of an 'unknown' curve. lambda(p) = a(p)/sqrt p;
S(alpha) = sum_{1e4 <= p <= 3e6} lambda(p) ln p Hann(sqrt p) e(-alpha sqrt p). For every candidate N' in [2, 6000] and
eps = +-1: matched filter of S against the predicted lines alpha_r = 2 sqrt(r/N'), r = a/b, b <= 4, a <= 1500, with the weights
c(r)/c of the one-point law (local factors from the a(l) of the data; the primes dividing N' treated as bad: multiplicative
if a(l) = +-1, additive if a(l) = 0). Estimate: (N_hat, eps_hat) = the candidate with the largest z among the 11,998.
The truth (PARI: ellglobalred, ellrootno, c = B/L(1, Sym^2)) is used only to score.

DECISIONS (fixed now)
  D1 primary: (N_hat, eps_hat) = (N, eps) exactly for at least 30 of the 40 curves. FALSIFIER of the method: fewer than 20.
     Chance level: 1/11,998 per curve.
  D2: the true (N, eps) is among the three largest z for at least 34 of 40.
  D3: the root number alone: z(N, eps_true) > 0 at the true conductor for at least 36 of 40.
  D4: for the curves found, the median of |c_hat/c - 1| is at most 0.05.
  Reported without decision: hits by stratum (N <= 500, N > 500) and by reduction type (semistable or not), z of the truth,
  the runners-up (multiples and divisors of N are expected), and for every miss the rank of the truth and c.

WHAT A PASS MEANS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT. A pass shows that the rational lines of the one-point law (fact 2055; round 156,
H3) carry enough of the bias between a(p) and the fractional part of 2 sqrt(rp/N) to identify N and eps from primes below
3e6. It is a use of the law, not a further law; the conductor of a curve is known from its equation, and analytic tests
that find N from the functional equation exist. Not searched yet: prior art for a detection of N on the prime side.
