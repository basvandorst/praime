Round 142, pre-registered: L'(rho, chi_D) at the zeros of real Dirichlet L-functions, and what the primes dividing
the conductor do to it (a missing Euler factor = a missing orbit). To be committed with predictions_lprime.json before any
L'-value of the fresh sets is computed.
State when drafted: L' was computed only at the zeros of the dev set B (r134_B, 184 L-functions, 149,523 zeros,
100 <= gamma <= 600); on B only noise was looked at (bootstrap se and residual scatter; lprime_test.py --noise-only ->
noise_B_out.txt); no fitted coefficient, level or comparison with a prediction was computed or looked at on B. Disclosed:
an earlier pilot (frontier scout, 24 Nov) looked at the k = -1 sums of 12 B L-functions (even D pooled 0.91 with the
factor, odd 1.04). The k = 1 recipe polynomial was checked on 813 zeros of zeta below 1200 (ratio 0.9989). The machinery
was validated end to end on synthetic values built on B's zero heights (synth_B_out.txt). R120, Dn and Fn: no L'-value
exists. Derivation: derivation.md. Code: lprime_predict.py, lprime_values.py, lprime_test.py (self-contained).

Why. The physics picture of rounds 117-138: zeros of L(s, chi) are levels of a chaotic system whose periodic orbits are the
prime powers; p | q is a missing orbit; confirmed for the two- and three-point functions (rounds 134-138). The derivative
at a zero is the next object: Pearce-Crump (arXiv 2606.25094, Conjecture 7, June 2026; no numerics) conjectures
sum_{0<gamma<=T} 1/|L'(rho,chi)|^2 ~ (A_q/2pi) T, A_q = (6/pi^2) prod_{p|q} p/(p+1). No numerical test of any discrete
moment or of the distribution of L'(rho, chi) with conductor primes was found in print (derivation.md, prior art).

Laws (derivation.md; statuses there):
  L1 (recipe, exact in L; conjecture: ratios/CFKRS, Conrey-Snaith 2007 sect. 7.1 with zeta -> zeta_q, t/2pi -> qt/2pi):
     sum_{100<gamma<=600} |L'(rho)|^2 = int_100^600 m1(t) dt, 2pi m1/c_q = L^4/12 + (2/3)c0 L^3 + (c0^2+2c1) L^2
     + (-2c0^3 + 10c0c1 - 2c2) L + 2c0^4 - 12c0^2 c1 + 14c1^2 + 16c0c2 - 20c3, c_j the Laurent coefficients of
     zeta_q(1+s)/c_q, c_q = prod_{p|q}(1 - 1/p). Absolute: level and per-prime coefficients 0 in log.
  L2 (Pearce-Crump; conjecture, leading order): per bad prime the k = -1 sum is multiplied by p/(p+1); first-order (1/L)
     correction: none (LDM, (1+k)^2 = 0).
  L3 (mean; derived here from the ratios pair correlation, first order): a bad prime raises mean(log|L'(rho)| - ln L) by
     ln p ln(p/(p-1)) <1/L> (+0.048, +0.044, +0.035 for p = 2, 3, 5 at L = 10).
  L4 (variance, kappa3, kappa4, k = +-1/2; leading order conjecture + first-order LDM term, heuristic): a bad prime removes
     the cumulants of its Euler factor, kappa2 = Li2(1/p)/2 at leading order, corrected by -1.8667 (ln 2)/L (p = 2),
     -0.9753 (ln 3)/L, -0.4961 (ln 5)/L (per unit, derivation.md 3.3).
  L5 (within one L-function; heuristic, follows from L4's model): E[log|L'| - ln L | gamma] = const + sum_{p good}
     (A_p + E_p), A_p = -ln|1 - chi(p) p^{-1/2-i gamma}|, E_p = -(2 ln p/L) Re[w/(1-w)], w = chi(p) p^{-1/2} e^{-i gamma ln p};
     no dependence on gamma ln p for p | q.
Hypotheses for the alternatives: H_none (no dependence on the conductor's primes beyond L); H_lead (leading order only,
no 1/L term); H_half (half the predicted contrasts); H_Ponly (zeros part independent of the phase: E_p coefficient 0);
H_kept (the orbit p | q kept: a bad prime has the phase signature of a good one).
