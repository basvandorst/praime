Round 81, pre-registered before the computation (commit order shows the order; STUDY.md section 1).

A law for the k-th neighbour (own construction): for class a and distance D, the number N of primes strictly between
p and p + D has, at order 2 of the Bahadur expansion around the conditioned base {0, D} (y = 13), the generating
function G_a(z) = exp( sum_i M_ai ln(1 + p_i (z-1)) + (1/2) sum_{i != j} M_ai M_aj R_ij w_i(z) w_j(z) ),
w_i(z) = p_i (z-1)/(1 + p_i (z-1)), p_i = rho0 S_{>13}({0, D, i})/S_{>13}({0, D}), R_ij the four-point ratio (the
same ingredients as the gap law, which is the case z = 0). Then P_a(p + D is the k-th prime after p) =
rho0 S_{>13}({0, D}) [z^{k-1}] G_a(z), the coefficient taken by a discrete Fourier sum over 48 points of the unit circle.
lam = ln(1.05e9), D <= 240, classes averaged uniformly.
Checks (not readings): k = 1 reproduces the one-step repeat probabilities (0.4477, 0.1824, 0.1027, 0.0454, 0.0309)
within 1e-4; k = 2 lies near round 79's two-gap law.
Readings: the repeat probabilities P(p_{n+k} = p_n mod q | p_{n+k} - p_n <= 240) for k = 3 and k = 4, q = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13,
each within 4 standard errors of the primes in [1e9, 1.1e9) (restricted the same way); and k = 2 within 4 standard
errors too.
