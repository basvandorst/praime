Round 141, pre-registered: two spectra on the same orbits. The cross-correlation of the zeros of two different
Dirichlet L-functions, prime by prime, and the Gram-lattice term of nearly equal conductors. To be committed with
predictions_cross.json, predictions_cross_pairs.npz and swap_pred_NN.npz / swap_pred_NO.npz before any cross statistic
of the fresh design was computed (commit order shows the order). When the predictions were made: the zeros of Fn (r138_Fn) were being computed for round
138 and were not read; on the used real sets R120 + B + Dn (546 L-functions, 100-600) only NOISE of the cross
covariances was computed (noise.py, noise2.py: the residual dispersion after the regression on the predictors and
jackknife standard errors; no coefficient, amplitude or comparison with a prediction was printed or looked at).
Disclosure: that residual dispersion (0.0039 per pair at w = 0.3, 0.003 at w >= 0.6, against a predicted spread of the
law over pairs of 0.008 at w = 0.3 and 0.025-0.079 at w >= 0.6) already shows that the used pair covariances are linear
in the registered predictors (residual variance 25 % of the predicted variance at w = 0.3, below 1.5 % at w >= 0.6); it
does not fix any coefficient. Derivation and code in writeup/code/study/lowzero/cross/: derivation.md, crosslib.py, swaplib.py, readings.py,
predict.py, leak.py, swap_run.py, rcross.py (the analysis), check.py (independent recomputation).

Why. The physics picture: the zeros of L(s, chi) are the levels of a chaotic system whose periodic orbits are the prime
powers, orbit p^m carrying the phase chi(p)^m. Two L-functions are two spectra on the SAME orbits with different
phases. Rounds 117-136 tested one spectrum (the missing orbits p | k); the phases chi(p) never entered a one-function
statistic (only |chi(p)|^2 does). A cross statistic is the first place where the phase of each orbit is visible.

The law (derivation.md sections 2-4; the ratios recipe and the semiclassical diagonal sum give the same term;
conjecture). chi_1 mod k1, chi_2 mod k2 primitive and distinct, psi = chi_1 conj(chi_2) (0 at p | k1 k2), eps = t2 - t1:
  R12(t1, t2) = dbar_1 dbar_2 + (1/2pi^2) Re D_psi(-i eps) + R12_swap,  D_psi(z) = sum_n Lambda(n)^2 psi(n) n^(-1-z)
  = (L'/L)'(1+z, psi) - sum_p ln^2 p psi(p)^2 p^(-2-2z)/(1 - psi(p) p^(-1-z))^2   (finite: psi is never principal).
  Window counts in the common height t: dn_j(t) = #{gamma_j in (t, t+w]} - (theta_j(t+w) - theta_j(t))/pi;
  Cov(dn_1, dn_2)(w) = (2/pi^2) sum_p sum_m Re[psi(p)^m] sin^2(m w ln p/2)/(m^2 p^m)
                     = (1/pi^2) ln(|L(1,psi)| / sqrt(|L(1+iw,psi)| |L(1-iw,psi)|)) - (m >= 2 correction).
  Real characters: prime p adds A_p(w) chi_1(p) chi_2(p) + B_p(w) 1[p not | k1k2]; A_p, B_p >= 0: characters that agree
  at p give positively correlated counts, characters that disagree negatively correlated ones. Peaks of A_p: 0.104
  (p = 2, w = 4.5), 0.068 (3), 0.040 (5), 0.029 (7); correlation coefficients up to 0.25, 0.16, 0.10, 0.07.
  Swapped term: (1/4pi^2){(k1/phi(k1))(k2/phi(k2)) eps_1 conj(eps_2) e^(2i(theta_2(t2) - theta_1(t1))) H(eps) + c.c.},
  H(eps) = L(1-i eps, psi) L(1+i eps, conj psi) A12(-i eps). Its phase drifts like (k2/k1)^(it): kept by the recipe only
  for k1 = k2; at finite height it survives for nearly equal conductors (up to 0.046 per pair, rms 0.002-0.005 over the
  3,080 near pairs of NN); for |D1| = |D2|
  (opposite parity) it is exactly odd and adds 0 to equal-window covariances. It is the cross version of the
  Bogomolny-Keating off-diagonal term and of the one-point Gram term (sum over zeros of e^(2i theta(gamma)) = -T/2pi + ...,
  in print for zeta): two spectra locked to nearly the same Gram lattice.

Hypotheses (parameter-free):
  H_law    the law above, in its finite-range form on the registered grid (orbits n <= 300 with the exact grid averages
           of the products of their cosines; leak.py): every amplitude reading has the expected value in the table.
  H_indep  no cross-correlation (the zeros of distinct L-functions independent, the leading-order statement in print):
           every amplitude 0.
  H_flip   the diagonal term with the wrong sign: every amplitude -1.
  H_abs    the orbits are shared but their phases are not: Cov = sum_p (A_p + B_p) 1[p not | k1k2]: gamma_p = 0,
           gammaB_p large.
  H_noswap the swapped term absent (for k1 != k2 it is what the recipe keeps as T -> infinity): gamma_sw = 0.

Decisions (thresholds fixed now; the jackknife se of the data replaces the expected se):
 D1 (primary falsifier) H_law passes if |gamma_p - E_p| <= 4 se for p = 2, 3, 5, 7, |gamma_full - E| <= 4 se, and chi2_2,
    chi2_3 are below the 0.1 % point of chi2(16) (39.3); it is falsified otherwise.
 D2 H_indep is rejected if gamma_full >= 5 se and gamma_2 >= 5 se; H_flip if gamma_full + 1 >= 5 se; H_abs if
    gamma_2 >= 5 se and gamma_3 >= 5 se.
 D3 (Gram-lattice term) H_law passes X6 if |gamma_sw - 1| <= 4 se; H_noswap is rejected if gamma_sw >= 5 se. The
    swap predictor carries its own scale uncertainty (Euler product of H to 5e4: slope 0.956 against 2e5, which is
    itself not converged): a failure with |gamma_sw - 1| <= 0.2 is recorded as within that allowance.
 D4 The theory's own uncertainty is NOT in the bands. Computed: the finite-range terms move the amplitudes by <= 6e-4
    (in the expected values); the prime cutoff of the pair predictions <= 1e-4 per pair; the Euler-product cutoff of H
    scatters the swap predictions by about 10 % per pair. Not computed: lower-order terms of the ratios recipe beyond
    the finite-range form. If D1 fails with every |gamma - E| <= 0.003 this is recorded as a finite-range offset (with its
    sign and the primes involved), not as a falsification of the orbit law; likewise a failure of X3 with every
    |s_p(w) - E s_p(w)| <= 0.003 max_w A_p(w) (3e-4 for p = 2, 2e-4 for p = 3; the per-width se are 2-9e-5, so X3
    is sharper than the theory is known to be). Beyond these allowances the law fails.

Statuses: the cross two-point function is a conjecture (ratios recipe = semiclassical diagonal sum, derived here for
two characters; the swapped term's constants are copied from the one-function case); the covariance formula, its
closed form with L(1 +- iw, psi), the per-prime decomposition and sign, the finite-range form and the swapped-term window
integral are derived here; the numbers are computed. In print (derivation.md section 9): the leading-order independence
of distinct L-functions (Bogomolny-Leboeuf 1994 heuristically, Rudnick-Sarnak 1996, Liu-Ye 2002 rigorously for restricted
support, Murty-Perelli 1999 as a conjecture); a ratios-conjecture derivation of the pair correlation of two different
L-functions in an unpublished preprint (Conrey-Simons-Smolyarenko, not opened); the one-point Gram term for zeta
(arXiv 1403.7126). No numerical test of the cross term on zeros was found.
