Found after the fact in round 115's 61 real L-functions: when a small prime divides D (so that prime is missing from the
Euler product of L(s, chi_D)), the zeros are more rigid: spacing variance 0.139 (2 | D) against 0.155 (Mann-Whitney
p = 6e-6), 0.138 against 0.153 for 3 | D, 0.137 against 0.152 for 5 | D. Reading (ours): every missing short "orbit"
ln p lowers the weight of the arithmetic form factor at small tau and makes the spectrum more rigid, the same mechanism as
L6 (no prime below 2) for zeta. Replication on fresh L-functions: the fundamental discriminants with 100 <= |D| <= 400
that round 115 did not use, taking every third starting at the second (offset 1; about 60), same method (zeros up to 600,
spacings of zeros with 100 <= gamma <= 600).
Readings: (1) the mean variance with 2 | D is below the mean with 2 not dividing D, Mann-Whitney p < 0.01;
(2) the same for 3 | D and for 5 | D, each with p < 0.05 and the same sign; (3) a straight-line fit of the variance on the
number of primes among 2, 3, 5 dividing D has a negative slope with |t| > 3.
