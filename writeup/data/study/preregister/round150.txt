Why.  The Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating picture treats ln|L| on an interval of length 2 pi as a log-correlated field with
one branching step per prime; a prime dividing the conductor is a missing step.  For zeta, Fyodorov-Keating (2014)
put the arithmetic factor a(x) into the high-point measure and left open how arithmetic enters the maximum.  For
L(s, chi_D) the missing prime p gives a sharp per-prime question.  Theorem (second moment with lower-order terms):
the mean square changes per missing prime by R_p = (1 - 1/p)(1 + 2 ln p/((p-1)L')), L' = L + 2 gamma +
2 sum_{l|q, l!=p} ln l/(l-1).  Theorem (twisted second moment): multiplying |L| by its Euler factor |E_p| ("removal")
reproduces R_p exactly, while dividing an L-function without p by an independent Euler factor ("addition", the GHK
splitting) gives 1 - 1/p exactly and misses the lower-order term (development check: 1.000 +- 0.002 for both
statements, every p).  In the frozen phase nothing is known.
