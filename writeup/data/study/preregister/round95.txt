A correction term from the 7/8 of the zero count (ours, derived after round 93 and review 29). The ln X-average of
R(X, delta) is the diagonal D(delta) = (1/delta) sum_rho |(1+delta)^rho - 1|^2/|rho|^2 over the actual zeros (both
signs). Hardy-Littlewood's exact value for relative intervals equals the same sum with the smooth density
N0'(t) = ln(t/2pi)/2pi (round 93, to 6e-5). For gamma << 1/delta each zero weighs about delta, and the actual count
exceeds the smooth one by 7/8 on average (N(T) = (T/2pi) ln(T/2pi e) + 7/8 + S(T) + O(1/T), S averaging 0), so
  D(delta) = HL_exact(delta) + (7/4) delta + O(delta^2),  HL_exact = ln(1/delta) + B + (1+delta) ln(1+delta)/delta - 1.
Test: D(delta) from the first 10,000 zeros (computed with python-flint by the twenty-ninth review, checked against
our 2,419 zeros) plus the smooth-density tail beyond the last zero (scipy, period by period), at delta = 0.05, 0.02,
0.005, 0.002 (not used before). Reading: (D - HL_exact)/delta within 10 % of 7/4 at all four; reported also at 0.1, 0.01.
