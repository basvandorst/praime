Round 140, pre-registered: the within part of consecutive-prime transition tables is the all-order
Hardy-Littlewood local model. Nothing below has been computed on the proposed windows.
Model values are frozen in writeup/code/study/allorder/reg_numbers.json and come from cgsim4.py there, which sieves
only rough numbers at random 120-digit starting points and lists no primes. The model is a sieve model: it serves as
the comparator that says whether the deficit of round 139 is Hardy-Littlewood at all orders; it is no formula.

WHY. Round 139 (fresh, lam 24.16) found that the k = 1 within part, summed over all directions, falls with ln H
1.4-2.0 times faster than the linear-response comparator truncated at y = 17 (q = 7, 9, 11, 13), and that the mod-5
mixed-direction excess does not replicate. Derivation (writeup/code/study/allorder/derivation.md):
 (a) the within marks are doubly centred, so the class-level four-pair Hardy-Littlewood terms vanish in every
     direction (proved), and the large-prime response power is isotropic (computed), so no direction type is special;
 (b) the comparator is not converged in y: each prime adds response power kappa/p (computed, p up to 499), and this
     power switches on inside H in [1e4, 1e6]. The first-order sum without truncation (a closed, convergent sum over
     that range, Sigma f_p/p = 0.625) overshoots the fresh slopes by 3-5 errors for q = 9, 11, 13;
 (c) the all-order local model CG(P) (every local factor up to P, independent thinning, random profinite start)
     converges in P by 1e4-1e5 for H <= 1e6. Retrospectively (fresh numbers known, nothing fitted) it gives the fresh
     slopes for ten (q, k) cells with chi^2 = 14.5 / 10 dof, and the levels W(1e4) to 0.0004.
This round tests (c) on fresh primes at a third height.

LAW L. For each modulus q and lag k, the block statistic W(H) of the primes (review44/dirs.py definition, all
(phi(q)-1)^2 directions averaged) and its slope s = (W(1e6) - W(1e4))/ln 100 equal those of CG(1e5) at the windows'
lambda. CG(P): a site with no prime factor <= P is a pseudo-prime independently with probability
prod_{p<=P}(1-1/p)^(-1)/lambda; same statistic, same window length 1e8, same H.
