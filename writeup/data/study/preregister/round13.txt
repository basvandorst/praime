Round 13, pre-registered before the counts below were made (commit order is the evidence).

Calibration of the residue-conditioned law of round 7. For Q = 30 (8 states) and Q = 210 (48 states), and for each
window train, A, B, C: the table of counts N[a, c] of primes p = a (mod Q) whose next gap has class c = min(g/2, 20),
against the expectation N[a, .] * P_a(c), with P_a the round-7 law for that window (r7.trans, frozen, renormalised
per state). Pearson chi^2 over the cells with expectation >= 5 (the rest of each row pooled into one cell when its
expectation is >= 5, else dropped); dof = number of cells minus number of states. Pass: |chi^2/dof - 1| <= 3 sqrt(2/dof).
Also reported: the same table against the unconditioned law (cluster law, no residue), to show what the residue adds.

Addendum (after the first run: chi^2/dof mod 210 = 0.691 at train, 0.857 at A, 0.995 at B, 0.894 at C; the test
machinery gives 1.00 +- 0.06 on multinomial samples from the law, also when each block is drawn from its own law):
hypothesis: stratification. Given p mod 210 the next gap still depends on p mod 11, 13, ...; the primes fill these
classes almost exactly equally (far more evenly than chance), so the counts per class mod 210 are stratified samples
and scatter less than multinomial ones. Test before running: the law conditioned mod 2310 (theory2.S_cond, gaps
h <= 38, class h >= 40 as the remainder); null = each class mod 2310 drawn from its own law with the actual number
of primes in it, aggregated to mod 210, chi^2 against the mod-210 law as before (20 draws). Prediction: the null
gives chi^2/dof near the observed values (0.69 at train). Also reported: the chi^2 of the data against the mod-2310
law itself.
