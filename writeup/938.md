# Puzzle 938: Soothsayer sequences, extended

> **In short:** The page asked to extend both soothsayer sequences: numbers N whose i-th digit counts the prime factors of N + i, without or with multiplicity. The method: (1) OEIS A323084 (2019) already has five more terms of the second; (2) all 33 terms are checked by factoring. The question stays open with no new term; heuristically both sequences are infinite, with 0.9 terms expected per length.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_938.htm)

**Status:** open · not new: the five further terms of sequence II are in OEIS A323084 (2019), all verified here; heuristic for the growth

## The question

G. L. Honaker, Jr.'s soothsayer sequence lists the k-digit numbers N whose digits give ω(N + 1), …, ω(N + k), the number of distinct prime factors of the following k integers. Sequence II does the same with Ω, which counts prime factors with multiplicity. The page lists:

- **I:** 0, 1, 12, 21, 22422, …, 5335524234335 (the last terms from Resta);
- **II:** 0, 1, 12, 21, 4224, 153426, …, 3624263478.

Its question is: "Extend both sequences."

## How we found it

The scan of puzzle-page lists against the local OEIS copy (`references/oeis/`) flagged that A323084 had terms missing from the page. All terms, the page's and OEIS's, were rechecked by factoring.

## The math

Take a random k-digit N. Each of its digits is uniform on 0 … 9 and independent of f(N + i), where f is ω or Ω and is at most 9 at these sizes. So each position matches with probability exactly 1/10, and the expected number of k-digit terms is

9·10^(k−1) · 10^(−k) = 0.9 for every k.

The observed counts for k = 5 … 13 are 12 terms for each sequence, about 1.3 per length. The excess suggests a mild positive correlation between neighbouring positions. Either way the count per length does not shrink, so both sequences are heuristically infinite. A 14-digit term, between 10¹³ and 10¹⁴, should exist with chance about 1 − e^(−0.9) ≈ 60 %.
