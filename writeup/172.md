# Puzzle 172: Shortest complete prime rulers for K = 9 and 10

> **In short:** The shortest complete prime rulers for K = 9 and 10 had lengths 113 and 144 (2018), not proved shortest. The proof: (1) a shortest ruler uses each prime once plus w ones; (2) an extra 1 keeps a ruler complete; (3) a full search finds none with 12 ones (K = 9) or 14 ones (K = 10). This proves 113 and 144 are least.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_172.htm)

**Status:** answered (Q1, K = 9 and 10) · lengths found by Kamenetsky (puzzle 914, 2018); minimality proved here

## The question

A ruler is a rod with marks on it. The gaps between neighbouring marks form a sequence D = (d₁, …, d_k), and the length is L = d₁ + … + d_k. The ruler is **complete** when every whole number from 1 to L is the distance between two marks, that is, a sum of some consecutive gaps. In a **prime ruler** every gap is 1 or a prime. The puzzle asks for the complete prime ruler of least length that uses 1 and each of the first K primes at least once. Ray Opao solved K up to 8; question 1 also asks for K = 9 and 10.

## How we found it

**Only the number of 1s matters.** If a prime appears twice, or a prime outside the first K appears, replace that gap by the same number of 1s. Every distance you could measure before can still be measured, and the length stays the same. So a shortest ruler uses each of the first K primes exactly once, plus some number w of 1s, and its length is

```
L = w + (2 + 3 + 5 + … + K-th prime)
```

Finding the least L is the same as finding the least w.

**More 1s never hurt.** Adding a 1 at the end of a complete ruler keeps it complete: the only new length, L + 1, is the whole rod. So if no complete ruler exists with w ones, none exists with fewer either. One exhaustive search at w − 1 that finds nothing proves w is the least.

**The search.** We place gaps from both ends of the rod at once, always extending the shorter side, and stop as soon as some small length can no longer be reached. A small length can only be measured near the ends, so this prunes early.

## The math

```
K = 9:   primes 2..23 sum to 100,   least w = 13,   L = 113   (488 rulers, counting reversals)
K = 10:  primes 2..29 sum to 129,   least w = 15,   L = 144
```

For K = 9 the search finds no complete ruler with 12 ones; for K = 10 none with 14 ones.

## Validation

This script checks that both rulers are complete, use 1 and each of the first K primes, and have the stated length.

```python
from sympy import prime

def is_complete(gaps):
    # every length 1..L must be a sum of consecutive gaps
    L = sum(gaps)
    reach = set()
    for i in range(len(gaps)):
        s = 0
        for g in gaps[i:]:
            s += g
            reach.add(s)
    return reach >= set(range(1, L + 1))

rulers = {
    9:  "1.1.1.1.1.1.1.3.19.17.7.23.2.13.5.11.1.1.1.1.1.1",
    10: "1.1.1.1.1.1.23.1.11.17.3.13.19.29.5.7.1.1.1.2.1.1.1.1.1",
}
for K, text in rulers.items():
    gaps = list(map(int, text.split(".")))
    first_primes = {prime(i) for i in range(1, K + 1)}
    assert sorted(g for g in gaps if g > 1) == sorted(first_primes)   # each prime once
    assert is_complete(gaps)
    print("K =", K, ": complete, L =", sum(gaps), ", ones =", gaps.count(1))
```

That nothing shorter exists needs the exhaustive search, which is too slow in Python. [code/172.c](code/172.c) does it in C: it takes K and the number of 1s w, and prints every complete ruler it finds.
