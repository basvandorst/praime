# Puzzle 641: Cunningham chains of semiprimes

> **In short:** A chain is s, 2s + 1, 4s + 3, … with every term a semiprime; the record was 17 terms. The construction: (1) the terms are 2ⁱ·K − 1 for one odd K; (2) if K is a multiple of 3·5·7·11·13 = 15015, no term has a prime factor up to 13; (3) a number without small prime factors is a semiprime much more often, so each step survives with chance 0.4 instead of 0.2. Among the 10⁸ odd multiples of 15015 below 3·10¹² this gives a chain of 18 terms, starting at 1415485281209.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_641.htm)

**Status:** beaten · all factors proved prime (below 2⁶⁴)

## The question

Q1 (every chain ends) is proved on the page. Q2: find a chain s(1), s(2), … of semiprimes with s(i + 1) = 2·s(i) + 1, where (s(1) − 1)/2 is not a semiprime, longer than the known ones.

## How we found it

1. Every chain is 2ⁱ·K − 1 for a fixed odd K (the page's observation). A search over all K found 17 terms near 10¹⁰ and gets slow beyond.
2. Which K are good? If 3 ∤ K, every second term is divisible by 3 and must be 3 times a prime; similar for 5, 7, …. If instead K is a multiple of these primes, 2ⁱ·K − 1 ≡ −1: no term is divisible by any of them.
3. Numbers without small prime factors are semiprimes far more often than ordinary numbers. Measured on a million K near 6·10⁹: 8 semiprimes in a row occur 19 times for arbitrary odd K and 695 times for multiples of 15015.
4. Trying 3·5·…·y for y = 7, 11, 13, 19, 23 on small samples, y = 11 and y = 13 gave the most long chains (larger y makes the numbers larger). The full run used y = 13.

## The math

**Form of a chain (proved).** s(i + 1) + 1 = 2·(s(i) + 1), so s(i) + 1 = 2ⁱ·K with K odd and s(i) = 2ⁱ·K − 1 = Nᵢ. The term N₀ = K − 1 is even.

**No small factors (proved).** If q | K then Nᵢ ≡ −1 (mod q) for every i. With 15015 | K no term is divisible by 3, 5, 7, 11 or 13, and for i ≥ 1 no term is even.

**Why that helps (heuristic).** Among numbers near N with no prime factor up to y, the share of semiprimes is about e^γ·ln y·(ln ln N − ln ln y)/ln N. For N = 10¹⁶ this is 0.10 for all numbers (ln ln N/ln N), 0.33 for y = 13. The measured chance per step is higher (the first steps have smaller N):

| K near 6·10⁹ … 4·10¹⁰, one million values each | ≥ 1 | ≥ 2 | ≥ 4 | ≥ 6 | ≥ 8 | ≥ 10 semiprimes in a row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| arbitrary odd K | 243481 | 49758 | 2770 | 215 | 19 | 3 |
| K = 15015·m, m odd | 422160 | 175503 | 29138 | 4427 | 695 | 95 |

**Full run (computed).** [`code/p641_search.py`](code/p641_search.py) `13 1 100000000 15` and `13 100000001 200000000 15`: number of K with at least r semiprimes N₁ … N_r:

| r | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 | 16 | 17 | 18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| m < 10⁸ | 2741 | 955 | 358 | 140 | 51 | 18 | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| 10⁸ < m < 2·10⁸ | 2171 | 757 | 287 | 109 | 42 | 5 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

The chain of 18 is K = 15015 · 47135707 = 707742640605. Its N₀ = K − 1 is divisible by 4, so the chain starts at N₁ = 1415485281209.

**Not claimed.** The chain is not shown to be the smallest of 18 terms: only multiples of 15015 were searched. Each further term costs a factor of about 2.7 in the number of K.
