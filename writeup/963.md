# Puzzle 963: Covering the first odd primes with arithmetic progressions of primes

> **In short:** The page asked for the fewest arithmetic progressions of primes (at least 3 terms each) that together contain the first X odd primes; the best covers had 26 progressions for X = 100 and 235 for X = 1000, with no proof of minimality. The proof: (1) only maximal progressions are needed, which leaves 453 candidate sets for X = 100 and 33361 for X = 1000; (2) covering is then an exact set-cover problem; (3) an integer program solves it to optimality for X = 100, and a separate branch-and-bound with exactly checked LP bounds confirms that 25 progressions cannot work. This proves 26 minimal for X = 100 and gives a cover by 232 progressions for X = 1000 (at least 203 are needed).

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_963.htm)

**Status:** beaten (Q2: 232 progressions) · proof (Q1: 26 is the minimum)

## The question

The first 10 odd primes can be covered by three arithmetic progressions of primes: (3, 17, 31), (5, 11, 17, 23, 29), (7, 13, 19). What is the smallest number of progressions of primes, each with at least 3 terms, needed to cover the first X odd primes?

- **Q1.** X = 100.
- **Q2.** X = 1000.

## What the page had vs ours

| X | best on the page | ours |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | 26 (Kamenetsky) | **26 is the minimum** |
| 1000 | 235 (Gusev) | **232** (at least 203 needed) |

## How we found it

A cover is a choice of sets from a fixed, finite family, so the question is a set-cover problem; the work is in making the family small and complete. A progression can always be replaced by the longest progression of primes with the same difference that contains it, because that covers more. So only maximal progressions matter. Then an integer program over these sets finds the optimum (X = 100), or a good cover plus a lower bound (X = 1000).

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). The rules.** Every term is one of the first X odd primes (3 … 547 for X = 100, 3 … 7927 for X = 1000), as in all covers on the page. Progressions may overlap. For X = 100 we also checked the looser reading, where a progression may continue with primes beyond the X-th odd prime: in a cover this only adds pairs {a, b} with 2b − a a prime above the range.

**Step 2 (proved). Maximal progressions suffice.** If a progression with difference d covers a set T, the maximal progression of primes in the range with the same difference through the same terms covers a superset of T. So some optimal cover uses only maximal progressions, those with a − d and b + d not both prime in the range. With at least 3 terms there are 453 of them for X = 100 and 33361 for X = 1000.

**Step 3 (proved). Structure.** For three odd primes a, a + d, a + 2d: d is even, and if 3 ∤ d then one of them is divisible by 3, so it is 3 itself. So every progression that does not start at 3 has 6 | d. Likewise five terms with 5 ∤ d run through all residues mod 5, so a progression of 5 or more terms without 5 has 30 | d. In the X = 1000 cover, 203 of the 232 progressions have 4 or more terms, and the most common differences are 210, 360, 30, 330, 570, 780 and 840.

**Step 4 (exact search).** Choose 0/1 variables, one per maximal progression, with "every prime covered" as constraints, and minimise their number (HiGHS).

- X = 100: the optimum is 26; the linear relaxation gives 23.5. Allowing progressions to run past 547 adds 420 pair-sets and does not lower the optimum.
- X = 100, independent check: a branch-and-bound on the 873 sets (both readings) shows that no 25 of them cover all 100 primes. It first removes sets contained in other sets and primes whose covering sets include those of another prime, then branches on the prime with the fewest covering sets. Each node is cut off by an LP dual solution, rounded to fractions and scaled to be exactly feasible: any cover needs at least Σ y sets. 32073 nodes, about 10 minutes.
- X = 1000: within 20 minutes the integer program finds a cover by 232 progressions; its lower bound is 203 (linear relaxation 202.4). A second run of 25 minutes found nothing better.

The 232 progressions are in [data/p963_cover_1000.txt](data/p963_cover_1000.txt), and the 26 for X = 100 are in [data/p963_cover_100.txt](data/p963_cover_100.txt).

**What this does not settle.** The exact minimum for X = 1000, somewhere between 203 and 232.
