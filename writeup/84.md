# Puzzle 84: Non-primes adding up to non-primes

> **In short:** The page lists the first 10, 8 and 7 members of three greedy sets of non-primes whose subset sums are all non-prime (any parity, all odd, all even after the 1) and asks for more terms. (1) OEIS A052349, A128687 and A128688 carry 12, 11 and 12 terms. (2) All 3584, 2048 and 4096 subset sums of these sets are non-prime. (3) A segmented sieve recomputes every term up to 1231572090, 78060135 and 6847205430 as the least admissible one. (4) The page's existence construction, run greedily, gives a 20-digit ninth odd term where the true one is 2658795.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_084.htm)

**Status:** open · attempted (no new terms; the OEIS terms beyond the page checked, and recomputed minimal up to 6.85·10⁹)

## The question

TWA asks for "the least non-prime numbers such that the addition of all of them or any of its subsets produces only non-prime numbers": 1, 8, 24, 25, 86, 1260, 1890, 14136, 197400, 10467660. Carlos Rivera adds the all-odd set 1, 9, 15, 39, 105, 731, 2805, 59535 and the set with every member after 1 even, 1, 8, 24, 86, 90, 780, 5940. Question 1 asks to extend them; question 2 (finite or infinite) is settled on the page by Chris Nash's congruence construction.

## How we found it

The three lists were looked up in the OEIS by their terms. The check is a single pass: with the prefix fixed, the next term is the least m above the last term, of the right parity, with m + s non-prime for every subset sum s of the prefix. A segmented sieve gives the primality of a window, the first six subset sums are applied to the whole segment, and the few survivors are followed one by one ([`code/p84_verify.py`](code/p84_verify.py)).

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). The greedy rule.** Let S = {a(1) < … < a(n)} be the prefix and Σ(S) its set of subset sums (0 included). A number m can follow iff m + s is non-prime for every s in Σ(S): the subsets of S ∪ {m} are the subsets of S and those plus m. If some m < a(n) fitted the whole prefix, it would also fit the shorter prefix below it and the greedy rule would have taken it earlier; so searching m > a(n) loses nothing.

**Step 2 (proved). Parity.** In the odd set a subset sum has the parity of the subset size, so an odd m only has to be checked against the even sums (half of them). In the even set all sums of members after 1 are even, and an even m has to be checked against the odd sums, those that contain 1.

**Step 3 (computed). The OEIS terms are valid.** The 12 terms of A052349 have 3584 distinct subset sums (24 + 1 = 25 makes sums coincide), the 11 odd terms 2048 and the 12 even terms 4096; none is prime.

**Step 4 (computed). Minimality.** The pass of step 1 from a(1) = 1 returns exactly the listed terms: a(2..11) of A052349 (all m ≤ 1231572090), a(2..10) of A128687 (m ≤ 78060135) and a(2..11) of A128688 (m ≤ 6847205430). The page's 10, 8 and 7 terms are confirmed with them. Not recomputed: the minimality of 682616834970, 67554224745 and 5027286840810.

**Step 5 (computed). Nash's construction in action.** Fix m modulo 2, then for p = 3, 5, 7, … take the residue of m that makes p divide m + s for the most remaining sums s, until none is left; the Chinese remainder theorem gives m. After the page's 8 odd terms this needs 16 moduli and gives 12788951677681420065 (20 digits) against the true a(9) = 2658795; after the 7 even terms 10 moduli give 4063539480 against 52350. The construction proves that the sets are infinite but is far from the least term.
