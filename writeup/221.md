# Puzzle 221: The next terms of 2, 7, 13, 43, 103, …

> **In short:** The sequence is A153136: each term is the smallest prime above the previous one such that no sum of two or more terms is prime. The page gives a(9) = 7338823 (Wiesenbauer, Andersen). OEIS adds **a(10) = 42939980423** (Donovan Johnson, 2008). We verified that all ten terms obey the rule, and that a(2) … a(9) are the greedy minimum.
>
> A short heuristic explains why the terms jump so fast:
> - p is odd, so only the even subset sums S can make p + S prime;
> - with E such sums, a random p works with probability about (1 − 2/ln p)^E;
> - E roughly doubles with every term (255 for a(10), 511 for a(11)).
>
> The heuristic places a(10) near 7.7·10¹⁰ (real 4.3·10¹⁰) and **a(11) near 10¹⁵**. That is why "the next five terms" never came.

## The question

Given 2, 7, 13, 43, 103, 1627, 25349, 315743, …, the page asks:

1. Find the definition of the sequence.
2. Find the next five terms.
3. Argue whether it is finite or infinite.

On the page, Wiesenbauer and Andersen found the rule and a(9) = 7338823. They also proved Q3 (the sequence is infinite, by arbitrarily long runs of composites).

## The math

For a new prime p > 2 and a nonempty subset of earlier terms with sum S, the number p + S is even whenever S is odd, so it is composite. Only the E subsets with an even sum matter. The terms are 2 and odd primes, so with k earlier terms E = 2^(k−1) − 1. Each even-S value p + S is prime with probability about 2/ln p, which gives

P(p works) ≈ (1 − 2/ln p)^E,

and the first solution lies where Σ_p 1/ln p · (1 − 2/ln p)^E reaches 1.

- For a(10) (E = 255) this gives 7.7·10¹⁰, against the real 4.29·10¹⁰.
- For a(11) (E = 511) it gives about 1.1·10¹⁵. A direct search would test some 3·10¹³ primes, each against hundreds of sums.
- A better route would pick p mod 3, 5, 7, … to cover as many of the 511 sums as possible, as in the covering constructions of problems 36 and 69. That is not tried here.

The heuristic ignores local factors, so treat 10¹⁵ as an order of magnitude.

## Validation

[`writeup/code/p221_verify.py`](code/p221_verify.py) checks the rule for a(1) … a(10) by testing every subset sum, and recomputes a(2) … a(9) greedily. Minimality of a(10) is from OEIS and is not rechecked: it would mean testing all primes up to 4.3·10¹⁰. The script then prints both heuristic estimates.
