# Puzzle 655: pq + rs, perfect square

> **In short:** For consecutive primes p < q < r < s the least p with pq + rs a square is 203233; the page asks for three more cases, and searches to 3.1·10¹⁰, by sieve to 4000·2³² and by a Pell-type equation to 40 digits found none. (1) The same equation, walked to 100 digits with wider gaps and a residue sieve, gives one more: p = 1150284232069964804556646431054740232316097446258616188423 with gaps 484, 610, 660. (2) The expected number grows like 0.354·ln ln p, so each further case costs a factor 17 in digits.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_655.htm)

**Status:** open · one new case of the three asked (58 digits, proved primes); the method, the proof and the validation are in [498](498.md)

## The question

"Let p < q < r < s be consecutive primes. The least prime p for which pq + rs is a perfect square is 203233. Q1: find three more cases." Ryan Bailey and Emmanuel Vantieghem found none up to 21940132061 and 31·10⁹; Jan van Delden found none with p < 4000·2³², and none up to about 40 digits for gaps s − p ≤ 500 by solving x² − 2y² = −d, also for ps + qr and pr + qs.

## How we found it

See [498](498.md): with half-gaps a < b < d and t = a + b + d the condition is U² − 8z² = t² − 8bd for U = 2p + t; the orbits of all fundamental solutions were walked to 100 digits ([`code/p655_pell.py`](code/p655_pell.py)). The new case has s − p = 660 = 5.02 ln p, beyond the gap bound 500 of the earlier search and beyond its 40 digits.

## The math

Steps 1 to 6 of [498](498.md) apply with C = bd. For the new case t = 242 + 305 + 330 = 877 and k = 877² − 8·305·330 = −36071. The model of step 5 expects 0.354·ln(ln 10¹⁰⁰/ln(4000·2³²)) = 0.71 cases between the sieve bound and 10¹⁰⁰, and 0.354·ln 3 = 0.39 more up to 10³⁰⁰; two more cases (to make three) are expected only when 0.354·ln(D/100) = 2, that is near D = 100·e^(2/0.354) ≈ 28600 digits, so Q1 as posed is out of reach for this method.
