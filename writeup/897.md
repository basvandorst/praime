# Puzzle 897, Q2: 1638 semiprimes whose reciprocals sum to 2

> **In short:** The best set of distinct semiprimes whose reciprocals sum to 2 had 2250 terms. The construction: (1) the sum is an integer exactly when every prime is balanced mod p; (2) start from the smallest semiprimes and repair the primes from the largest down; (3) close the primes 2…13 with an exact dynamic programme whose cost also counts the change in sum, so that it lands on 2 and not on 1. This gives 1638 terms; at least 1234 are needed.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_897.htm)

**Status:** beaten (Q2, the integer 2) · exact (rational arithmetic)

## The question

A. W. Johnson (1978) wrote 1 as a sum of 48 reciprocals of semiprimes.

**Q2.** Send your best solutions for any integer > 1 using only semiprime denominators.

## How we found it

This is the method of [problem 76](prob-76.md), with the prime 2 allowed. The page's 2250-term set and its notes already use the balance criterion and repair from the largest prime down. Our gain comes from the closing step. A dynamic programme over all 30030 residue states of {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13} picks the cheapest set of moves that balances them. Its cost is Δ(terms) − X₀·Δ(sum), not only Δ(terms). Without the second part it prefers to drop valuable terms like 1/6 and 1/10 and land on the integer 1.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved; also on this page). Squarefree and balanced.** A square p² can never be a denominator: the terms containing p would then add up to (1 + p·A)/p² with p ∤ 1 + p·A. For squarefree semiprimes pq, the terms containing p sum to (1/p)·Σ 1/q, so the sum has no p in its denominator iff Σ q⁻¹ ≡ 0 (mod p) over the partners q of p. If every prime is balanced the sum is an integer.

**Step 2 (proved). Lower bound.** The reciprocals of the 1234 smallest squarefree semiprimes (6, 10, 14, 15, …, 4571) are the first to reach 2.

**Step 3 (construction).**

1. *Bulk:* all squarefree semiprimes pq ≤ X₀ = 7000.
2. *Repair:* every prime v > 13, from the largest down, by toggling edges to smaller primes. For small v, all subsets of these edges are tried; an "upward gadget" is also allowed: an edge to a larger prime u plus edges u·f, f ≤ 13, that keep u balanced. Options are scored by Δ(terms) − 0.6·X₀·Δ(sum).
3. *Close* {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13}: moves are the edges inside the set and gadgets (v; Q ⊆ {2, …, 13}) with Σ ±q⁻¹ ≡ 0 (mod v). The cost is as in the How we found it section.

With seed 14 the result has 1638 terms on 228 primes and an exact sum of 2. Over 16 seeds each at X₀ = 7000 and 7150 (λ = 0.5, 0.6) the best results were 1638, 1642, 1643 and 1646. Below X₀ ≈ 7000 the repaired sum stays under 2, and the closing step can only reach 1.

**What this does not settle.** The minimum for 2 lies between 1234 and 1638. For 3 we have no result yet.
