# Puzzle 580: An equivalence for twin primes, proved

> **In short:** It was claimed that P(n) − P(n−k) − (n−k)·P(k) = 0 exactly when P(n) and P(k) are twin primes; the page has only a sketch. The proof: (1) with m = n − k, the case m = 1 is the twin condition itself; (2) for m ≥ 2 the expression is negative: for large primes Dusart's short-interval bound makes P(m+k) − P(m) smaller than m·P(k); (3) the remaining small cases, m, k ≤ 33608, are checked one by one. This proves the equivalence for all k < n.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_580.htm)

**Status:** answered · proof (explicit bounds plus a finite check)

## The question

Let P(i) be the i-th prime and k < n positive integers.

**Q.** Prove that P(n) − P(n−k) − (n−k)·P(k) = 0 if and only if P(n) and P(k) are a twin prime pair, or find a counterexample.

## What the page had vs ours

| | On the page | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| the claim | a sketch: n − k must be even, and "for larger n, (n−k)·P(k) is already greater than P(n)"; "similar arguments" for n − k = 4, 6, … | a complete proof |

The sketch has two gaps. Its parity step ignores k = 1, where P(1) = 2 is even. And "(n−k)·P(k) > P(n)" is false in general: for (n, k) = (4, 2), 2·P(2) = 6 < P(4) = 7. The statement survives because P(n−k) is also subtracted.

## How we found it

Put m = n − k and E(m, k) = P(m+k) − P(m) − m·P(k). The claim says E = 0 only for m = 1 with twin primes. For m ≥ 2, E(m, k) < 0 means that the m + k-th prime lies below P(m) + m·P(k). That interval is long: it contains at least k gaps' worth of room. So explicit gap bounds settle all large cases, and a direct check covers the small ones. This is the same pattern as [conjecture 76](conj-76.md) (explicit Rosser–Schoenfeld and Dusart bounds above a threshold, a finite check below it).

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). m = 1.** E(1, k) = P(k+1) − 2 − P(k) is 0 iff P(k+1) − P(k) = 2, i.e. P(k), P(k+1) are twin primes. Conversely, if P(n) − P(k) = 2 then n = k + 1, because two steps already give P(k+2) − P(k) ≥ 3 (and ≥ 4 for k ≥ 2). So for m = 1 the statement is exactly the twin condition.

**Step 2 (theorems used).**
- Dusart (2010): for x ≥ 396738 there is a prime in [x, x + x/(25 ln²x)]. Applied at x = p + 1, the gap after a prime p ≥ 396738 is at most G(p) = 1 + (p+1)/(25·ln²(p+1)), and G is increasing.
- Rosser–Schoenfeld: P(n) ≤ U(n) = n(ln n + ln ln n) for n ≥ 6.
- Dusart: P(n) ≥ L(n) = n(ln n + ln ln n − 1) for n ≥ 3500.

All in FACTS 2012 and 2033.

**Step 3 (proved). m ≥ 2, large cases.** Let M₀ = π(396737) = 33608.

- *Case A: k ≤ m and m > M₀.* The primes P(m), …, P(m+k−1) are at least 396738 and at most P(2m), so each of the k gaps is at most G(P(2m)) ≤ 1 + (U(2m)+1)/(25·ln²(2m)). For m > M₀ this is below 0.0089·m < m, and the bound per m decreases with m. Hence P(m+k) − P(m) < k·m ≤ m·(P(k) − 1) < m·P(k).
- *Case B: k > m ≥ 2 and k > M₀.* P(m+k) − P(m) < (P(k+m) − P(k)) + P(k) ≤ m·G(P(2k)) + P(k). Moreover P(2k) ≤ U(2k) ≤ 2.3·L(k) ≤ 2.3·P(k) (the ratio U(2k)/L(k) is at most 2.299 for k > M₀ and decreases). So G(P(2k)) ≤ 1 + (2.3·P(k) + 1)/(25·ln²396738) < P(k)/2. Then m·G < m·P(k)/2 ≤ (m−1)·P(k), and P(m+k) − P(m) < m·P(k).

**Step 4 (computed). m ≥ 2, small cases.** Everything not covered by step 3 has m ≤ M₀ and k ≤ M₀. A direct computation over all 2 ≤ m ≤ 33608 and 1 ≤ k ≤ 33608 gives E(m, k) < 0 every time; the largest value is −2.

So E(m, k) < 0 for every m ≥ 2. The expression vanishes only for m = 1 with P(k), P(k+1) twin primes, which proves the equivalence.
