# Puzzle 1277: Few coprime neighbours in a 100 × 100 grid

> **In short:** Arrange 1 … 10000 in a 100 × 100 grid with as few coprime edge-neighbours as possible; the best grid had 2075 coprime pairs. The construction: (1) 1 and the 560 primes above 5000 are coprime to every other entry, so they go on the border, where they have the fewest neighbours; (2) primes with only a few multiples get exactly those multiples as neighbours (p next to 2p on the second ring, p inside 2p, 4p, 6p, 8p in the interior, and so on); (3) the remaining cells are filled by local swaps, with the structure fixed. This gives a grid with 1452 coprime pairs.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1277.htm)

**Status:** beaten (Q3: 1452 coprime pairs for 100 × 100; record 2075)

## The question

Arrange the numbers 1 through n² in an n × n grid so that as few pairs of edge-neighbours (up, down, left, right) as possible are coprime.

- **Q1, Q2.** Verify the small optimal grids and extend the sequence.
- **Q3.** Send your best grid for n = 100.

## How we found it

Most numbers can be placed next to numbers that share a factor with them: evens next to evens, odd multiples of 3 next to multiples of 6, and so on. The cost comes from numbers with few or no multiples in range. So we place those first, by hand-designed rules, and only then let a local search arrange the bulk. A local search alone, from the same border layout, stops near 2100: it cannot assemble the five-cell groups that a prime p needs around it.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). Lonely numbers.** A prime p > 5000 has no other multiple up to 10000, so it is coprime to every other entry, and so is 1. There are 1 + π(10000) − π(5000) = 561 such numbers, and every grid edge touching one of them is coprime. For a set B of cells, the number of edges touching B is 2|B| + (∂B − sB)/2, where ∂B is the number of edges leaving B and sB the number of cell sides of B on the border of the grid. So these numbers belong on the border, where sB is large: the outer ring holds 396, and the other 165 continue on the second ring. In the start layout 1117 edges touch them (1126 in the final grid); a compact block in the middle of the grid would touch about 1170.

**Step 2 (proved). Primes with few multiples.** A prime p in (10000/(j+1), 10000/j] has exactly j − 1 other multiples up to 10000 (2p, …, jp). With 4 neighbours, at least 4 − (j − 1) of its edges are coprime, unless some neighbours are lonely cells whose edges are paid for already. That gives the placement:

- p in (3333, 5000] (199 primes, only 2p) and 24 primes in (2500, 3333]: on the second ring, with 2p directly inside. The edge to the outer ring is already counted, and the edge to 2p is not coprime. What remains are the edges between neighbouring primes along the ring, about one per prime.
- the other primes in (2500, 3333] (multiples 2p, 3p), all but three: on the third ring under the lonely cells, in groups p, 3p, 3q, q (3p and 3q share 3), with 2p inside p and a multiple of 6 inside 3p. That costs one edge per group of two primes.
- p in (2000, 2500] (2p, 3p, 4p): in the interior with neighbours 2p, 4p, 3p; one coprime edge each. The other neighbours of 3p are multiples of 6.
- p in (1250, 2000]: neighbours 2p, 4p, 3p and 6p or 5p; no coprime edge.
- p in [101, 1250): a plus shape, p surrounded by 2p, 4p, 6p, 8p; no coprime edge.

**Step 3 (computed). The bulk.** The other cells (evens, odd composites, primes below 100) are filled by simulated annealing with swaps, with the structure of steps 1–2 fixed. A final pass at low temperature lets every cell move. This gives 1452.

**Where the 1452 coprime pairs are:**

| the two numbers | coprime pairs |
|---|---|
| at least one lonely number (1 or a prime > 5000) | 1126 |
| two primes in (2500, 5000], neighbours on the second and third rings | 256 |
| a prime in (2000, 2500] and a non-lonely number | 65 |
| other | 5 |
| **total** | **1452** |

**What this does not settle.** We claim no optimality. Step 1 alone forces at least 2·561 − 200 = 922 coprime pairs in any grid (∂B ≥ 0 and sB ≤ 400), and 1117 in our layout of the lonely numbers.

## Validation

[code/p1277_verify.py](code/p1277_verify.py) reads [data/p1277_grid100.txt](data/p1277_grid100.txt). It checks that every number 1 … 10000 appears once, counts the coprime neighbour pairs (1452), and lists them by kind. [code/p1277_build.py](code/p1277_build.py) builds the start grid with steps 1–2 fixed, and [code/p1277_grid.c](code/p1277_grid.c) does the annealing. Run after `pip install sympy`:

```
python3 p1277_verify.py                        # a few seconds
python3 p1277_build.py start.txt mask.txt
gcc -O2 -o p1277_grid p1277_grid.c -lm
./p1277_grid 100 300000000 5 a.txt 0.4 start.txt mask.txt     # structure fixed: 1483
./p1277_grid 100 300000000 8 b.txt 0.08 a.txt zeros.txt       # all cells free (zeros.txt: a 100 x 100 mask of 0s): 1455
./p1277_grid 100 400000000 9 c.txt 0.06 b.txt zeros.txt       # 1452 (each run a few minutes)
```
