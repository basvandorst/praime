# Puzzle 977, Q1: a trio of 566-digit primes with 4q, 3r, 2s consecutive

> **In short:** The largest known primes q, r, s with 4q, 3r, 2s consecutive had 552 digits. The construction: (1) 4q, 3r, 2s consecutive means (q, r, s) = (3m − 1, 4m − 1, 6m − 1); (2) with m = k·2¹⁸⁵⁰ all three are of the form h·2ⁿ − 1, which a Lucas–Lehmer–Riesel test proves prime in milliseconds; (3) a sieve over k leaves 242063 candidates for probable-prime tests. This gives two trios with q of 566 digits, all six numbers proven prime.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_977.htm)

**Status:** beaten (Q1) · proven primes (LLR)

## The question

113, 151, 227 are primes with 4·113 = 452, 3·151 = 453 and 2·227 = 454 consecutive.

**Q1.** Find your largest trio of primes {q, r, s} like this.

## Record vs ours

| | q | Digits | Proof |

| Proven record (G. Gusev, 2019) | 156925447·2¹⁰⁰⁰ + 1 | 310 | proven |
| **Ours** | **3·152439497·2¹⁸⁵⁰ − 1** | **566** | proven (LLR) |

```
q = 3·152439497·2^1850 − 1     (566 digits)
r =   152439497·2^1852 − 1     (566 digits)
s = 3·152439497·2^1851 − 1     (566 digits)
```

A second trio comes from k = 139751997 (also 566 digits).

## How we found it

We wrote the condition as one parameter m and chose m so that all three numbers have a form with a fast primality proof. This is the [puzzle 435](435.md) idea of building everything around one free parameter, here combined with Riesel numbers.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). The shape.** If 4q = N − 1, 3r = N and 2s = N + 1, then N ≡ 1 (mod 4), N ≡ 0 (mod 3) and N ≡ 1 (mod 2) force N = 12m − 3. So q = 3m − 1, r = 4m − 1 and s = 6m − 1 = 2q + 1, which makes q and s a Sophie Germain pair.

**Step 2 (proved). Riesel forms.** With m = k·2ⁿ and k odd: q = 3k·2ⁿ − 1, r = k·2ⁿ⁺² − 1 and s = 3k·2ⁿ⁺¹ − 1, each h·2^N − 1 with h odd and h < 2^N. For such numbers the Lucas–Lehmer–Riesel test with Rödseth's starting value is a proof: N is prime iff u_(N−2) ≡ 0, where u₀ = V_h(P, 1) and u_(i+1) = u_i² − 2. Here P is the first integer ≥ 3 with Jacobi symbols ((P−2)/N) = 1 and ((P+2)/N) = −1.

**Step 3 (computed). Sieve and test.** For n = 1850 and odd k ≤ 10⁹, every prime p ≤ 10⁷ removes the three classes of k for which p divides q, r or s. That leaves 242063 candidates, about 1 in 2000. Fermat tests (base 3) on r, then q, then s found two trios: k = 139751997 after 34111 tests and k = 152439497 after 37122. A model check agrees: about 2.6% of the survivors pass each single test (33, 39 and 40 of 1500), and the expected number of trios over all survivors is about 3. A first run at n = 1900 (242293 candidates) found none, an outcome with about 3% probability.

## Validation

[code/p977_verify.py](code/p977_verify.py) checks the LLR test against sympy on all h·2ⁿ − 1 with odd h < 400 and n < 40. It then proves all six primes of the two trios and checks that 4q, 3r, 2s are consecutive; with `--pari` it also proves them by APRCL. [code/p977_sieve.c](code/p977_sieve.c) is the sieve. Run after `pip install gmpy2 sympy` (under a second):

```
python3 p977_verify.py
gcc -O2 -o p977_sieve p977_sieve.c && ./p977_sieve 1850 1000000000 10000000 > survivors.txt
```
