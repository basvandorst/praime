# Puzzle 41 (F1 and I): Palindromes from anagrams of 1..9

> **In short:**
> - **F1.** For a 9-digit anagram A of 1..9, the middle digit cancels in A − Rev(A), and only the four differences d_i = a_(8−i) − a_i are left: |A − Rev(A)| = 99·|1010101·d₀ + 101010·d₁ + 10100·d₂ + 1000·d₃|. Of all 16⁴ nonzero patterns, only ±(1, −1, 1, −1) and ±(5, 5, −5, −5) give a palindrome. They give 90900909 and 544505445, the only two values the page found.
> - **I.** A/9 always has 8 digits, because 9 digits would force the digit 9 twice. An even-length palindrome is divisible by 11, so every A/9 palindrome is composite.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_041.htm)

**Status:** answered · F1 and I proved

## The question

**F.** |A9D − Reverse(A9D)| is a palindrome for many anagrams A9D of 1..9, but only with two values, 544505445 and 90900909. F1 asks why. (The page writes "+" in its examples; the numbers only work as differences.)

**I.** The 34 palindromes A9D/9 are all composite. Is there a reason?

The page has no answers to F1 or I.

## F1

Write A = a₈a₇…a₀. The middle digit a₄ cancels, so

```
A − Rev(A) = Σ_(i=0..3) (a_(8−i) − a_i)·(10^(8−i) − 10^i)
           = 99·(1010101·d₀ + 101010·d₁ + 10100·d₂ + 1000·d₃),   d_i = a_(8−i) − a_i.
```

The digits are distinct, so every d_i is a nonzero integer in [−8, 8]. That leaves 16⁴ = 65536 patterns. Checking them all, even without requiring distinct digits, gives a palindrome for exactly four patterns:

| (d₀, d₁, d₂, d₃) | value |
|---|---|
| ±(1, −1, 1, −1) | 99·918191 = 90900909 |
| ±(5, 5, −5, −5) | 99·5·1100011 = 544505445 |

Both patterns can be realised with distinct digits. A pass over all 9! anagrams finds the two values for 240 and 48 anagrams.

## I

Every anagram of 1..9 has digit sum 45, so 9 | A. Suppose P = A/9 had 9 digits. Then A ≥ 9·10⁸, so A starts with 9. Also 10⁸ ≤ P ≤ 987654321/9 < 1.1·10⁸, so the palindrome P starts, and therefore ends, with 1. Then A = 9P ends in 9. That puts the digit 9 in A twice, which is impossible.

So P has 8 digits. An even-length palindrome is divisible by 11, because its alternating digit sum is 0. So all 34 palindromes are composite.
