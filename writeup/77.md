# Puzzle 77: A prime pine with 10 rows

> **In short:** In a "prime pine" every number is the average of the two below it, and all numbers are different primes. Pines with 9 rows were known since 2012; nobody had found one with 10. We built one by making every number leave remainder 1 when divided by 3, 5, 7 and 11, so those small primes can never divide any of the 55 entries. All of them even end in 1.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_077.htm)

**Status:** first answer (R = 10) · proven primes

## The question

A pine of R rows is a triangle of numbers: 1 at the top, 2 below it, and so on, R in the bottom row. Each number X touches two numbers Y and Z in the row below, and must be their average, X = (Y + Z)/2. All numbers in the pine must be different primes. Question 1 asks for the least such pine for R > 8. Question 3 asks for large "one-colour" pines where every prime ends in the same digit.

## What the page had vs ours

| | Rows | Note |
|---|---|---|
| On the page (J. Tramu; J. van Delden, 2015) | 9 | van Delden: "Couldn't find the 10 solution yet" (2012) |
| On the page, Q3 (J. van Delden) | 9 | one-colour pine, every prime ending in 1 |
| **Ours** | **10** | 55 different primes, every one ending in 1 (so also a one-colour pine for Q3) |

We do not claim our pine is the least one with 10 rows, only that it is the first.

## How we found it

**Everything is decided by the bottom row.** Each number is the average of two below it, so the number h rows above the bottom is a weighted average of h + 1 bottom numbers, with binomial weights divided by 2^h. For the top to be a whole number, all ten bottom numbers must leave the same remainder modulo 2⁹ = 512.

**The obstacle.** Earlier searches died at the last stage. Each new bottom number creates a whole new diagonal of entries at once, and with 10 entries in one go, a small prime like 3, 5, 7 or 11 is very likely to divide one of them.

**The fix.** We made every bottom number of the form 1 + 1155·512·d, where 1155 = 3·5·7·11. Then every entry in the whole pine, not just the bottom, leaves remainder 1 when divided by 3, 5, 7 and 11. Those primes can never divide an entry, which removes most of the failures. We then chose d₁, d₂, … one at a time, keeping many partial pines alive at each stage, until a full diagonal of primes appeared for the tenth number.

## The math

Write the bottom row as b_i = 1 + M·2⁹·d_i with M = 1155. Averaging is linear, and the average of numbers that are all ≡ 1 stays ≡ 1, so the entry h rows up is

```
T(h, j) = 2^(−h) · Σ_i C(h, i) · b_(j+i)  =  1 + M · 2^(9−h) · D(h, j),     D(h, j) = Σ_i C(h, i) · d_(j+i)
```

So every entry is 1 plus a multiple of M = 3·5·7·11, and none of these four primes divides it. At the top (h = 9) the entry is 1 + M·D, which is odd when D is even. Every entry is also odd and ≡ 1 (mod 5), so it is ≡ 1 (mod 10) and ends in the digit 1: the pine is one-colour for free.

## Validation

This script builds the bottom row from the ten values d, averages upwards to the top, and checks the rules: all 55 entries are different primes, each is the average of the two below, and each ends in 1.

```python
from sympy import isprime

M = 3 * 5 * 7 * 11                       # every entry will be 1 mod these primes
d = [6, 21, 92, 595, 623, 5813, 13868, 31590, 80752, 1368095]

bottom = [1 + M * 2**9 * x for x in d]   # all equal mod 2^9, so averages stay whole
rows = [bottom]
while len(rows[-1]) > 1:
    below = rows[-1]
    pairs = list(zip(below, below[1:]))
    assert all((y + z) % 2 == 0 for y, z in pairs)
    rows.append([(y + z) // 2 for y, z in pairs])   # each entry is the average of the two below
rows.reverse()                           # top row first

entries = [x for row in rows for x in row]
assert len(rows) == 10 and len(entries) == 55
assert all(isprime(x) for x in entries)              # all prime (exact below 2^64)
assert len(set(entries)) == 55                       # all different
assert all(x % M == 1 for x in entries)              # never divisible by 3, 5, 7 or 11
assert all(x % 10 == 1 for x in entries)             # one-colour: every prime ends in 1
assert rows[0] == [6076960891] and sum(entries) == 2010610336405

for row in rows:
    print(" ".join(map(str, row)))
```
