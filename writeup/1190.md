# Puzzle 1190: The largest 10-digit Friedman prime

> **In short:** The record 10-digit Friedman prime was 9999999001, not the largest 10-digit prime. The construction: (1) the largest is 9999999967 = 10¹⁰ − 33; (2) seven 9s make 10¹⁰; (3) 6, 7 and a 9 make −33. This gives 9999999967 = ((9·9+9)/9)^(9+9/9) − 6·7 + 9, which no 10-digit Friedman prime can beat.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1190.htm)

**Status:** beaten (k = 10, closed) · proven prime

## The question

A **Friedman number** can be written in a non-trivial way using its own digits, each exactly once, with + − × / ^, parentheses and gluing digits together. Example from the page: 995347 = 4·(7 + (9 + 3)⁵) − 9. The puzzle asks for the largest prime Friedman numbers with k = 7, 8, 9 and 10 digits.

## Current record vs ours

| | Prime | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| Current record (A. Stinchcombe, 2024) | 9999999001 | 10^(9+9/9) − 999 + 0·9 |
| **Ours** | **9999999967** | ((9·9+9)/9)^(9+9/9) − 6·7 + 9 |

## How we found it

Stinchcombe already used the right idea for 7, 8 and 9 digits: take the **largest** prime of that length and look for a way to write it with its own digits. Numbers like 9999991 are full of 9s, and 9s easily make powers of 10 (for example (9 + 9/9) = 10). For 10 digits he settled for 9999999001, "not the first prime under 10¹⁰ but close".

The largest 10-digit prime is 9999999967 = 10¹⁰ − 33. Its digits are eight 9s, one 6 and one 7. Eight 9s are enough to build 10¹⁰, and 6 and 7 with one more 9 give −42 + 9 = −33. So the very largest candidate works, and the 10-digit case is closed.

## The math

```
(9·9 + 9) / 9 = 90 / 9 = 10           uses four 9s
 9 + 9/9      = 10                     uses three 9s
10^10 − 6·7 + 9 = 10^10 − 42 + 9 = 10^10 − 33 = 9999999967      uses 6, 7 and one more 9
```

That is eight 9s, one 6 and one 7: exactly the digits of 9999999967. Every number from 9999999968 to 9999999999 is composite, so no larger 10-digit prime exists.

## Validation

This script evaluates the expression, checks that it uses exactly the digits of the prime, and confirms that 9999999967 is the largest 10-digit prime.

```python
from collections import Counter
from sympy import isprime

expr = "((9*9+9)/9)**(9+9/9)-6*7+9"
value = eval(expr)
assert value == 9999999967                                   # the expression gives the prime

# the digits used in the expression are exactly the digits of the number
used = Counter(c for c in expr if c.isdigit())
assert used == Counter("9999999967")

assert isprime(9999999967)                                   # prime (exact below 2^64)
assert not any(isprime(n) for n in range(9999999968, 10**10))   # nothing larger with 10 digits
assert 9999999967 > 9999999001                               # beats the listed record

print("9999999967 = ((9*9+9)/9)^(9+9/9) - 6*7 + 9, the largest 10-digit prime")
```
