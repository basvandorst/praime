# Puzzle 319: Approximations to π(n)

> **In short:** Four closed forms for π(N) were measured, but how they were derived was unknown. The analysis: (1) li(N) = (N/A)(1 + 1!/A + 2!/A² + …), A = ln N; (2) each formula expands as 1, 1, 2, then a different 4th coefficient; (3) so all match li(N) for three terms. This explains them: (c) is best near 10¹⁴ by a cancellation, and li(N) wins beyond.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_319.htm)

**Status:** answered (Q a and b; the derivation Terr asked about) · series expansions exact; accuracy measured against the exact π(10^k)

## The question

Ernst Morawetz sent four approximations to π(N), with A = ln N and B = 1/A + 2/A² + 3/A³ + 4/A⁴ − 5/A⁵:

- a) N(0.5 − (0.25 − 1/A)^0.5)
- b) (N/A)(A − 1)/(A − 2)
- c) (N/A)(A − 1 − ln 10/A)/(A − 2 − ln 10/A)
- d) "(N/A)·(1 + 1/A − 2 − B)/(A + 24.6/A⁵)", as printed

Q a) asks how accurate they are, and Q b) whether they are interesting. David Terr found them all better than 10⁻⁴ at N = 10¹⁴, with (c) best at 1.06·10⁻⁷, but wrote: "I don't know how they were derived." Luis Rodríguez proposed N/(ln N − 1 − 1.5721/(ln N)^1.1).

## The math

li(N)/(N/A) has the asymptotic expansion 1 + 1/A + 2/A² + 6/A³ + 24/A⁴ + … (coefficients k!). Expanding each formula divided by N/A in powers of 1/A gives:

| formula | coefficients | agrees with li for |
|---|---|---|
| (a) A(½ − √(¼ − 1/A)) | 1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 42 (Catalan numbers) | 3 terms |
| (b) (A − 1)/(A − 2) | 1, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 | 3 terms |
| (c) (A − 1 − ln 10/A)/(A − 2 − ln 10/A) | 1, 1, 2, 4 + ln 10 = 6.30, … | 3 terms (4th off by 5 %) |
| (d) 1 + 1/(A − 2 − B + 24.6/A⁵), our reading | 1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 40, … | 3 terms |

The printed (d) cannot be right: as written it starts with −1. Read as (b) with A − 2 replaced by A − 2 − B + 24.6/A⁵, it reproduces Terr's "better than 10⁻⁴ at 10¹⁴" and behaves exactly like (a).

So the formulas are three-term approximants with different "tails". A truncated asymptotic series leaves a relative error of order c/A³, where c is the error in the 4th coefficient: 1 for (a), (b) and (d), and 0.30 for (c). At N = 10¹⁴, 1/A³ ≈ 3·10⁻⁵, which matches the errors of a few times 10⁻⁵ for (a), (b) and (d). For (c), the deviation in the 4th coefficient and the higher-order terms cancel near 10¹⁴.

**Relative error (formula − π(N))/π(N)**, with π(10^k) exact (OEIS A006880):

| N | (a) | (b) | (c) | li(N) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁸ | −1.6·10⁻⁴ | −3.8·10⁻⁴ | 6.3·10⁻⁵ | 1.3·10⁻⁴ |
| 10¹² | −6.8·10⁻⁵ | −1.3·10⁻⁴ | −2.4·10⁻⁶ | 1.0·10⁻⁶ |
| 10¹⁴ | −4.1·10⁻⁵ | −7.6·10⁻⁵ | 1.06·10⁻⁷ | 9.8·10⁻⁸ |
| 10¹⁸ | −1.8·10⁻⁵ | −3.4·10⁻⁵ | 1.2·10⁻⁶ | 8.9·10⁻¹⁰ |
| 10²² | −9.3·10⁻⁶ | −1.8·10⁻⁵ | 1.0·10⁻⁶ | 9.6·10⁻¹² |

(d) agrees with (a) to two digits throughout.

**Are they interesting (Q b)?** As compact closed forms, yes: (a) is a pretty way to encode the Catalan numbers, and (c) is well tuned for the range 10¹²–10¹⁶. As approximations, li(N) beats all of them beyond 10¹⁴, and its relative error keeps shrinking, like √N·ln N/N under the Riemann hypothesis. Any formula built from finitely many terms of the asymptotic series must eventually stall at a relative error of order 1/A^k.
