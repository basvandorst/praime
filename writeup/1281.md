# Puzzle 1281: Largest primes whose digit blocks are pairwise coprime

> **In short:** Find the largest prime in which every block of k adjacent digits is a k-digit number coprime to all the other blocks (k = 2 and 3; and the same with no block prime). The page had 8997941735371319 (k = 2), a 44-digit prime (k = 3), 94951 and 97377998951 (no prime blocks), none proved largest. The proof: (1) blocks are pairwise coprime exactly when their sets of prime factors are disjoint, so a string is valid iff no prime is used twice; (2) a composite k-digit block has a prime factor below 10^(k/2), so with no prime blocks there are at most 4 blocks (k = 2) and 11 blocks (k = 3); (3) an exhaustive search over digit strings, keeping the largest prime of each length, finds the maximum. This proves the page's primes largest for k = 2 and for both no-prime cases. Case 2 (44 digits) is not settled: neither a SAT solver nor a constraint solver decides in 15 minutes whether 45 digits are possible.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1281.htm)

**Status:** answered (cases 1, 3, 4: the primes on the page are the largest possible; case 2 open) · proof (exhaustive search)

## The question

1. The largest prime in which every block of two adjacent digits is a two-digit number coprime to every other such block.
2. The same with blocks of three digits.
3. Cases 1 and 2 again when no block may be prime.

## What the page had vs ours

| case | best on the page | ours |
|---|---|---|
| 1: 2-digit blocks | 8997941735371319 (16 digits) | **the largest possible**: no valid string has 17 digits, and this is the largest 16-digit prime among the valid strings |
| 2: 3-digit blocks | 88779773974337937335997199113713191933173139 (44 digits) | not settled (see step 4) |
| 3: 2-digit blocks, none prime | 94951 | **the largest possible** (at most 4 blocks, so at most 5 digits) |
| 4: 3-digit blocks, none prime | 97377998951 | **the largest possible** (at most 11 blocks, so at most 13 digits; the longest valid strings have 11) |

## How we found it

Digits are 1 to 9: a block that starts with 0 is not a k-digit number, and every digit except the last starts a block. Two numbers are coprime exactly when they share no prime factor, so a set of blocks is pairwise coprime iff every prime divides at most one block. That makes the search a walk over digit strings in which each new block may only use primes not used before. The state is small (the last k − 1 digits and the set of used primes), and the walk ends when no block fits. For each length the search keeps the largest prime string; primality is tested only when a string beats the current best of its length.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). Pairwise coprime = disjoint prime sets.** gcd(a, b) = 1 iff no prime divides both. So blocks b₁, …, b_m are pairwise coprime iff the sets of prime factors are pairwise disjoint, i.e. no prime divides two of them.

**Step 2 (proved). Length bounds.** Every block has at least one prime factor, and all these primes are distinct, so the number of blocks is at most the number of primes that can divide a block. For composite blocks more is true: a composite number below 10^k has a prime factor below 10^(k/2). For k = 2 that prime is 2, 3, 5 or 7, so a string of composite 2-digit blocks has at most 4 blocks, 5 digits. For k = 3 the prime is one of the 11 primes below 32, so at most 11 blocks, 13 digits.

**Step 3 (computed). The search** ([code/p1281_search.py](code/p1281_search.py)).

| case | blocks | primes in play | strings visited | longest string | largest prime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 81 | 25 | 8221347 | 16 digits | 8997941735371319 |
| 3 | 60 | 15 | 437 | 5 digits | 94951 |
| 4 | 601 | 92 | 28511 | 11 digits | 97377998951 |

In case 1 the longest valid strings have 16 digits; so the page's 16-digit prime, the largest prime among them, is the answer. The largest primes of 15, 14 and 13 digits are 979437311917159, 99795943171373 and 9979594371317. In case 4 no valid string reaches 12 digits, two short of the bound of step 2.

**Step 4 (not settled). Case 2.** With 3-digit blocks and primes allowed, 44 digits means 42 pairwise coprime blocks out of 729 zero-free candidates over 165 primes; the walk of step 3 cannot be exhausted. A Boolean model (one variable per block value, at most one block per prime) was given to CaDiCaL and to CP-SAT ([code/p1281_case2_sat.py](code/p1281_case2_sat.py)); neither found a 44-digit string, let alone decided 45, within 15 minutes. The page's claim that 44 is the maximum length rests on a search that was not described.
