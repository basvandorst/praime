# Puzzle 1198: ssd(n) = σ(σ(n))

> **In short:** ssd(n) is the sum of σ(d) over the divisors d of n; asked are n with ssd(n) = σ(σ(n)). Known: 1, 2, 360 and 15360620700. The method: for n = m·p with a prime p not dividing m, the equation is linear in the cofactor of p + 1, so p is solved directly from m. Searched: every m ≤ 5.75·10⁶, and 7.6 million products m = s·q with σ of every prime power 31-smooth. Result: only 360 and 15360620700 come out again; no fifth number in these families.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1198.htm)

**Status:** open · no new solution in the families searched (computed)

## The question

σ(n) is the sum of the divisors of n, ssd(n) = Σ σ(d) over d | n. The numbers 1, 2 and 360 have ssd(n) = σ(σ(n)). Q1: find others. Q2: or prove there are none. The page adds 15360620700 = 2²·3·5²·3967·12907 and a direct search to 10⁶.

## How we found it

1. A direct search needs the factorisation of σ(n) for every n: about 10⁵ numbers per second, so 15360620700 is already out of reach that way.
2. ssd is multiplicative, and for a prime p not dividing m: σ(mp) = A·(p + 1) and ssd(mp) = B·(p + 2), with A = σ(m), B = ssd(m). So the unknown prime enters only through p + 1.
3. Split p + 1 = g·h, where g contains the primes that p + 1 shares with A and h is coprime to A. Then σ(A·(p + 1)) = σ(A·g)·σ(h). If h is a prime r, the equation σ(A·g)·(r + 1) = B·(g·r + 1) is linear in r. So for each m and each small g, the prime p is computed, not searched.
4. The known solution shows what a good m looks like: all σ-values of its prime powers are built from 2, 7 and 31. A second run takes only such m.

## The math

**Multiplicativity (proved).** ssd = σ * 1 (Dirichlet convolution of multiplicative functions), so ssd(p^e) = σ(1) + σ(p) + … + σ(p^e) and ssd(mp) = ssd(m)·(p + 2) for a prime p ∤ m.

**The last prime (proved).** Let n = m·p, A = σ(m), B = ssd(m), p + 1 = g·h with every prime of g dividing A and gcd(h, A) = 1. Then gcd(A·g, h) = 1 and

σ(σ(n)) = σ(A·g·h) = σ(A·g)·σ(h), ssd(n) = B·(g·h + 1).

- h = 1: the condition is σ(A·g) = B·(g + 1), with p = g − 1.
- h = r prime: c·(r + 1) = B·(g·r + 1) with c = σ(A·g), so r = (c − B)/(B·g − c). This needs B < c < B·g.

Check on the page's solution: m = 1190100 = 2²·3·5²·3967, A = 2⁹·7·31², B = 11·5·38·3969, g = 28, r = 461, p = 28·461 − 1 = 12907.

**What is covered (computed).** A solution n is found when it has a prime p ‖ n such that m = n/p is in the list and p + 1 = g·r or p + 1 = g, with g ≤ G built from primes of σ(m) and r a prime not dividing σ(m).
