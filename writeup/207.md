# Puzzle 207: All cycles of the inventory sequences — the largest period is 4

> **In short:** It was conjectured, but not proved, that every inventory sequence ends in a cycle of period at most 4. The proof: (1) after finitely many steps every term has at most 21 digits; (2) the digit counts of the next term depend only on the digit counts of the current one, and the full state is the counts plus the order in which digits first appear; (3) a complete enumeration of these states finds 151 cycles of the counts and cycle periods 1, 2, 3 and 4 only. This proves the largest period is 4, gives all 766347 self-inventoried numbers (9278 of them prime), and finds the four period-2 cycles that consist of primes only.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_207.htm)

**Status:** answered (Q2, Q6, Q7; Q4 and Q5 confirmed) · proof (complete enumeration, two independent programs)

## The question

The inventory step g reads the digits of n in order of first appearance and writes, for each, its total count followed by the digit: g(1211) = 3112. Starting from 1 the sequence ends in the cycle 23322114 ↔ 32232114. Numbers with g(n) = n (like 22) are *self-inventoried*.

- **Q1.** Does every inventory sequence end in a cycle?
- **Q2.** Is there a finite number of cycles? What is the largest period?
- **Q3.** More than 7 consecutive primes in an inventory sequence?
- **Q4, Q5.** Are there infinitely many self-inventoried composites, or primes? If not, the largest?
- **Q6.** Extend the list of self-inventoried primes.
- **Q7.** A starting number whose final cycle consists only of primes?

## How we found it

The inventory of n depends on how often each digit occurs and on the order in which the digits first appear, nothing else. So instead of numbers, which could have up to 21 digits, we followed states (count vector, order). There are about 4·10⁷ count vectors, and an order map on top of each cycle of counts. Both are small enough to enumerate completely.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). At most 21 digits.** If n has L digits, g(n) has Σ (1 + len(c_d)) digits over the digits d present, with counts c_d summing to L. The largest possible value F(L), computed exactly, is at most 21 for L ≤ 21 and below L for every L ≥ 22. For example, counts (10, 1, …, 1) give 19 → 21 digits. So every sequence reaches, and then stays in, numbers with at most 21 digits. Pe's lemma on the page says 20, but 21-digit self-inventoried numbers exist (Wharf).

**Step 2 (proved). The count map.** The digit counts c′ of g(n) depend only on the counts c of n: c′_e = [c_e > 0] + (the number of digits e among the decimal counts c_d, d present). The number g(n) itself is the string c_(σ₁)σ₁ c_(σ₂)σ₂ …, where σ is the order of first appearance in n. So (σ, c) determines g(n), and the dynamics on states (σ, c) is deterministic. A cycle of numbers is a cycle of states.

**Step 3 (complete enumeration). Cycles of the counts.** All 44352165 count vectors with digit sum ≤ 21 were followed under the count map. There are 151 cycles, of periods 1 (110 cycles, one of them the trivial empty vector), 2 (31) and 3 (10). Allowing sum ≤ 22 adds no cycle.

**Step 4 (complete enumeration). Cycles of the orders.** Take a count cycle of period P′ starting at c₀, and let S be the set of digits of c₀. Every order σ of S occurs (some n has counts c₀ and first-appearance order σ). One turn around the count cycle maps σ to a new order F(σ) of S, and the number cycles lying over this count cycle are the cycles of F, of period P′ times their length. Computing F on all |S|! orders (up to 10! = 3628800) for all 151 count cycles gives:

| period | number cycles |
|---|---|
| 1 (self-inventoried numbers) | 766347 |
| 2 | 2227 |
| 3 | 20 |
| 4 | 30 |

So the largest period is 4; the cycle of 40 on the page is one of the 30.

**Step 5 (computed). Self-inventoried numbers and primes.** The 766347 self-inventoried numbers have 2 (only 22), 8, 10, 19 or 21 digits. 9278 are prime, all with at most 19 digits. The largest composite is 221111918171615141310, and the largest prime is 1111918171614121013, both as on the page. Every 21-digit self-inventoried number is divisible by 3.

**Step 6 (computed). Q7.** Of the 2277 cycles of period 2–4, exactly four consist of primes only, all of period 2:

```
23322771161410181519 ↔ 32232771161410181519
233226611814101519   ↔ 322326611814101519
233226611014151719   ↔ 322326611014151719
233226611410151917   ↔ 322326611410151917
```

All eight are proven primes (PARI). Any start that reaches one of these, for instance the cycle members themselves, answers Q7; so do the 9278 self-inventoried primes, as cycles of period 1.

## Validation

- [code/p207_phi.c](code/p207_phi.c) enumerates the count cycles of step 3.
- [code/p207_orders.c](code/p207_orders.c) computes the order maps of step 4. It reads the cycles from `cycles.txt`; run `./p207_phi > cycles.txt` first.
- [code/p207_verify.py](code/p207_verify.py) is independent of both. It checks step 1, then finds the count cycles a different way, through the image of the count map: 122111 images, 151 cycles. It also checks the maximum period 4 by its own order dynamics, and every entry of [data/p207_selfinv_primes.txt](data/p207_selfinv_primes.txt) and [data/p207_cycles.txt](data/p207_cycles.txt). With `--full` it treats all supports and recounts the 766347 self-inventoried numbers and 9278 primes.

Run after `pip install sympy gmpy2`:

```
python3 p207_verify.py            # about 30 seconds
python3 p207_verify.py --full     # about 20 minutes
gcc -O2 -o p207_phi p207_phi.c && ./p207_phi > cycles.txt
gcc -O2 -o p207_orders p207_orders.c && ./p207_orders
```
