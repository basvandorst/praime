# Puzzle 1020: 4786 numbers with three prime factors whose reciprocals sum to 1

> **In short:** The best set of squarefree numbers with three prime factors whose reciprocals sum to 1 had 4922 terms (and 54280 on this page). The construction: (1) the sum is an integer exactly when every prime is balanced mod p; (2) start from the smallest such numbers and repair the primes from the largest down, with "upward" repairs that keep the valuable small terms; (3) close the primes 2…13 exactly. This gives 4786 terms; at least 4402 are needed.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1020.htm)

**Status:** beaten · exact (rational arithmetic)

## The question

Write 1 as a sum of reciprocals of distinct numbers that are each the product of three different primes, with fewer terms than the 73035 of puzzle 899. The same question is 899 Q2 and 897 Q3.

## How we found it

This is the method of our [problem 76](prob-76.md) moved from pairs of primes to triples. The local criterion is already stated on puzzle 897 (2017), and the 4922-term set there was found by repairing the primes from the largest down. Two changes gave the improvement:

- **Upward repairs** for the small primes 17 … 61. Instead of removing a valuable term like 1/(2·3·17), a term a·v·u with a larger prime u is toggled, and u is rebalanced with terms u·f·g, f, g ≤ 13.
- **An exact closing step** for 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13: a dynamic programme over all 30030 residue states, not a local search.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved; also on puzzle 897). The balance criterion.** For a finite set S of squarefree numbers pqr, the terms containing p sum to (1/p)·Σ 1/(qr), and the other terms have no p in the denominator. So the sum has no p in its denominator iff

```
Σ_(pqr ∈ S) (qr)⁻¹ ≡ 0 (mod p)        ("p is balanced")
```

If every prime is balanced the sum is an integer, and a sum between 0 and 2 is then exactly 1.

**Step 2 (proved). Lower bound 4402.** The reciprocals of the 4402 smallest squarefree numbers with three prime factors (30, 42, 66, …, 23309) are the first to reach 1.

**Step 3 (construction).**

1. *Bulk:* all squarefree pqr ≤ X₀ = 24600, about 4650 terms with reciprocal sum just above 1.
2. *Repair* every prime v > 13, from the largest down. If v is not balanced, toggle one term v·a·b with a, b < v. The solution of (ab)⁻¹ ≡ −r (mod v) gives one candidate b for each a. If no single term works, two terms are toggled. For v ≤ 61 an upward gadget is also allowed: toggle a·v·u with u > v, chosen so that v is fixed, plus one or two terms u·f·g (f, g ∈ {2, …, 13}) that keep u balanced. Each option is scored by Δ(terms) − 0.7·X₀·Δ(sum).
3. *Close* {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13} exactly. Their residues form one of 30030 states. The moves are the 20 triples inside this set, and gadgets (v; pairs f·g) with Σ ±(fg)⁻¹ ≡ 0 (mod v), which keep v balanced. A dynamic programme finds the cheapest combination that balances all six.

With seed 1 the result has 4786 terms on 365 primes, and the exact sum is 1. Over the settings tried (X₀ between 24600 and 24900, 8 seeds each) the results ranged from 4786 to 4834 terms; at X₀ = 24300 the repairs left the sum just below 1, so it could not close.

**What this does not settle.** The minimum lies between 4402 and 4786.

## Validation

[code/p1020_verify.py](code/p1020_verify.py) reads the 4786 numbers from [data/p1020_solution.txt](data/p1020_solution.txt). It checks that they are distinct, squarefree, with three prime factors each, and that their reciprocals sum to exactly 1 (exact fractions). It also checks that every prime is balanced and recomputes the lower bound 4402. [code/p1020_build.py](code/p1020_build.py) rebuilds the set in about 3 seconds. Run after `pip install sympy numpy`:

```
python3 p1020_verify.py
python3 p1020_build.py 24600 1 0.7 out.txt
```
