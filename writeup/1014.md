# Puzzle 1014: Three consecutive primes and sums of two squares

> **In short:** For consecutive primes p < q < r with p > 13, the page observes that none of p | q² + r², q | p² + r², r | p² + q² holds, and asks for an explanation. The page explains it by gap sizes but gives no proof. (1) With gaps g = q − p and h = r − q, the three conditions reduce to p | g² + (g + h)², q | g² + h², r | (g + h)² + h². Each right side is positive, so it must be at least the prime. (2) A direct check of all triples with p < 1.1·10⁷ finds only p = 2, 5, 13, all for the first condition. (3) Every gap below 4·10¹⁸ is at most 1476, so all three right sides are at most 1476² + 2952² = 10892880 < 1.1·10⁷ ≤ p. This proves the observation for all p ≤ 4·10¹⁸. Beyond, an exception needs a gap of size √(p/5), and at most O(X^(0.13+ε)) such gaps lie below X; the general case stays open.

## The question

For consecutive primes p < q < r, none of (a) p | q² + r², (b) q | p² + r², (c) r | p² + q² holds once p > 13. Explain it or find a counterexample. On the page:

- the same reduction to the gaps, with the empirical thresholds 2477, 293 and 2203 beyond which the residues are smaller than the prime (checked to 10⁸);
- the remark that a proof would need gaps of order √p, through Cramér–Granville.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved): the reduction.** With q = p + g and r = p + g + h: mod p, q² + r² ≡ g² + (g + h)² = x. Mod q, p² + r² ≡ g² + h² = y. Mod r, p² + q² ≡ (g + h)² + h² = z. All three are positive. So (a) needs x ≥ p, (b) needs y ≥ q, and (c) needs z ≥ r.

**Step 2 (computed): p < 1.1·10⁷.** Over all consecutive triples, (a) holds exactly for p = 2, 5, 13 (2 | 34, 5 | 170, 13 | 650), and (b) and (c) never hold. The last triples with x ≥ p, y ≥ q and z ≥ r are at p = 2477, q = 293 and r = 2203, the page's thresholds.

**Step 3 (proved from the literature): 1.1·10⁷ ≤ p ≤ 4·10¹⁸.** Every gap below 4·10¹⁸ is at most 1476 (complete computation of Oliveira e Silva, Herzog and Pardi; FACTS 3023). So x, z ≤ 1476² + 2952² = 10892880 and y ≤ 2·1476² < 1.1·10⁷ ≤ p < q < r. None of the conditions can hold.

**Step 4 (proved from the literature): beyond.** An exception needs x ≥ p, y ≥ q or z ≥ r, and each forces max(g, h) ≥ √(p/5) ≥ p^0.45 for large p. The gaps ≥ x^0.45 in [x, 2x] have total length O(x^(0.63+ε)) (Järviniemi 2022, arXiv:2212.10965). So at most O(x^(0.13+ε)) of them have length √(x/5), and up to X there are O(X^(0.13+ε)) possible exceptions. A full proof needs a gap bound of order √p, as for [conjecture 44](conj-44.md) and [puzzle 1073](1073.md).
