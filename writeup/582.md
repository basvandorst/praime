# Puzzle 582: p·q = c² + 1, and composite a² + k² built from primes c² + k²

> **In short:** The page asks for a composite m = a² + k² whose prime divisors are all of the form c² + k²; for k = 7, 8, 9 none was found. The method: (1) m is such a number exactly when a product of Gaussian integers c + k·i has imaginary part ±k, a polynomial equation that is linear in each c; (2) an even number of primes is impossible by parity, and three primes are impossible modulo 20 for k = 7; (3) all but the largest prime are enumerated and the largest is solved. This gives m for k = 7, 8, 9 and for every k up to 20.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_582.htm)

**Status:** answered · proven primes · smallest m computed, except k = 12 (best found)

## The question

Q1 and Q2 (two primes a² + k² and b² + k² with product c² + k²) are settled on the page: only 2·5 = 3² + 1. The page then asks: if m = a² + k² is not prime, can every prime divisor of m be of the form c² + k²? It gives the smallest m for k = 1 … 6, says "relatively small" m exist for k = 10, 11, 14, 15, 16, 20, and reports no m for k = 7, 8, 9.

## How we found it

1. A search over a (factor a² + k², look at the factors) reaches a ≈ 10⁷. The answers for k = 7 and 12 have a > 10¹².
2. Turned around: list the primes c² + k² first and ask which products are a² + k². In the Gaussian integers this is one equation in the numbers c.
3. A complete list of all products of three or more such primes below 10¹⁸ gave k = 8, 9, 13, 17, 19, and nothing for k = 7, 12, 18.
4. The reason: for these three k a product of three primes is impossible modulo 5 and 4 (or 5 and 16), so five primes are needed. The equation is linear in each c, so four primes are enumerated and the fifth is solved. This gave k = 7 (25 digits), k = 18 (27 digits) and k = 12 (33 digits).
5. For k = 7 and 18, all products of seven or more primes below the found m were listed as well: none works.

## The math

Fix k > 1. A prime p = c² + k² is called good. Good primes are odd and coprime to k (a common factor g of c and k would give g² | p).

**The equation (proved).** In Z[i], a good prime is p = (c + ki)(c − ki), and c + ki is a Gaussian prime. Let m be a product of n good primes, m = a² + k². Then a + ki has norm m, so by unique factorisation

a + ki = u · ∏ (cⱼ + ki), with a unit u ∈ {±1, ±i} and signed cⱼ (changing cⱼ to −cⱼ replaces the factor by −1 times its conjugate).

Expand: ∏ (cⱼ + ki) = R + k·E·i with

E = e₍ₙ₋₁₎ − k²·e₍ₙ₋₃₎ + k⁴·e₍ₙ₋₅₎ − …, R = eₙ − k²·e₍ₙ₋₂₎ + …,

where eⱼ is the j-th elementary symmetric function of c₁ … cₙ. For u = ±i we would need R = ±k; but R ≡ c₁⋯cₙ (mod k) is coprime to k, impossible. So u = ±1 and

**m = a² + k² exactly when E = ±1 for some choice of signs.**

For n = 3: c₁c₂ + c₁c₃ + c₂c₃ = k² ± 1. For n = 5: e₄ − k²e₂ + k⁴ = ±1.

**Two primes (proved).** n = 2 gives E = c₁ + c₂ = ±1 with signed c, so |c₁| and |c₂| differ by 1 and one of the two primes is even: only 2·5 for k = 1. (This is the page's answer to Q1 and Q2.)

**Local conditions (proved by enumeration modulo q).** [`code/p582_local.py`](code/p582_local.py) lists, modulo q, all products of n residues c + ki with c² + k² coprime to q, and looks for one with imaginary part ±k.

- No even n works (proved): good primes are odd, so for odd k all cⱼ are even and for even k all cⱼ are odd. For even n, e₍ₙ₋₁₎, e₍ₙ₋₃₎, … have odd index: with all cⱼ even they are even, and with all cⱼ odd, e₍ₙ₋₁₎ is a sum of n odd terms, even, while the other terms carry the even factor k². So E is even, never ±1. The script confirms this modulo 16.
- For k = 7, n = 3 fails modulo 20. By hand: c = 2a, the equation is a₁a₂ + a₁a₃ + a₂a₃ = 12 (the value 50/4 is not an integer); 4a² + 49 ≢ 0 (mod 5) forces a ≡ 0, ±1 (mod 5), and then the left side is 0, 1, 3 or 4 modulo 5, never 2.
- For k = 12, 13, 17, 18, n = 3 fails modulo 80.

So for k = 7, 12, 13, 17, 18 the number of prime factors is 5, 7, 9, …

**Five primes (computed, complete).** E = A + x·B is linear in the last number x, with A and B computed from the other four. [`code/p582_five.py`](code/p582_five.py) runs through all signed c₁ … c₄ with p₁p₂p₃p₄² ≤ M (the four smallest primes), solves x = (±1 − A)/B and keeps x when it is an integer, |x| ≥ |c₄| and x² + k² is prime. This finds every five-prime m ≤ M.
