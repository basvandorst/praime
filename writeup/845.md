# Puzzle 845: Runs of consecutive integers with an even number of prime factors

> **In short:** Ω(n) counts prime factors with multiplicity. The first run of exactly n consecutive integers with Ω even was known for n ≤ 30, the first of exactly 31, 32, 36, 39, 40, 42, 43 not. (1) A segmented sieve toggles the parity for the multiples of every prime power and tracks the cofactor, so a leftover large prime is detected without division; two threads cover 10¹¹ in under an hour. (2) The 30 known terms and the record runs are reproduced. (3) The first run of exactly 31 starts at 15576298827, the first of exactly 32 at 9021905758; none of exactly 36 or more starts below 10¹¹, as the expectation 2^{n+2} predicts.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_845.htm)

**Status:** answered (first occurrences of exactly 31 and 32 consecutive integers with even Ω, exhaustive below 10¹¹; 36, 39, 40, 42, 43 still open, none below 10¹¹)

## The question

G. L. Honaker, Jr. noted (Curio 31) that the first run of exactly 31 consecutive integers with an even number of prime factors had never been found. The page lists A275508 (first occurrence of a run of exactly n; known for n ≤ 30, the last two being 4082994208 and 3944563444) and the record runs A066793 (…, 2864568747364 starts a run of 41, 3155591650029 one of 44), and asks for the first run of exactly 31, 32, 36, 39, 40, 42 or 43, or a record run longer than 44.

## How we found it

Only the parity of Ω matters, and parity is additive over prime powers: Ω(n) ≡ Σ_{p^k | n} 1 (mod 2). A segmented sieve over [lo, hi) toggles a bit for every multiple of every prime power p^k ≤ hi with p ≤ √hi and multiplies a per-number tracker by p at the same time; at the end a number whose tracker is smaller than itself has exactly one prime factor above √hi, which toggles the parity once more. No division is needed. Runs are read off the parity bits; the first start of each exact length and the record lengths are kept. Two threads take contiguous halves of the range and recompute a margin, so a run across the boundary is counted by the half where it starts ([`code/p845_runs.c`](code/p845_runs.c)).

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). The sieve is exact.** For n < hi every prime factor of n above √hi occurs at most once and there is at most one such factor; all others are ≤ √hi and are counted with multiplicity by the toggles at p, p², p³, …. The cofactor tracker equals the product of the prime powers p^{v_p(n)} over p ≤ √hi, so it differs from n exactly when the large prime factor exists.

**Step 2 (computed). Check against the literature.** The sieve to 10⁸ returns the first occurrences 1, 9, 14, 33, 54, 201, 140, 2170, 213, 4529, 10403, 1934, 35811, 162144, 38405, 414225, 200938, 389409, 1792209, 5606457, 18493931, 30947532, 61190563, 8405437, and 68780189 for n = 28, the terms of A275508, and the record starts 1, 9, 14, 33, 54, 140, 213, 1934, 35811, 38405, 200938, 389409, 1792209, 5606457, 8405437, 68780189 of A066793. The run to 10¹¹ reproduces all 30 known terms and the record runs 880346227 (33), 85910903650 (34) and 87635944146 (35).

**Step 3 (computed). New terms.** Below 10¹¹ the first run of exactly 31 consecutive integers with even Ω starts at 15576298827 (so 15576298826 and 15576298858 have odd Ω) and the first run of exactly 32 at 9021905758. These are A275508(31) and A275508(32). No run of exactly 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42 or 43 starts below 10¹¹; the only runs of 36 or more are the known record runs above 2.8·10¹¹.

**Step 4 (heuristic). Where the missing terms should be.** The parity of Ω behaves like a fair coin (Liouville's function has mean zero), so a run of exactly L starting at a given position has probability about 2^{−(L+2)}: the first run of exactly L is expected near 2^{L+2}, that is 8.6·10⁹ for L = 31, 1.7·10¹⁰ for 32, 2.7·10¹¹ for 36, 2.2·10¹² for 39, 4.4·10¹² for 40 and 1.8·10¹³ and 3.5·10¹³ for 42 and 43. The known terms fit: the first run of exactly 29 and 30 are at 4.1·10⁹ and 3.9·10⁹ against 2.1·10⁹ and 4.3·10⁹ expected. Our terms for 31 and 32, at 1.6·10¹⁰ and 9.0·10⁹, sit where expected; the first run of exactly 36 is expected near 2.7·10¹¹, just beyond this search, and those of exactly 39 and 40 near 2·10¹² and 4·10¹². A run to 10¹² (about ten times the cost) should settle 36 and has a fair chance at 39.
