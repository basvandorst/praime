# Puzzle 1271: 91997

> **In short:** 91997 is a prime whose five digits, appended one by one to 91996, 91995, 91994, 91993, 91992, give five primes; the page asks whether a larger such prime exists, and searches to 1.8·10¹³ found none. The answer: (1) every digit of such a prime must be 1, 3, 7 or 9, since each appended digit becomes a last digit; (2) a k-digit candidate needs k + 1 primes of about k digits, so the expected number of k-digit solutions is about 4ᵏ·(5/(2k ln 10))^(k+1), which is 2.6, 1.1, 0.38, 0.11, 0.026, 0.0055 for k = 2 … 7 and falls faster than geometrically; (3) the solutions below 10⁷ are 3, 7, 19, 773, 91997, in line with the expectation; the expected number of solutions with 14 or more digits, beyond the search, is 7·10⁻⁹. So 91997 is almost surely the largest, though not provably.

## The question

Sariyar's curio: 91997 is the largest prime n such that, writing n = d₁d₂…d_k, the numbers (n − i) with the digit dᵢ appended are all prime: 919969, 919951, 919949, 919939, 919927. Q: is there a larger example? Branicky found none below 10¹², Cavegn none below 1.8234·10¹³; Jameel argued that only finitely many can exist.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved): the digits.** The i-th appended number is 10(n − i) + dᵢ, whose last digit is dᵢ. For it to be a prime larger than 10, dᵢ ∈ {1, 3, 7, 9}. So n has all its digits in {1, 3, 7, 9}: there are 4ᵏ candidates with k digits.

**Step 2 (heuristic): expected counts.** A k-digit string over {1, 3, 7, 9} is odd and prime to 5, so it is prime with probability about (5/2)/ln(10ᵏ). Each of the k appended numbers is odd, prime to 5 and of size 10ᵏ, with the same probability. Treating the k + 1 events as independent,

```
expected k-digit solutions ≈ 4ᵏ · (5/(2k ln 10))^(k+1).
```

The sequence of expectations, k = 2 … 8: 2.56, 1.10, 0.38, 0.107, 0.026, 0.0055, 0.0010, then 1.7·10⁻⁴, 2.6·10⁻⁵, …; the ratio of consecutive terms is about 4·(5/(2k ln 10)) → 0, so the series converges fast. Up to 7 digits the model expects 4.2 solutions and there are 4 (19, 773, 91997 and, for k = 1, the primes 3 and 7 with 3 → 29, 7 → 67). Beyond 13 digits the expectation is 6.6·10⁻⁹.

**Step 3 (computed).** A sieve to 10⁸ checks every prime below 10⁷ with digits in {1, 3, 7, 9}: the solutions are exactly 3, 7, 19, 773, 91997.
