# Puzzle 1279: Sierpiński and Riesel numbers that need 12 covering primes, and numbers with five covering sets

> **In short:** Q3 asks for the least Sierpiński and the least Riesel number whose covering set needs 12 or more primes. These are the missing terms a(12) of OEIS A206001 and A206430, which end at a(11). Q4 asks for the least number with five distinct covering sets; the best on the page has four, with 15 digits. (1) For a prime p with m = ord_p(2), p divides k·2ⁿ ± 1 for exactly one class of n mod m, and only when k lies in a coset of ⟨2⟩ mod p. So a set covers k or not according to k mod 2P, and the check runs on n mod M. (2) A block sieve over odd k tests the necessary condition that n = 0 … 63 are covered. The survivors are tested on all n mod 10080, with the 97 primes p < 2³² for which ord_p(2) divides 10080. For every covered k all minimal covering sets are listed. (3) The search reproduces a(6) … a(11) of both sequences and the OEIS lists of numbers with two covering sets. A second implementation without the sieve agrees on 1351 covered k. (4) Q3: the least Riesel number with a 12-prime covering set is 22828394579, set {3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 31, 37, 151, 331, 433, 631}. The least Sierpiński number is 228288720707, set {3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 31, 37, 41, 151, 181, 433}. Each has no other minimal covering set, so each needs 12 primes. (5) Q4: the Sierpiński number 3278650021 has six minimal covering sets and is the least Sierpiński number with even three. The least Riesel numbers with five or more are 141719191807 (six sets) and 203675118523 (exactly five). Both searches cover k < 2.283·10¹¹ and covering sets drawn from the 97 primes.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1279.htm)

**Status:** answered (Q3: 22828394579 and 228288720707; Q4: 3278650021 and 141719191807; least for covering sets drawn from the stated 97 primes, complete search below 2.283·10¹¹, computed)

## The question

A Sierpiński number k makes k·2ⁿ + 1 composite for every n ≥ 1; a Riesel number makes k·2ⁿ − 1 composite. A covering set is a finite set of primes, one of which divides each k·2ⁿ ± 1. Only minimal sets count: no prime can be dropped. The page asks:

- Q1: the first number with a 5-prime set. The page answers that 6 primes are the minimum.
- Q2: the first number with a 10-prime set. The page answers A206001 a(10) = 11679013547 and A206430 a(10) = 10909924841.
- **Q3:** "What are the minimal S or R integers that need 12 or more distinct primes in its CS?"
- **Q4:** "What is the minimal S or R integer having five distinct CS?"

Problem 92 has the record for many covering sets. It gives a Sierpiński number with four sets, 218164505156603, and a Riesel number with four, 16107442173648029. Our [problem 92 write-up](prob-92.md) built numbers with five (27 digits) and eight (253 digits), but did not ask for the least.

## How we found it

The known sets for a(6) … a(11) all have periods dividing 720, except A206430 a(10), which needs 288. So the search allows every period dividing 10080 = 2⁵·3²·5·7. That also admits the primes of order 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, … (127, 43, 337, 29, 113, 71, …), which no known set uses. Most odd k fail on one of the first 64 exponents, and a cheap sieve on n = 0 … 63 removes them. About 1 odd k in 3250 reaches the full test, and 1 in 75000 is covered. For each covered k all minimal covering sets are listed, so the search also counts numbers with several sets. That count, not the 12-prime question, gave the surprise: about 1.1 % of the covered numbers have two or more sets, and six sets turn up below 10¹⁰.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved): one class per prime.** Let p be an odd prime with m = ord_p(2) and s = ±1. Then p | k·2ⁿ + s iff 2ⁿ ≡ −s·k⁻¹ (mod p). This is solvable iff −s·k⁻¹ ∈ ⟨2⟩, and then exactly the n in one class a (mod m) work. So p is useful for k iff k ≡ −s·2^(−a) (mod p) for some a. These are m residues mod p, and each covers its own class a. A set CS covers k iff its classes cover Z/M, where M = lcm of the orders. This depends only on k mod 2P, P = ∏ CS.

**Step 2 (proved): the search is exact for the prime list.** If the primes cover k, they cover n = 0 … 63. For each prime p and each useful residue r, the sieve ORs the 64-bit mask {n < 64 : n ≡ a (mod m)} into every k ≡ r (mod p) of a block of 2²⁰ odd numbers. A k with an incomplete mask cannot be covered. The survivors get the full test on Z/10080 with all 97 primes. If it passes, a depth-first search lists every minimal covering subset of at most 20 primes. It branches on the primes that cover the least uncovered n. Every minimal set is reached this way, because one of its primes covers that n, and each set found is checked for minimality. A counter for covered k without any minimal subset of at most 20 primes stayed at 0, so the 20-prime cap loses nothing.

**Step 3 (computed): the prime list.** The primes with ord_p(2) | 10080 are the prime factors of 2^d − 1 for d | 10080. For d ≤ 64 they come from the full factorisation of 2^d − 1. For d > 64 every p = 1 + j·d below 2³² was tested for 2^d ≡ 1 (mod p) and order exactly d. That gives 97 primes ([`data/p1279_primes.txt`](data/p1279_primes.txt)), from 3 (order 2) to 4278255361 (order 80), including 20161 (order 10080), 61681 (order 40), 1153 (order 288) and the order-7 family 127, 43, 337, 29, 113.

Both 12-prime sets of Q3 have period 360, with orders {2, 4, 3, 10, 12, 8, 5, 36, 20, 15, 180, 72} and {2, 4, 3, 10, 12, 8, 5, 36, 15, 30, 72, 45}. All 197 numbers with three or more sets are listed with their sets in [`data/p1279_multi.txt`](data/p1279_multi.txt).

**Step 5 (computed): how one number gets six sets.** For 3278650021 the core {3, 5, 7, 19, 37} covers every class of n mod 36 except 0, 8, 20 and 32. The other primes it can use cover single classes: 13 covers 8 (mod 12), that is 8, 20 and 32 mod 36. 73 covers 0 (mod 9), 109 covers 0 (mod 36), 17 covers 0 (mod 8), 241 covers 20 (mod 24) and 433 covers 36 (mod 72). So the gap can be closed in three ways:

- with 13, the class 0 mod 36 is left, and 73 or 109 closes it (two sets of period 36);
- with 13, split 0 mod 36 into 0 and 36 mod 72: 17 takes 0 and 433 takes 36 (one set);
- without 13: 17 and 241 take everything but 36 mod 72, and 73, 109 or 433 closes it (three sets).

That is 2 + 1 + 3 = 6 minimal sets. Several sets arise whenever the residues of k mod a few extra primes land on the classes the core leaves open, so they are common: 1.1 % of all covered numbers have two or more. The earlier lists stayed short because they used a restricted search (next step).

**Step 6 (computed): the OEIS lists of numbers with three covering sets.** A394518 (Sierpiński) starts at 56889499253 and A394519 (Riesel) at 14995746487, but the search finds 59 + 46 + 1 Sierpiński and 36 + 53 + 2 Riesel numbers with three or more sets below 2.283·10¹¹, starting at 3278650021 and 3355171871. Each listed set was rechecked from scratch. In the searched range, the OEIS terms are exactly the k that have three sets of at most 7 primes avoiding 673: the 4 Sierpiński terms below 2.283·10¹¹ and the 6 Riesel terms. So those lists come from a restricted search, and the definition stated in the OEIS ("at least three distinct finite sets of primes") admits many more numbers. The lists of numbers with two sets (A263391, A263392) agree with the search on all their terms.

**Step 7 (heuristic): rates.** A Sierpiński set and its mirror Riesel set have the same density (the conversion k ↦ 2P − k, see [puzzle 1280](1280.md)), so both sides should show the same rates. They do for the common sizes, with 979756 six-prime numbers on each side. For 12-prime sets the counts are 1 and 5: 6 events, with a split this uneven 22 % of the time by chance. The joint rate is 2.6·10⁻¹¹ per odd k on each side, so the first 12-prime set was expected near 8·10¹⁰ on each side (1/rate odd numbers); 2.3·10¹⁰ and 2.3·10¹¹ are within the normal spread of an exponential waiting time.

**What is not covered.** The search is complete only for covering sets drawn from the 97 primes. A covering set with a prime of order not dividing 10080, or a prime above 2³², could in principle give a smaller k with 12 primes or more sets. Widening the period from 720 to 10080 added 64 primes and did not change the covered numbers or their set sizes below 1.2·10⁹. Q4 is answered as the least number with five or more sets; the least Sierpiński number with exactly five sets is above 3.423·10¹¹. An extension of the Sierpiński scan from 2.283·10¹¹ to 3.423·10¹¹ (same program) found 20 numbers with three sets, 22 with four, none with five or more, and no further 12-prime set.

## Validation

- [`code/p1279_scan.c`](code/p1279_scan.c): the sieve search (both sides, 8 runs of 5.7·10¹⁰, about 10 minutes each on one core). It prints every k with a 12-prime set, every k with two or more sets, and the totals of Step 4.
- [`code/p1279_scan_direct.c`](code/p1279_scan_direct.c): a second implementation without the sieve. It computes k mod p directly for every prime, looks the residue up in a hash table, and lists the minimal sets by include/exclude recursion. On ten windows of 2·10⁷ around the results and at random places, both programs print the same 1351 covered k, with the same number of sets and the same sizes.
- [`code/p1279_primes.c`](code/p1279_primes.c), [`code/p1279_primes_list.py`](code/p1279_primes_list.py): the prime list.
- [`code/p1279_validate.py`](code/p1279_validate.py) (35 s), in Python with plain integers:
  1. It rebuilds the prime list independently (factorisations for d ≤ 64, a vectorised 2^d mod p test over all p = 1 + j·d < 2³² for d > 64) and finds the same 97 primes.
  2. It checks both 12-prime sets on every n mod 360, their minimality, and that they are the only minimal sets from the list.
  3. It checks the sizes of the known terms a(6) … a(11).
  4. It checks every set of all 197 multi-cover numbers, and that the lists are complete for the prime list.
  5. It derives the least numbers with 3, 4, 5, 6 sets and the restricted rule that gives the OEIS lists.
