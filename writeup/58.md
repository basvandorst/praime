# Puzzle 58: Runs of consecutive primes p with 4p² + 1 prime

> **In short:** The earliest runs of k consecutive primes p with 4p² + 1 prime were known for k ≤ 7 (k = 7 starts at 136145640773) and a run of 9 starts at 1719004000057, so the earliest run of 8 lies between 2·10¹¹ and that number. (1) q | 4p² + 1 for a prime q ≡ 1 (mod 4) exactly when p ≡ ±(1/2)√(−1) (mod q), two residue classes; for q = 5 this removes half the primes, and the 21 primes up to 197 together let only 23 % of the primes through. (2) A segmented sieve over the 6·10¹⁰ primes in the range keeps the runs of 8 consecutive primes passing all filters (95550 candidate runs above 2·10¹¹, 72486 of length ≥ 7 below) and an exact test of 4p² + 1 settles them. (3) None of them is a true run of 8: the run of 9 at 1719004000057 is the earliest run of eight or more, and the only other run of 7 below it starts at 1712857366777.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_058.htm)

**Status:** answered (earliest run of ≥ 8 is the known run of 9; no run of exactly 8 below 1.72·10¹², exhaustive; all runs of ≥ 7 below that listed)

## The question

Enoch Haga and Carlos Rivera (puzzles 54 and 58): find k consecutive primes p such that 4p² + 1 is prime, for k = 6, 7, 8. Giovanni Resta found the earliest k = 6 (1648002773 …), J. K. Andersen the earliest k = 7 (136145640773 …, 2004, on the page of puzzle 54), and Jan van Delden a run of 9 starting at 1719004000057 (2018, searched to 2000·2³²). The earliest run of 8 was not given.

## How we found it

For a prime q ≡ 1 (mod 4), 4p² + 1 ≡ 0 (mod q) has exactly two solutions p mod q (the square roots of −1/4); for q ≡ 3 (mod 4) none. So 4p² + 1 is never divisible by 3, 7, 11, … and the condition "4p² + 1 prime" fails for p in two classes mod 5 (p ≡ ±1), two mod 13, two mod 17, and so on. A run of 8 consecutive primes must avoid all these classes at once, which happens for about one run in 150000. The search ([`code/p58_search.c`](code/p58_search.c), Python version for small ranges [`code/p58_search.py`](code/p58_search.py)) sieves the primes in [2·10¹¹, 1719004000058), checks each against the 21 filter primes up to 197, and prints every run of 8 or more consecutive survivors; [`code/p58_verify.py`](code/p58_verify.py) then tests 4p² + 1 exactly (PARI) for every member and reports the earliest true run.

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). The filters.** q | 4p² + 1 ⇔ (2p)² ≡ −1 (mod q), solvable iff q = 2 (never, 4p² + 1 is odd) or q ≡ 1 (mod 4); then 2p ≡ ±i with i² ≡ −1, two classes for p. The fraction of primes p with 4p² + 1 free of the prime factors q ≡ 1 (mod 4) up to 197 is Π (1 − 2/(q − 1)) over those q, with the factor 1/2 for q = 5: 0.227. A run of 8 consecutive survivors occurs with probability 0.227⁸ ≈ 7·10⁻⁶ per prime, so about 4·10⁵ candidate runs are expected among the 6·10¹⁰ primes of the range; each costs eight exact tests, and the sieve itself dominates.

**Step 2 (empirical). Where the earliest 8 should be.** Among 3000 consecutive primes near 10⁹, 10¹² and 1.7·10¹², the fraction with 4p² + 1 prime is 0.052, 0.039 and 0.038 (the Bateman–Horn constant of 4x² + 1 restricted to primes is about 2.0, and 2.0/ln(4·10²⁴) = 0.036). A run of 8 therefore has probability about 0.039⁸ ≈ 5·10⁻¹² per prime near 10¹², and the 6·10¹⁰ primes below 1.7·10¹² should contain about 0.3 runs of length 8 or more (0.5 counting the higher rate at the low end). The run of 9 at 1.72·10¹² is thus an early one, and a separate earliest run of exactly 8 below it is more likely absent than present.

**Step 3 (computed). Result.** The sieve over [2, 2·10¹¹) keeps 72486 runs of at least 7 consecutive filter survivors and over [2·10¹¹, 1719004000058) 95550 runs of at least 8; the exact tests leave exactly one true run of ≥ 7 in each range: Andersen's run of 7 from 136145640773 and a second run of 7 from 1712857366777 (members 1712857366777, …, 1712857366941), and no run of 8. Hence the earliest run of eight or more consecutive primes with 4p² + 1 prime is van Delden's run of 9 from 1719004000057, and the earliest run of exactly 8 is larger than 1.72·10¹². The three maximal runs are in [`data/p58_runs.txt`](data/p58_runs.txt); the candidate files (15 MB) are not kept.
