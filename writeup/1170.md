# Puzzle 1170: Perfect numbers and Ore's harmonic numbers

> **In short:** A harmonic number has an integer harmonic mean of divisors; every perfect number is harmonic. Q1 asks for a proof that a perfect number has no harmonic proper divisor other than 1; Q2 asks whether 6200 is the only non-perfect harmonic number with that property. (1) For an even perfect number 2^(p−1)(2^p − 1), the harmonic mean of a proper divisor 2^a or 2^a(2^p − 1) is a fraction whose denominator contains 2^(a+1) − 1, coprime to everything else; so Q1 holds for every even perfect number. (2) Among all 10006 known harmonic numbers (complete below 10¹⁴), the only ones with the property are the eight even perfect numbers in range and 6200.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1170.htm)

**Status:** answered (Q1 proved for even perfect numbers, i.e. for every known perfect number; for an odd perfect number it reduces to Ore's conjecture on its divisors. Q2: 6200 is the only one among the 10006 known harmonic numbers up to 6.4·10¹⁹; complete below 10¹⁴, literature)

## The question

A number k is **harmonic** (an Ore number, OEIS A001599) when the harmonic mean of its divisors, H(k) = k·τ(k)/σ(k), is an integer. Ore proved that every perfect number is harmonic. The page asks:

- **Q1.** Is it provable that the perfect numbers are harmonic numbers none of whose non-trivial divisors (divisors other than 1 and k) is harmonic?
- **Q2.** 6200 is a harmonic number that is not perfect but has the same property. Are there others?

Gennady Gusev writes: "The number 6200 is the only one among the first 10,000 Ore's harmonic numbers with this property."

## How we found it

An even perfect number is 2^(p−1)·M with M = 2^p − 1 prime, so its divisors are 2^a and 2^a·M. For these the harmonic mean has a closed form, and a divisibility argument shows it is never an integer except for 1 and the number itself. For Q2 we took the known harmonic numbers from the OEIS and tested every proper divisor of each.

## The math

Write H(n) = n·τ(n)/σ(n). Let N = 2^(p−1)·M with M = 2^p − 1 prime (p prime). Every divisor of N is 2^a (0 ≤ a ≤ p − 1) or 2^a·M (0 ≤ a ≤ p − 1).

**Step 1 (proved). Divisors 2^a with a ≥ 1 are not harmonic.** τ(2^a) = a + 1, σ(2^a) = 2^(a+1) − 1, so H(2^a) = 2^a(a + 1)/(2^(a+1) − 1). The denominator is odd, so it must divide a + 1. But 2^(a+1) − 1 > a + 1 for a ≥ 1. So H(2^a) is not an integer.

**Step 2 (proved). Divisors 2^a·M with a ≤ p − 2 are not harmonic.** τ = 2(a + 1) and σ = (2^(a+1) − 1)(M + 1) = (2^(a+1) − 1)·2^p, so

H(2^a·M) = 2^a·M·2(a + 1) / ((2^(a+1) − 1)·2^p) = M(a + 1) / (2^(p−a−1)·(2^(a+1) − 1)).

Since gcd(2^(a+1) − 1, 2^p − 1) = 2^gcd(a+1, p) − 1 and 1 ≤ a + 1 ≤ p − 1 < p with p prime, this gcd is 1. So 2^(a+1) − 1 must divide a + 1, which fails for a ≥ 1 as in step 1. For a = 0 the denominator is 2^(p−1) and the numerator M is odd, so H(M) = 2M/2^p is not an integer either (p ≥ 2).

**Step 3 (proved). Q1 for even perfect numbers.** Steps 1 and 2 cover every divisor except 1 and N = 2^(p−1)·M itself. So no even perfect number has a harmonic proper divisor other than 1. Every known perfect number is even (Euclid–Euler).

**Step 4 (literature). Odd perfect numbers.** If an odd perfect number N exists, a harmonic proper divisor d > 1 of N would be an odd harmonic number > 1. Ore conjectured that none exists; Cohen and Sorli (Math. Comp. 79, 2010) proved that an odd harmonic number > 1 exceeds 10²⁴. So Q1 for odd perfect numbers is Ore's conjecture restricted to divisors of odd perfect numbers: open, but any counterexample divisor exceeds 10²⁴.

**Step 5 (computed, literature). Q2.** The OEIS b-file of A001599 has 10000 terms, up to 6.43·10¹⁹; Goto and Shibata proved the list complete below 10¹⁴ (Math. Comp. 73, 2004, and Goto's extension). Borysiuk's list a001599_1.txt (the conjectured 10000 smallest) adds 6 numbers, for 10006 known harmonic numbers in all. For each of them we tested every proper divisor d > 1 for H(d) ∈ ℤ. Exactly nine have no harmonic proper divisor other than 1: the eight even perfect numbers 6, 28, 496, 8128, 33550336, 8589869056, 137438691328, 2305843008139952128, and 6200 = 2³·5²·31 with H(6200) = 10. A proper divisor of a harmonic number below 10¹⁴ is below 10¹⁴, so for those the result does not depend on the list being complete above 10¹⁴; it is complete there by the literature, and complete to 6.4·10¹⁹ if the b-file is.
