# Puzzle 1049: Chains of primes under p → Σ dᵢ^dᵢ

> **In short:** f(p) = Σ dᵢ^dᵢ over the digits of a zero-free prime p; the page wants the least p giving n further primes, had n ≤ 3, a doubted n = 4 and a non-minimal 6-chain. (1) f depends only on the digit multiset; among the 293929 multisets of at most 12 digits exactly one has an image with three more primes, so 112222268681 is minimal for n = 4. (2) For n = 5 the image q is one of the 5566 prime arrangements of that multiset and the fewest digits with Σ d^d = q is a coin-change problem: minimum 321 digits, and the smallest prime arrangement is the least n = 5 term. (3) n = 6 needs more than 10³¹¹ digits.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1049.htm)

**Status:** answered (n = 4 proved minimal; n = 5 found and proved minimal under the rule that every chain member is a zero-free prime; n = 6 out of reach for every computer)

## The question

Paolo Lava: take a zero-free prime p and sum its digits each powered to itself; if the result is prime, repeat. The least zero-free primes producing n further primes are 11 (n = 1: 11 → 2), 1433 (n = 2: 1433 → 311 → 29) and 122527 (n = 3: → 826681 → 33647749 → 389114797). Q: the minimum values for n > 3. Emmanuel Vantieghem, Giorgos Kalogeropoulos and Simon Cavegn found 112222268681 for n = 4 (→ 33647767 → 2564251 → 53171 → 826697), which Vantieghem suspected not to be minimal; he also gave a 6-chain starting from a 341-digit prime ("almost impossible to handle the minimal question").

## How we found it

The map f(p) = Σ dᵢ^dᵢ depends only on how many times each digit occurs. So "the least prime with a chain of length n" is a two-stage problem: find the digit multisets whose image starts a chain of length n − 1, then the smallest prime arrangement of such a multiset. For n = 4 the multisets of at most 12 digits (293929 of them) are enumerated directly ([`code/p1049_search.py`](code/p1049_search.py)). For n = 5 the image q must be an n = 4 term, which pins q down to the prime arrangements of 111222226688; then "fewest digits with Σ d^d = q" is a coin-change problem with the coins 1, 4, 27, 256, 3125, 46656, 823543, 16777216, 387420489, solved exactly by a search with lower bounds, and the least arrangement is found by walking the permutations of the optimal multiset in increasing order until one is prime ([`code/p1049_n5_strict.py`](code/p1049_n5_strict.py)).

## The math

**Step 1 (proved). Reduction to multisets.** f(p) = Σ_d c_d·d^d where c_d is the number of digits d of p; so f(p), and the whole chain after p, depend only on the multiset. The least prime with a given multiset is its smallest prime permutation. Hence the least p with chain ≥ n is the minimum, over the multisets whose image S = Σ c_d d^d is a zero-free prime with chain(S) ≥ n − 1, of the smallest prime arrangement; fewer digits always wins.

**Step 2 (computed). n = 4.** Over all multisets of 1 to 12 digits from 1 … 9, exactly one image S is a zero-free prime with three more zero-free primes behind it: 111222226688 → 33647767 → 2564251 → 53171 → 826697 (the next value 405114564 is composite). Its smallest prime arrangement is 112222268681. Every other prime with at most 12 digits has a shorter chain, so 112222268681 is the minimal n = 4 term.

**Step 3 (proved). The shape of an n = 5 term.** If p has chain ≥ 5 then q = f(p) is a zero-free prime with chain ≥ 4, so q ≥ 112222268681 by Step 2, and since f(p) ≤ 9⁹·(digits of p), p has at least 112222268681/387420489 > 289.6, that is 290 digits. Conversely q ≤ 9⁹·D for a p with D digits; the least D turns out to be 321 (Step 4), so every competing q is below 321·9⁹ < 10¹² and has 12 digits; its own image f(q) is then the image of a 12-digit multiset with chain ≥ 3, which by Step 2 forces q to be an arrangement of 111222226688. There are 5566 prime arrangements, all with chain exactly 4.

**Step 4 (computed). The least digit count and the least p.** For each of the 5566 values q, D(q) = the least number of values d^d (d = 1 … 9, repetitions allowed) summing to q, a coin-change problem solved by a depth-first search over the counts c₉, c₈, … with the lower bound "remaining value / largest remaining coin", and independently by a DP table for the coins up to 7⁷ combined with an enumeration of c₉ and c₈. The least value is D = 321, attained by q = 112826622281 (multiset 9²⁹¹ 8⁵ 7⁴ 6¹ 5¹⁰ 4⁷ 3¹ 2¹ 1¹) and q = 116226228821 (9³⁰⁰ 6¹ 5¹¹ 4⁴ 3² 2³). For comparison, a 290-digit p with a nines would need q − a·9⁹ to be a sum of 290 − a values d^d with d ≤ 8: a = 289 leaves 257747360 for a single value (at most 8⁸ = 16777216), a ≤ 288 leaves at least 645167849 for at most two values; both impossible, and the exact optimum is 31 digits higher. Of the first multiset, the 319th arrangement in increasing order is the first prime; it is smaller than every arrangement of the second multiset (whose sorted form starts 2223…). So the least zero-free prime with a chain of five zero-free primes is the 321-digit

with the chain p → 112826622281 → 33647767 → 2564251 → 53171 → 826697 (→ 405114564, composite). p is proven prime (PARI, APR-CL), the 318 smaller arrangements are composite.

**Step 5 (proved). n = 6.** An n = 6 term p has f(p) equal to an n = 5 term, so f(p) ≥ the number above, about 1.23·10³²⁰, and p has at least 1.23·10³²⁰/9⁹ > 3·10³¹¹ digits. The sequence therefore ends, for all practical purposes, at n = 5.

**Remark (reading of the rule).** The page's chains have no zeros anywhere, and Vantieghem notes of 405114564 that it "is not prime nor zerofree", so we required every chain member to be a zero-free prime. Under the looser reading, where only p itself must be zero-free, the n = 4 minimum is unchanged (a second multiset, 112333346669, also qualifies, but its arrangements are larger) and the n = 5 minimum drops to a 311-digit prime whose image is q = 114693336623, with the chain 114693336623 → 387560827 → 35251331 → 6337 → 870253 (two members contain a zero); the candidates q are then the prime arrangements of the 11- and 12-digit multisets whose image has a loose chain of 3, 14193 of them ([`code/p1049_loose.py`](code/p1049_loose.py), [`data/p1049_loose.txt`](data/p1049_loose.txt)). Under that reading the n = 6 bound becomes 10³⁰¹ digits.
