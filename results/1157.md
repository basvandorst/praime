# Puzzle 1157: Floor formulas for consecutive primes

> **In short:** For consecutive primes p < q, Sebastián Martín Ruiz's three formulas ⌊(p² + q²)/(2p)⌋ = q, ⌊(p² + q²)/(2q)⌋ = p and ⌊(p² + q²)/(p + q)⌋ = (p + q)/2 come down to the gap g = q − p being below √(2p), √(2q) and √(2(p + q)) respectively. Emmanuel Vantieghem reduced them on the page and noted there is no proof. We prove all three for every p ≤ 4·10¹⁸: formula 1 fails only at p = 7, and formulas 2 and 3 never fail. Below 2·10⁸ this is a direct check, and above that no gap up to 4·10¹⁸ exceeds 1476. Beyond 4·10¹⁸ at most O(X^(0.07+ε)) primes p ≤ X can be exceptions.

**Puzzle:** [primepuzzles.net](https://www.primepuzzles.net/puzzles/puzz_1157.htm)

**Status:** answered (Q2) · all three formulas proved for p ≤ 4·10¹⁸ (formula 1 fails only at p = 7); at most O(X^(0.07+ε)) exceptions beyond; open in general (needs gaps < √(2p))

## The question

Let q > p be consecutive primes. Sebastián Martín Ruiz proposes

1. ⌊(p² + q²)/(2p)⌋ = q (fails only for p = 7, q = 11),
2. ⌊(p² + q²)/(2q)⌋ = p,
3. ⌊(p² + q²)/(p + q)⌋ = (p + q)/2 for q > p > 2,

and asks whether they are interesting (Q1) and to prove them or find a counterexample (Q2). Emmanuel Vantieghem showed on the page that they are equivalent to g < √(2p), g < √(2q) and g < √(2(p + q)), "stronger than Andrica's conjecture but weaker than Oppermann's". He added: "As far as I know there is no proof for this up to now."

## The math

| # | Claim | Why | Validated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | formula 1 ⇔ g² < 2p; formula 2 ⇔ g² < 2q; formula 3 ⇔ g² < 2(p + q) | (p² + q²)/(2p) = q + g²/(2p), (p² + q²)/(2q) = p + g²/(2q), (p² + q²)/(p + q) = (p + q)/2 + g²/(2(p + q)) (Vantieghem) | every pair below 2·10⁸, exactly |
| 2 | below 2·10⁸ the only failure of any formula is formula 1 at p = 7 (g = 4, 16 > 14) | direct check | all consecutive primes below 2·10⁸ |
| 3 | for 2·10⁸ < p ≤ 4·10¹⁸ all three hold | every gap there is ≤ 1476 (complete computation of Oliveira e Silva, Herzog and Pardi), and 1476² = 2178576 < 2p | theorem (literature) |
| 4 | #{p ≤ X : formula 1 fails} ≪ X^(0.07+ε), and so for the others | a failure needs a gap ≥ √(2p) > √p; gaps ≥ √x in [x, 2x] have total length ≪ x^(0.57+ε) (Järviniemi, arXiv:2212.10965) | theorem (literature + one line) |

The near-misses show how much room there is. After p = 7 the largest values of g²/(2p) are 196/226 = 0.87 (p = 113) and 36/46 = 0.78 (p = 23). For 1327 (gap 34) it is 1156/2654 = 0.44, and above 10⁴ it stays below 0.09 (checked up to 2·10⁶).

**Q1 (are they interesting?).** They are three faces of one fact, small gaps, that is known to hold everywhere anyone can check and that nobody can prove. Formula 1 is the strongest of the three. It sits between Andrica's conjecture (g < 2√p + 1, roughly) and Oppermann's (g < √p). The same pattern settles [puzzle 997](997.md) and conjectures [44](conj-44.md) and [53](conj-53.md).

**Beyond 4·10¹⁸ (conditional).** The published list of 85 maximal gaps (OEIS A005250; the 85th is 1854 after 101412319996363309069, found in 2026) comes from distributed searches with no refereed statement of completeness (FACTS 3023). If that list is complete, every gap below 1.01·10²⁰ is at most 1854, and the same argument extends the result to p < 1.01·10²⁰, since 1854² ≈ 3.4·10⁶ and (2·1854)² ≈ 1.4·10⁷ are both far below 2·10⁸.
