Puzzle 1231 A follow-up to Puzzle 1229

Thinking twice on the solutions given by Oscar Volpatti to Puzzle 1229, I guess that he has not only shown that the smallest solution now is bounded between 2*10^14 (G. Resta)  and 201033 000620 001000 001 (O. Volpatti, 21 digits), but -the most important thing is that- he also has shown us implicitly an approach to continue reducing this bound, until...???

Of course that his approach is still basically a trial & error approach; nevertheless give us one clue on how to tackle this puzzle.

Except that someone of you discover another better approach, of course...
Q. Can you produce a smaller solution to Puzzle 1229 -smaller than 201033000620001000001?

Oscar wrote:

I wasn't able to find new approaches.
Exaustive search with fixed digit sum 23 found no solutions with 18 digits or less.
Previous work provides several constraints on desired primes p1...p7 and digit sums s1...s7.
p7 has at most 21 digits, hence s7 is smaller than 21*9 = 189:
s7 is at most 181 and s1 is at least 23 (values up to 19 already checked);
within such range, the cumulative gap s7-s1 is at least 24;
minimum value obtained for s1 = 23,29,37,59, or 89;
consequently, the cumulative gap p7-p1 must be at least 798;
minimum value obtained when:
either p1 ends with 001 and p7 ends with 799,
or p1 ends with 101 and p7 ends with 899,
or p1 ends with 201 and p7 ends with 999.
Among primes with 21 digits or less, the average gap is less than 50 and the average cumulative gap is less than 300, so the value 798 is already quite big.
I decided to check only primes matching such "minimum" gap.
