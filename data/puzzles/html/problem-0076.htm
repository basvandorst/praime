This is the way I worked :
________________________________

 
In the sequel, a squarefree odd semiprime will be called a SOSP.

 
The set of all  SOSPs will be denoted by  Z.
Let's start with taking a finite subset  A  of  Z  such that the sum  t(A)  of all reciprocals of  A is greater than  1.
Then take a subset  B  of  A  and  Let  t(B)  be the sum of the reciprocals of the members of  B.
If  t(B) > 1, select from  B  an element  b  such that  t(B) - 1/b  has a denominator as small as possible.
Replace  B  by  B \ {b}.  We repeat that process till the sum of the reciprocals is no longer bigger than  1.  If  t(B) = 1  you are lucky !
If  t(B) < 1  we select from  A \ B  an element  a  that is not in  B  such that  t(B) + 1/a   has a denominator as small as possible.
Replace  B  by  joining  a  to  B  We repeat this until the sum of reciprocals is no longer smaller than 1.   If  t(B) = 1  you are lucky !
But in most cases this process enters into an infinite loop.  This is the case when  a = b.
Therefore, the success will depend on the choice of a good  A  and  B.
After a lot of attempts I took for  A  the set of SOSPs with greatest prime divisor  p(80) = 409  and for  B  I took the set of  SOSPs with greatest prime divisor  p(79) = 401.

So, to find a solution that way, you need more luck than intelligence ... !

Also, minimality is not ensured.
When I apply this to find Egyptian fractions with semiprime denominator that sum to  1  I find a set of  111  members.
In this site solutions of  47  members are presented by Watanabe.
