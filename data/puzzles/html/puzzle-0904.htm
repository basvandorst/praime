Puzzle 904. Follow-up to Puzzles 902 & 903

Emmanuel Vantieghem sent the following follow-up to Puzzles 902 & 903:

Given P&Q such that Q=P

could these be combined by the sumproduct(P, Q) operation giving a power b^n, n>1 of b>1?

Examples by Emmanuel:

P = (41, 47, ... , 173)  (28 primes) : P x P = 586^2 
P = (157, 163, ... , 1097)  (148 primes) : P x P = 8174^2
P = (547, 557, ... , 971)  (64 primes) : P x P = 6088^2
P =(50891, 50893, ... , 51199} (28 primes) : P x P = 270106^2

 Emmanuel wrote "I could not find third or higher powers".

By my way I [CR] found one cubic solution:

P = (477973,...478417) (35 primes) : PxP=20003^3

Q. Find more vectors P such that sumproduct(P, P)=b^n for some b>2 & n>2.

Contribution came from Dmitry Kamenetsky

***

Dmitry wrote (Dec. 18, 2107):

For n>2 I couldn't find any more solutions for b^n<9*10^18. For n=2, there is a sequence in the OEIS: oeis.org/A270424
