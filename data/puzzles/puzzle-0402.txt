Puzzle 402. 1263560563

Perhaps you already know the following curio by Blanchette:

58^n + 38^n + 05^n + 23^n + 33^n is prime for n = 0 to 10. Note that 5838052333 is a 10-digit prime. [Blanchette]

Unexpectedly, I found two more but smaller examples of this type: 1263560563 & 5605126363 (which is a recombination of the first one), both primes for n=0 to 10.

Extending the idea, the smallest example that I have found of primes abcd... such that a^i+b^i+... is prime for n=0 to 9 is 1113997.

Questions.

Q1. Find an example of the Blanchette type prime for n=0 to 11 or more.

Q2. Find an example of my type prime for n=0 to 10 or more.

Contributions came from Adam Stinchcombe, Giovanni Resta, Luke Pebody, Farideh Firoozbakht & Fred Schneider.

***

All of them noticed that prime repunits are infinite trivial solutions to Q2.

Fred wrote:

Q1) 1033307589047 is the minimal Blanchette prime for n=0 to 11 (in

fact, it holds for n=12 as well)

Q2) Note that no digit can be zero because 0^0 is undefined.

With these points in mind, I found the following minimal non-trivial solutions:

n minimal prime such that the n is the highest power

10 11111111225256599 (17 digits)

11 12223333333334558899889 (23 digits)

12 1111122233444555999 (19 digits)

13 11333334555577777777777777777777777888799 (41 digits)

14 1133333344444444455555555666977777779 (31 digits)

There's no solutions s for n=15 such that s < 10^42

Note: That MANY other solutions for a given n can be found just by

randomly permutating the different digits for a given solution above.

In fact, my approach was find all the orderings of digits (or blocks

of digits) such that they were non-decreasing. Storing each block in

an array eliminated the need to do any division. Only adding was

necessary (once the prime powers were pre-computed).

Once a solution was found I could zero in on the minimum prime

solution usually by just permutating the last few digits of the

non-decreasing ordering. Inspecting the solutions should clarify what

I mean.

Bonus (for blocks of 3 digits):

When considering blocks of 3 digits (rather than blocks of 2 and 1

respectively in Q1 and Q2), I found the minimal number such that the

relation holds for n=0 through 11:

1,031,588,010,209
