Puzzle 839. Follow up to Puzzle 835.

Emmanuel Vantieghem sent the following nice puzzle:

Do there exist NxN squares of positive integers such that all 2N(N-1) sums of adjacent elements give the set of the consecutive primes from 3 till the 1+2N(N-1)-th prime?

The first I could find was N=3:

1 2 3

6 11 8

17 20 21

which gives the first twelve odd primes:

3 = 1 + 2 ; 5 = 2+3

17 = 6 +11 ; 19 = 11+8

37 = 17+20; 41 = 20+21

and

7 = 1 + 6 ; 23 = 6+17

13 =2 +11 ;31 = 11+20

11 =3 + 8 ; 29 = 8 +21
This is my biggest example. N=7:

1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 6, 11

18, 29, 38, 33, 46, 37, 90

5, 68, 21, 116, 15, 136, 91

24, 89, 218, 23, 176, 57, 106

79, 192, 119, 228, 173, 50, 267

198, 161, 248, 191, 38, 129, 164

185, 236, 21, 242, 71, 260, 119

Q. Find the smallest solution for N=3 to 10. "Smallest" means using the smallest larger integer (in bold&red in the Emmanuel's solutions)
