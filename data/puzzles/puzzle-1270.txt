Puzzle 1270 100057

Still tied to the recently celebrated pi-day, C. Rivera came up with another pi-puzzle.

In the always interesting Prime Curios! pages of my friend G. L. Honaker, Jr., we may read the following one:

100057 is "The first missing prime in the first million digits of π. Found in January 2001. [Blanchette]"

Q. Can you find the first missing prime for the first 10^n digits of pi, for n>6, up to the largest n you can evaluate?

From Jun 5-11, 2026 contributions came from Rahid Zaman, Gennady Gusev, J. M. Rebert, Paul Cleary

***

N.B. by CR: 1002887 is first "missing prime" in the first one hundred million digits of π. [De Geest]", on March 2002. .

***

Rahid wrote:

Using a streaming digit scan, I verified the known result for the first 10^6 digits of pi:

n = 6: 100057

I then found the following results for larger powers:

n = 7: 211889

n = 8: 1002887

n = 9: 10036087

Here I counted digits after the decimal point. For n = 9, I verified the result with two independent digit sources: streamed PILookup chunks and MIT’s one-billion-digit pi file.

The method was to scan the digit stream once, mark every observed k-digit block in compact bitsets, then iterate primes with primesieve and return the first prime whose decimal representation was not observed.

A note on credit: this was primarily AI-assisted computational work. I gave the puzzle to OpenAI Codex, used GPT-5.5 Pro to help research and optimize the approach, and then used Codex to implement and run the verification. My role was mainly prompting, supervising, relaying the optimization plan, and checking that the result was verified against independent digit sources.

If useful, I’m happy to send the source files and run notes. Please feel free to list the result as AI-assisted, or however you think is appropriate
