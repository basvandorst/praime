Puzzle 166 Solution
Eric Brier
No Institute Given
1 Equivalent condition
The problem is for any even integer n, to ﬁnd a prime q(n) and an integer f (n)
such that:
∀p prime, p > q (n), pn = 1 mod f (n)
Of course, the number f (n) must be as large as possible. One can replace the
condition by the equivalent one:
∀x ∈ N, gcd(x, n) = 1 , xn = 1 mod f (n)
If this condition is satisﬁed, the initial condition is obviously satisﬁed. Suppose
now that there exist x with xn ⁄= 1 mod f (n) and gcd( x, f(n)) = 1. By Dirich-
let’s Theorem on primes in arithmetic progression, there exist inﬁnitely many
primes p = x mod f (n). Any of these primes verify:
pn = xn ⁄= 1 mod f (n)
2 How to compute the values ?
To compute f (n), one must ﬁrst identify the odd primes that can divide f (n).
If q divides f (n), then one must have
∀x ∈ N, gcd(x, q) = 1 , xn = 1 mod q
Since x can take any value except zero modulo q, the integer n must be a multiple
of q −1. In other words, q is one plus a divisor of n. Then, to compute the highest
power of q that can divide f (n), one has to check that:
∀x ∈ N, gcd(x, q) = 1 , xn = 1 mod qα
In other terms, the integer n must be a multiple of the order of the group Z/qαZ.
This order is ( q − 1)qα−1. Concerning the power of two that divides f (n), one
can check that it is the power of two that divides n plus two. At the end, f (n)
is deﬁned as the product of these prime powers.
